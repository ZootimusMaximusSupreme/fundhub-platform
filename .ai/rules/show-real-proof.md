# Show real proof: the picture and the copy prove each other

**Owner law (2026-10-31):** Chris, on the backend selling slideshows: "We're telling people through copy and then we're telling people through images. Why not show them through images? And then the copy supports the image and vice versa, or the image supports the copy. So now the copy is proof. They see that as truth instead of us just saying it." And: "Diagrams are great, but if we can show something real, we always sway to that, or we merge them together, a little bit of diagram, a little bit of real."

This covers every slideshow, page, ad picture, email picture and sales picture.

## Always

- Show the real thing whenever we have it. That means the real credit report and the bureau's own reason lines, the real agreement clause, real product screens (the portal, the Blueprint dashboard, the lender list, the dispute letters), and real approval screenshots.
- Merge them when a point needs explaining. Put the real thing on the slide, then add a little diagram on top of it: an arrow, a circle, a label or a line that points at what matters. An example is the real report with the bank's reason circled and an arrow that says "the bank reads this line".
- Use a diagram on its own only when nothing real shows the point, such as the order of steps or how a process flows. Diagrams are good. Real wins when we have it.
- Make the picture and the words say the same thing. The words point at what the picture shows, and the picture shows what the words claim. Then the words read as proof.
- Fill a real screen or a real report from a sample file when no client said yes. The sample is one file, and the picture says Sample (law: sample-clients-consistent).
- Draw every diagram and every mark in Fundhub's own style (`public/app/fundhub-brand.css`): white sheets, thin hairline panels, the pastel status colors, mono labels and ink for the one thing that matters. The layouts can be simple sketches, but the look is always ours. Pick the picture with Visual Thinking (law: copy-skills-foundation).

## Never

- Draw something we could show for real.
- Let the picture and the words say different things, or let the words claim something the picture does not show.
- Put a real client's name, file or numbers on a page without that client's written OK, and never without blurring what identifies them (law: proof-cards-from-source).
- Invent proof: an amount, a reason line, a bank, a quote or a face (law: proof-cards-from-source).
- A hand-drawn or off-brand look, stick figures, or a grid of tiles.

## Example

```text
Slide: "When a bank denies good credit, the reason was on the credit report first."

❌ A drawn report with a made-up line, "Card balances too high".
❌ A diagram on every slide, whether or not it helps.
✅ The real "Why this score" block of the sample report, with the bureau's own words
   ("RATIO OF BALANCE TO LIMIT ON BANK REVOLVING OR OTHER REV ACCTS TOO HIGH"), labeled Sample,
   with an arrow on top that says "the bank reads this line", and copy that says what the reader sees.
```
