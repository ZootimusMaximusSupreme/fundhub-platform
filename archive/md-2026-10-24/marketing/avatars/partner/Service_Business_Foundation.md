## PART 1 — CORE SERVICE OFFERING

### 1. Primary Service (one sentence)

FundHub lets brokers, closers and operators who already generate funding deals run a funding company under their own brand — FundHub does 100% of the fulfillment behind them, and the partner keeps 50% of funding and credit-repair revenue, front end and back end, including half the 10% success fee, for a one-time $10,000 entry fee with no monthly base fee.

### 2. Service Category

**White-label business-in-a-box for the business-funding vertical.** More precisely: a branded front-end license on top of a done-for-you fulfillment operation (underwriting, lender matrix, credit repair, document collection, client communication, compliance). It is sold as a partnership with a revenue split, not as software, not as a course, and not as a franchise. The offer page states it directly: *"You are not buying a course, a lead list, or a licence to resell something. You're being plugged into a working operation as the front of it."*

### 3. The Core Problem

**People who can generate funding deals cannot get paid like owners, because fulfillment is a second company they can't build.**

The offer page names the three versions of this problem (section "The Pain"):

- **The broker:** sends the deal elsewhere, gets a slice, and "rents the relationship" — the client, the renewal, and the back end all belong to someone else.
- **The would-be operator:** tried to build fulfillment and stalled on "lender relationships nobody hands you, underwriting you have to actually learn, compliance in a regulated business, and staff to run files. Most people quit somewhere in month four."
- **The affiliate:** it pays, "it's just not a business — you can't put your name on it, you can't sell it, and you don't own the client."

The page's own diagnosis, which is the strategic core of the whole offer: **"None of those are a lack of hustle. They're a lack of infrastructure. Deal flow is one job. Fulfillment is a completely different company."**

## PART 2 — CLIENT PROFILE & TRANSFORMATION

### 4. Ideal Client (specific)

Desire-first, per Schwartz: the demographic is secondary; the qualifying trait is a **pre-existing belief plus a circumstance**. Per `docs/ads/ascension-ads.md` §2, these people are in-market — they *already believe funding works as a business*. They need "which and who, not whether."

Four documented segments (from the ads doc, in priority order — start with 1 and 3):

| # | Segment | Already believes | Circumstance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brokers sending deals to a funder** (biggest) | Funding makes money — they watch it happen | They found the client and rent the relationship |
| 3 | **Operators who tried to build it** (highest-intent, smallest) | The model works — they attempted it | They stalled on lenders, underwriting or staff |
| 2 | Affiliates on a referral cut | The offer converts — they've been paid on it | It pays, but it isn't a business they own |
| 4 | Closers with no book of their own | They can sell this — they do it daily | They're selling somebody else's company |

Hard qualification lines (offer page, "Qualification" section):
- Can already generate deals, or knows exactly how they will
- Can hold **ten funding clients a month** (the production floor — below it, the partnership ends)
- Willing to connect their ad account (free, required — "It is how attribution actually works")
- Wants to own a business, not collect a referral fee
- Does **not** need to be fundable themselves — their own credit file "is never a requirement and never a gate," and financing to a 405 FICO exists as a payment method only

Explicitly disqualified: passive-income seekers, anyone expecting the full internal CRM or the lender list, anyone who can't hold the floor, anyone expecting marketing bundled in.

### 5. The "Before" State

Documented on the offer page and in the ad copy:

- **Working, but not owning.** "You send the deal, they keep the client. The renewal is theirs. The back end is theirs." (ads doc, Ad set B hook 2)
- **A folder of dead deals and no answers.** From the $27 autopsy page: declined files come back with "a category, not a cause — 'credit profile', 'does not meet criteria', or nothing at all... you keep making the same submission mistake, because nobody ever told you which one it was."
- **A stalled build.** Attempted the funding company themselves; hit the lender/underwriting/compliance/staff wall around month four.
- **Emotional state** (per Ad set B body): the frustration of knowing "deal flow is one job. Fulfillment is a different company. Stalling on the second isn't a hustle problem." They are competent at the front end and blocked by the back end — and they know it.

### 6. The "After" State

Per the offer page ("What You Get" and "The Close"):

- **Their name on the front of a working machine:** their brand, their domain, their phone numbers, their payment processor, a client portal with their name on it — "the funding company is yours as far as the market is concerned."
- **Owner economics:** 50% of funding revenue, 50% of repair revenue, front and back end, half the 10% success fee — "not an introductory rate and not tiered... It never moves."
- **The back office gone as a problem:** "Soft pulls, underwriting, letter generation, inquiry removal, document collection, lender submission, chasing money. You never build any of it."
- **Visibility on their own book:** a partner view showing their clients and where every file stands — honestly scoped: "it's your slice, not the whole internal system."
- **Live from day one:** "Brand set up, ad account connected, your book opens. Fulfillment is running behind you from day one."

The transformation in one line, from the close: **"What doesn't exist yet is your name on the front of it."** From renting the client they found to owning the relationship, the renewal, and the back end.

## PART 3 — SERVICE DELIVERY & PROCESS

### 7. Service Methodology

**Acquisition path — the Ascension ladder** (locked terms + `public/partner/menu/index.html`; no required order, nothing is a prerequisite):

1. **$27 Decline Autopsy** — the front door. Cold direct-response traffic buys the buyer at break-even (ads doc §1). Every buyer has watched FundHub read a file.
2. **$47/mo Winner's Board** — the funding vertical's ads, sorted by survival time.
3. **$297 Live Trial (optional, never required)** — seven days under the prospect's own brand with real calls, real files, and the actual partner view. "Not a sandbox, not a recorded walkthrough."
4. **$10,000 White-Label Partnership** — never sold to cold traffic (ads doc: "Do not run cold traffic at the $10,000 page").
5. **Affiliate downsell** (20% direct / 5% second-tier, no entry fee, no floor) for anyone who doesn't fit.

**The gate:** a short application, a human read of it, then **a review call where a person decides** — "never a lender, never a score." Financing (down to 405 FICO) is how an accepted partner pays, not how anyone qualifies.

**Delivery** (offer page steps + Drive addendum §3): partner onboarding runs as a pipeline — Application → Vetting → Agreement → Org Provisioned → Live. The partner gets their own org on the platform: brand, domain, pipelines, dashboards, message templates, AI agents seeded from FundHub's prompts then tuned to the partner's voice, their own registered phone numbers, their own processor, a branded client portal. FundHub runs fulfillment inside its own system; the partner works their book through the partner view.

**Ongoing standard:** ten funding clients per month, enforced. "If a partner stays below the floor, the partnership ends."

### 8. Key Deliverables

From the offer page's eight-card "What You Get" grid, verbatim in substance:

1. Your brand and your domain — clients see the partner the whole way through
2. Your own phone numbers (registered, ready to send) and your own payment processor
3. Pipelines, dashboards and message templates — first contact to funded
4. The AI agents, on your prompts — chase documents, answer clients, keep files moving
5. A client portal with your name on it
6. The whole back office, run for you — soft pulls, underwriting, letter generation, inquiry removal, document collection, lender submission, chasing money
7. Your book, in a partner view (your slice, not the whole internal system)
8. Your ad account connected — free, and required, for attribution

Explicitly **not** deliverables (stated on the page, not buried): the lender list (never shared with anyone), the full internal CRM, marketing (a separate monthly, stackable, optional add-on priced on the review call), and any share of courses/digital products (those stay 100% FundHub).

### 9. Pricing Model

| Item | Term |
|---|---|

## PART 4 — MARKET LANDSCAPE & DIFFERENTIATION

### 10. Main Competitors (2–3)

Named as categories in FundHub's own copy (no specific rival company is named anywhere in the repo or Drive — that absence is itself a finding):

1. **Broker/finder arrangements and funding-referral programs** (direct). The status quo the offer attacks: send the deal to a funder, take a slice, lose the client, the renewal, and the back end. The entire "Pain" section of the offer page is positioned against this.
2. **"Funding coach" and course/licensing programs at $3K–$5K** (direct-adjacent). Called out repeatedly in `FundHub-Copy-Source-of-Truth.md`: "Funding coaches charge three to five thousand dollars to hand you a PDF and wish you luck" — teach-you-to-do-it products with no fulfillment behind them.
3. **Build-it-yourself** (indirect, and the biggest). The prospect's own attempt: lender relationships, underwriting, compliance, staff. "Most people quit somewhere in month four." Segment 3 (operators who tried) is the highest-intent audience precisely because they lost to this competitor already.

### 11. Your Differentiator

**FundHub sells the one thing the alternatives don't have: a fulfillment operation that already runs, every day, for real clients — and gives the partner the front of it at a split that never moves.**

Supporting differentiators, all from the live copy:

- **Real operation, not a license to figure it out.** "Every item above is a piece of a company that already runs, every day, for real clients." The VSL positioning cuts at competitors directly: "Most funding brokers have never funded a single business... They outsource everything to third-party fulfillment companies." FundHub *is* the fulfillment company.
- **Radical candor as positioning.** The page discloses the unflattering terms up front: the ten-client floor, the partner-view-not-full-CRM limit, the never-shared lender list, marketing as a paid add-on, the trial not crediting toward the fee. "We'd rather you know that on this page than find out later."
- **A human gate, not a credit gate.** Acceptance decided by a person on a review call; financing to 405 FICO exists so the score is never the filter; partners are never required to get funded themselves.
- **Economics that don't decay.** One fee, no monthly base, and a 50% split that "does not shrink when you grow and does not get renegotiated."

### 12. Client Objections

Every one below is documented on the offer page FAQ, the menu page, or the ads doc §5 objection pillar — these are the objections FundHub itself has already committed answers to:

1. **"Do I have to get funded myself?"** — No; never a requirement, never a gate.
2. **"Which lenders do you use?"** — Never shared, with anyone, at any price. Protecting the lender relationships "is part of why the 50% is worth what it's worth."
3. **"Do I get the whole CRM?"** — No; a partner view of your own book, because FundHub runs fulfillment inside the full system.
4. **"Is marketing included?"** — No; separate paid add-on, monthly and stackable.
5. **"Ten a month sounds like a lot — what if I miss it?"** — It's a real floor and staying below it ends the partnership; stated on the page, not in the contract.
6. **"$10,000 is a lot / is there a monthly fee?"** — Once, financeable to 405 FICO, no monthly base fee ever.
7. **"Does the 50% ever change?"** — No, by volume, tenure, or renegotiation.
8. **"What's the ad-account connection for?"** — Attribution; free and required; "nobody runs blind."
9. **"What if I don't qualify?"** — The affiliate track (20%/5%), presented as a genuine alternative, not a consolation prize.
