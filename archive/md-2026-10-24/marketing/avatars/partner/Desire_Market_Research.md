# Desire_Market_Research.md
## FundHub Service Avatar Project — Stage: Desire-Based Market Research
**Prepared:** 2026-08-31 · **North Star (Core Problem):** *People who can generate funding deals cannot get paid like owners, because fulfillment is a second company they can't build.*

**Provenance note (read before trusting quotes):** Direct fetches of Reddit, DailyFunder, Trustpilot, BBB, YouTube, LinkedIn, Quora, and Facebook were blocked in the research environment. All quotes below were captured verbatim from search-index excerpts of those pages and attributed to their real URLs. Anything not word-for-word is labelled **[PARAPHRASE]**. Nothing is invented. First-person Reddit voice and YouTube comments were not capturable this round — that absence is a documented gap, not a filled one.

## 1. Wants & Needs Analysis

### Functional Wants (what they say they want, ranked by frequency across all source families)

1. **Own the renewal / the book.** The dominant theme on DailyFunder. The stated reason to go independent is building "a portfolio of renewal merchants" you can eventually eat off. Brokers at shops get 20–35% of renewals at best; many fear not being paid renewals at all when funders auto-renew files behind them.
2. **Go direct / be the brand, not the middleman.** A whole DailyFunder thread is titled "How do you become a 'Funder'? Step by Step Guide request." In adjacent verticals (hard-money real estate lending), an entire product category — Roc Capital, TVC, Unitas — already sells "operate under your brand, our capital/compliance/back office." The desire FundHub monetizes is proven and productized next door.
3. **Full economics, not a slice.** Quora question in the market's own words: "What is the process to open a Merchant Cash Advance company to attain full commissions?" — the stated motive for building a company is capturing the whole commission instead of a broker's cut. This is the Core Problem verbatim from the market.
4. **Lender access without a track record.** New ISOs post repeatedly asking "which lenders would pick me up?" The catch-22: funders want volume history; you can't build volume without funders. In reviews, the only thing a $25k course buyer valued was the lender list — lender access is perceived as the scarce asset.
5. **A first closed deal, fast.** Speed-to-first-check is how this market decides whether it was scammed. Every negative review is, at bottom, "I paid and no deal ever closed."
6. **Getting paid what was promised.** Payout reliability is a functional want in itself — brokers complain about commissions unpaid, clawed back, or skimmed. The split matters less than whether the check arrives.
7. **A person who answers.** The highest-rated program in the category (BLB, 4.9 Trustpilot) earns its stars for named support humans, not income. What this market rewards is never being left alone with the file.
8. **Leads they don't have to buy blind + a full product menu.** Lead sellers resell the same lead to multiple brokers; one-product shops lose the client to whoever has the second product. Working brokers' #1 ask in groups is inbound lead flow.
9. **No-capital, no-experience, no-license entry.** "How do I start a loan business without money?" recurs on Quora; "no experience needed" dominates YouTube titles aimed at this market.

### Emotional Needs (what's underneath)

1. **To stop being the naive one.** The rawest thread found: "New Broker/ISO looking for an honest chat. What have I gotten myself into?" The fear of being the mark — bait-and-switched by shops, backdoored by lenders, resold by lead vendors — saturates every source family. The avatar has usually already been burned once.
2. **Ownership as identity.** "Be your own broker," "go on your own," "your own finance company." Employment at a shop is framed as a temporary apprenticeship; ownership is the destination. Free agent programs still attack ownership-priced competitors because the identity upgrade is what people are actually shopping for.
3. **Fear of the vanish-after-the-wire.** The moment of maximum emotional exposure is the moment after payment clears. Reviews obsess over what happened *after* the money moved — tone changes, unanswered emails, disconnected phones.
4. **Legitimacy / not being the sleazy guy.** The industry's own members call bad brokers "sleazy" and compare the trade to used-car sales. A real company with a real back end is the emotional antidote.
5. **Fear of the grind with no asset.** The revolving-door image: shops full of reps "sold a dream" who churn out with nothing. The need is for effort to compound into something sellable.
6. **Owner economics without balance-sheet risk.** On going direct-funder: you assume all the risk — "the money is made on collecting, not on lending" [PARAPHRASE, Quora]. They want to be the owner without the collections desk.
7. **Not being alone in it.** Commercial loan brokering described as "lonely at times"; the positive pole is being "blessed to be a part of the DAC family." Independent agents crave being inside an operation, not outside it.
8. **Income escape velocity vs. a job.** The comparison object in their head is their salary, not an empire: even one deal a month beats a 40–50-hour week job [PARAPHRASE, Quora]. "One deal replaces a paycheck" is the pitch that lands.

## 2. Pains & Frustrations (the big recurring ones, ranked by heat)

1. **Backdooring — deals and clients stolen by the lender.** The market's own swear-word for "renting the relationship." Multiple dedicated threads ("Keep getting back doored please help!!!!"). One report: a lender took a file to final stage, went silent 15 days, then funded the merchant directly. This is the #1 unspoken objection FundHub will face: *the deepest scar in this market is trusting the party who holds the back end.*
2. **Paid for a business, got a course.** The gap between "own a funding company" marketing and "here are some videos and a weekly call" delivery is the #1 complaint across review sites — compounded by no-refund terms, refund windows that expire before you can test anything, and upsell ladders pitched exactly when the base course isn't producing.
3. **Commission splits that insult — and checks that never come.** New reps get 15–20% of the ISO's commission; forum verdict: "you are getting RIPPED OFF." Plus BBB complaints of commissions promised and never paid, and clawbacks.
4. **The solo-operator stall.** Expect 1–2 years to profitability if you are simultaneously the cold caller, ISO-agreement negotiator, processor, accountant, closer, and tech support. The admin burden is "very underestimated." Empirical confirmation of "most people quit somewhere in month four."
5. **Deal flow without conversion = zero.** One agent contacted 150+ businesses in 6 months, not one application [PARAPHRASE]. Another sent 100+ clients, zero approvals [PARAPHRASE]. Fulfillment and conversion are a second job the front-end person cannot do alone.
6. **The back end they don't control ghosts them.** Renewal desks not answering emails; a white-label software user's branded site down 30+ days with robotic support. Your brand, their downtime.
7. **The category's trust is scorched earth.** The biggest name in "start your own commercial finance company" training (CCTG's owner) implicated in federal wire fraud; the FTC sued Seek Capital as a "bogus business finance scheme"; a white-label program (Novae) sanctioned for earnings claims. Scam-vigilance content ("STOP Buying Blueprints!") now outranks the offers themselves.
8. **Lead starvation and the cold-call grind.** Leads are "the hardest part," good sources are never disclosed, bought leads are resold to multiple brokers, and they hate outbound.

## 5. New Desire Opportunities (untapped or underserved — the gaps)

1. **"Renewals" as the headline desire word.** The single most emotionally loaded term found. "Are you in it for the renewals?" functions as a status question inside the trade. No existing solution leads with back-end ownership; every one of them either withholds it (shops, agent programs) or leaves you to build it alone (courses, software). FundHub's 50% *on the back end* speaks directly to the market's most-wanted, least-delivered asset.

2. **The structural anti-backdoor guarantee.** The market's deepest scar is trusting whoever holds the back end — and FundHub is asking for exactly that trust. Nobody in the category answers "how do I know YOU won't steal my clients" structurally. The 50/50 split on front AND back is the answer: FundHub only eats when the partner eats. This must be framed as the mechanism that makes backdooring irrational, not as a promise.

3. **Infrastructure, not information.** The market has fully priced in that courses are "glorified affiliate programs." What no one sells at this price point is the *operating fulfillment company* — processors, lender relationships, renewals desk, compliance — plugged in on day one. The $10k fee sits under the $25k seminar anchor while delivering the one thing the $25k buyers said was missing. "You paid to learn; you needed an operation" is the sophistication-stage-appropriate attack.

4. **Owner economics without balance-sheet risk.** The Quora record shows people want full commissions but recoil from funding capital, underwriting risk, and collections. The white-label middle path — owner brand and owner pay, someone else's balance sheet and back office — is proven in real estate lending (Roc/TVC/Unitas) and **has no established occupant in business funding**. No complaint corpus for a direct FundHub-shaped competitor was found anywhere. That absence is the white space — and a round-2 verification target, not a settled fact.

5. **The first 30 days after the wire.** The dominant category trauma is the post-payment vanish. Whatever FundHub does immediately after $10k clears — a named human, the first file visibly moving, dashboard access to front- and back-end economics — is the underserved desire the whole review record is screaming for. This market writes its reviews about the moment after the money moved.

6. **Legitimacy as a product feature.** They want to sell money without feeling like a predator. A real branded company with real fulfillment behind it is the antidote to the "sleazy broker" identity — an emotional deliverable no course or agent link provides.

7. **Belonging without employment.** "Lonely at times" on one side, "blessed to be part of the family" on the other. An owner who is *inside an operation* — peers, a desk that answers, a team behind the brand — is a desire the solo-ISO path and the course path both leave empty.

8. **Proof over claims — the desire ladder in order.** Positive reviews reveal the sequence the market itself uses: lenders that close → first check fast → a person who answers → residual checks → belonging. Downstream copy should promise in exactly that order, demonstrated (live files, named humans, first-deal speed), never merely asserted. Claims are dead in this category; mechanism and proof are the only currency.
