## Avatar Name: **"Backdoored Brandon"**

*(Distinct from "Growth-Stalled Gary," the existing CLIENT avatar. Gary needs funding. Brandon SELLS funding — and keeps getting robbed of what he builds.)*

### Profile Summary

Brandon can find a business that needs money and get them to yes. That is not his problem and it never was. His problem is what happens after yes. He runs a one-man broker shop: he sources his own deals through outbound and referrals and sends the files to a funder for a 15–20% slice. From the second he submits, the client, the renewal, and the back end belong to someone else. He has been backdoored once — a lender went quiet on his file for two weeks and then funded his merchant directly — and it changed how he reads every offer in this industry. He has paid for one "become a funding broker" program already, and what arrived was videos and a weekly call, not a business. He priced out building fulfillment himself and hit the wall on paper: lenders who won't sign an ISO with no track record, underwriting he'd have to apprentice into, compliance in a regulated vertical, and six jobs at once — so he ran the math and never started. He is now past *whether* funding works — he watches it work every week, on deals he found. He is stuck on *which* vehicle and *who* he can trust with the back end. He is in-market, scarred, fluent in the trade's vocabulary ("files," "renewals," "backdoored," "my book"), and shopping with one hand over his wallet.

## Marketing & Messaging Blueprint

*Frame note: the "you found the client, you should keep the client" frame is [ASSUMED framing] on documented pain — anchored to the sourced backdooring quote and the renewals thread title, and pending validation against real recordings (Open Items #1, #3).*

### Core Message to Resonate

> **"You found the client. You should keep the client. You've never lacked hustle — you've lacked infrastructure. Deal flow is one job; fulfillment is a completely different company. We're not going to teach you to build that company, and we're not going to make you live without it. We run it every single day, for real clients — and we'll put your name on the front of it. Your brand. Your book. Your renewals. 50% of funding and 50% of repair, front end and back end, at a split that isn't introductory and isn't tiered. And because we keep half of the funding and repair revenue on every client you bring, backdooring you would cost us half of every future dollar on that client."**

Register notes: speak to *which and who*, never *whether* — he's past whether. Lead with mechanism and printed limits, never claims. Name the scar (backdooring, course-that-wasn't-a-business) before he raises it.

**Alignment guardrail (hard rule):** never write "we only get paid when you do" or any equivalent. FundHub collects the $10,000 fee, the $297 trial, the $27 autopsy, and paid marketing add-ons regardless of partner results. The only true alignment claim is the narrow one above: the *ongoing* funding and repair revenue is shared 50/50, so backdooring is bad math on that revenue.

**Split guardrail (hard rule):** the split is 50% of funding and 50% of repair. Never "50% of everything" or "every dollar" — the $10,000 fee, trial revenue, and marketing add-ons are not split.

**Perpetuity guardrail (hard rule):** no written term backs "never renegotiated," "never moves," or "forever," and the ten-client floor means the partnership can end. Until a contract term exists in writing for post-termination book/renewal ownership, copy may say the split "isn't introductory and isn't tiered" and no more.

### Key Belief to Shift

**FROM:** *"To own a funding company I'd have to build the fulfillment company myself — lenders, underwriting, compliance, staff — and everyone offering a shortcut is selling a course or waiting to steal my clients. Whoever holds the back end wins, and it's never me."*

**TO:** *"Fulfillment is a second company — and it doesn't have to be built or rented. It can be transplanted, already running, behind my brand. The one operation that actually runs it daily will put my name on the front, and the ongoing deal is 50/50 on funding and 50/50 on repair — front end and back end — which makes stealing my clients bad math instead of a broken promise. Ownership was never a building problem. It was an infrastructure problem, and the infrastructure already exists."*
