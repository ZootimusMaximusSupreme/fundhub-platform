# VSL Stacking: examples and evidence

## Which marketers or companies show the method publicly?

### Takeaway

The exact public label “VSL stacking” is currently tied most clearly to Josh Hamilton. His public post describes a stack of videos placed at separate friction points in a sales process. Other public sources show adjacent practices—control VSLs, segment cuts, retargeting companions, objection videos, and video A/B tests—but I did not find an independently verified case study that tests the full “stack” as a single causal intervention.

### Cited Findings

- **Josh Hamilton — exact named method, public claim.** Hamilton defines VSL stacking as putting a video at each point where a human would normally answer a question or handle an objection. His example is a home-services business: (1) website video selling a free report and personalized 3D home model, targeting 10–20% more bookings; (2) a video for leads who do not answer the phone, targeting 5–10% more bookings; (3) a pre-appointment video explaining the visit, targeting 20% more appointments that sit; and (4) an optional iPad video during the home visit, targeting 5–10% more closes. He says the combined target would move a business from roughly $6.5M/month to $9.5M–$11.3M/month on the same leads and ad spend. These are **claims in a social post**, not reported experiment results; no sample, control, dates, observed outcomes, or client confirmation are provided. [Josh Hamilton on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)

- **Josh Hamilton — useful stage map and titles.** The post gives practical names for the video jobs: “website” / free-report VSL, “no pickup” objection video, “appointment show-up” video, and “on-site iPad” close-support video. It also mentions a 2–5 second objection clip, a 2–8 minute proof/nurture video before the appointment, and walkthrough videos after a discovery call or site visit. The examples are useful as script briefs, but not proof that each asset caused the stated lift. [Josh Hamilton on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)

- **Talking Heads — adjacent multi-video system.** The company recommends one control VSL plus hook/opening variants, segment cuts, proof refreshes, retargeting or mid-funnel companions, and short reinforcement clips for checkout or application pages. Its placement map covers cold-traffic landing pages, warm email/retargeting, webinar replay or post-demo, checkout/application, and partner pages. This is a **vendor strategy article**, not a disclosed client experiment; it reports no causal lift for the system. [Talking Heads, “VSL Videos That Keep Converting”](https://talkingheads.com/vsl-videos-multiple-over-time/)

- **ElasticFunnels / TrackPlay — adjacent testing infrastructure.** ElasticFunnels publicly describes testing two videos, a VSL versus a text sales letter, or two checkouts behind the same video. It recommends choosing winners by sales, revenue/EPC/profit, upsell take rate, and revenue by campaign/ad—not clicks alone. This establishes that multi-video and video-versus-text testing is operationally supported, but the page does not provide a disclosed customer experiment or result. [ElasticFunnels VSL funnel builder](https://elasticfunnels.io/solutions/vsl-funnel)

- **Mark Pescetti — short VSL variants.** A public YouTube video is titled “2 Minute VSLs | How To Write A Two Minute Video Sales Letter (VSL) for multiple parts of your funnel.” The description says 60-second to 2-minute VSLs use the same strategy and elements as a long-form presentation and can be used to test and optimize funnels faster. It is an educational example, not a documented performance case study. [Mark Pescetti, YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x4ZPBqU2y6c)

- **Ash Davis — standard VSL funnel walkthrough.** Davis’s public walkthrough shows an opt-in page, VSL page, application page, scheduling page, and call-confirmation page for an appointment funnel. It is useful for identifying a conventional single-core-VSL path and the likely places where targeted follow-up videos could be added. It does not document a stacked-VSL experiment or verified result. [Ash Davis, “How To Create a Video Sales Letter Funnel”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vUFC2V4YDy0)

- **Instagram examples supplied by the project.** The supplied Instagram URLs are relevant leads, but the web fetch was throttled, so I could not independently inspect their content or verify the claims: [DadGnlYt9tt](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DadGnlYt9tt/), [DeHX8vivm3A](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeHX8vivm3A/), and [Dd4CFzZPnFt](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dd4CFzZPnFt/). Treat them as unverified source leads until their captions/transcripts or original videos are available.

### Inferences

- Public material supports a working definition: **one video per next-step friction point**, with the video’s job tied to a specific downstream event. It does not yet support the stronger claim that adding more videos automatically compounds revenue.

- The most reusable public script briefs are stage-specific: “why give me your details,” “why answer/book now,” “what will happen at the appointment,” “why trust the recommendation,” “why buy after the proposal,” and “what happens next after purchase.”

### Gaps

- I found no public, independently audited or experimentally documented full-stack VSL case with exposure counts, randomization, stage-level control groups, and downstream revenue.
- I found no verified evidence that the exact Hamilton home-services example reached the projected $9.5M–$11.3M/month range.

## Are there actual case studies, experiments, and reported metrics?

### Takeaway

There are real experiments on video in landing pages and real analytics guidance for measuring video-assisted conversions. They show strong context dependence. The experiments do not prove VSL stacking specifically, but they provide a sound measurement model and clear warnings against treating watch time or one-stage conversion as revenue proof.

### Cited Findings

- **The Social Man / VWO — controlled test of a video-oriented CTA.** Cold traffic from a dating website was sent to an email opt-in page. The team tested a button saying “Get Instant Access” against “Watch the Video.” The reported conversion rate rose from 11.9% to 15.3%, a 28% increase in sign-ups. The page’s stated goal was newsletter sign-up and getting visitors to see a video; the source does not disclose visitor counts, test duration, or a confidence interval. This is a real A/B-test case study, but it tests CTA framing and the next step—not a multi-VSL stack. [Wingify/VWO success story](https://wingify.com/success-stories/the-social-man/)

- **Vidyard / Unbounce — landing-page video test.** Vidyard reports testing a coming-soon page with no video, an embedded video, and a lightbox video. The reported averages were 6.5% without video, 11% with embedded video (69% lift), and 13% with a lightbox video (100% lift). The source identifies the page purpose and variants, but it does not show sample size, test duration, or statistical significance in the published post. Treat as a company case study with numbers, not independently verified evidence. [Unbounce case study](https://unbounce.com/conversion-rate-optimization/case-study-using-video-to-lift-landing-page-conversion-rate-by-100/)

- **Grow Your Own Groceries / Instapage — add-to-cart test.** Instapage’s A/B-testing guide reports that adding a video to a landing page increased clicks on the Add to Cart button by 12.62%. The guide does not provide the original experiment page, sample size, duration, or significance details. It is a secondary case-study summary and should not be used as proof of sales lift. [Instapage A/B-testing PDF](https://instapage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/what-is-ab-testing.pdf)

- **NextAfter donation experiment — video removal increased conversion.** NextAfter reports a randomized split test with 82,675 total visitors. The control had video on the email/landing page and converted at 0.08%; the treatment removed the video and used a more personal written story, converting at 0.24%, a 203% relative increase with 100% reported confidence. The source says the required sample size was 4,697 and that the result exceeded 95% confidence. This is strong evidence that video can add friction in a donation context; it is not a VSL-stack test and the treatment changed more than video alone. [NextAfter experiment](https://www.nextafter.com/experiments/how-video-donation-page-affects-donor-conversion/)

- **Unbounce aggregate analysis — video often neutral or negative.** Unbounce analyzed customer landing pages by video presence, conversion goal, industry, traffic source, and device. Its published conclusion is that video pages performed roughly as well or slightly worse than pages without video overall; distributions were lower for video pages across traffic channels, and video appeared negative on both mobile and desktop. This is observational, not randomized, so selection effects remain possible. [Unbounce analysis](https://unbounce.com/landing-pages/video-on-landing-pages-means-more-conversions-right-wrong-heres-why/)

- **Wistia — experimentation process and metrics.** Wistia says it ran more than 150 A/B tests over three years across pages, content, product, email, sales, and plans. Its public process emphasizes hypotheses, test documents, roadmaps, result logging, and learning from wins and losses. Wistia’s video A/B-testing guidance names unique views, play rate, engagement, actions, CTA conversion, and video length as measurable variables. These are useful measurement practices, not evidence that stacking itself works. [Wistia experimentation process](https://wistia.com/blog/approach-to-a-b-testing) and [Wistia video A/B testing](https://wistia.com/blog/split-testing-video)

- **Google Analytics / Ads — engaged-view measurement.** Google defines an engaged-view key event as a viewer watching at least 10 seconds of a YouTube video and then triggering a key event on a site or app within three days. Google also warns that reporting can differ by reporting identity. This supports measuring downstream conversions after video exposure, rather than using views alone. [Google Analytics engaged-view key events](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12846214?hl=en) and [Google Ads engaged-view conversions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10048752?hl=en)

- **Academic evidence on video framing and length.** A 2026 Food Policy study used a between-subjects online survey and discrete-choice experiment to compare live-action versus animation and short versus long informational videos. The abstract reports that live-action increased involvement and willingness to pay, while longer videos increased involvement and shorter videos were more effective for willingness to pay; it found no consistent video-length effect. This is independently published academic evidence about video persuasion, not direct evidence about VSL funnels. [Yang and Caputo, Food Policy](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfpoli/v140y2026ics0306919226000126.html)

- **Academic evidence on attention and cognitive load.** A 2023 Computers in Human Behavior experiment found that multitaskers paid less attention to ads and had similar cognitive load to single-medium users; lower attention reduced counterarguing and increased later brand attitude in that study. It does not show that more videos improve sales. It suggests that attention quality and context matter, and that a stack can create more exposure opportunities without guaranteeing deeper processing. [Chang and Thorson, Computers in Human Behavior](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563222003648)

### Inferences

- A credible VSL-stack test should randomize or otherwise isolate each stage’s video treatment and track the downstream event it is meant to move: booked call, show rate, qualified opportunity, close rate, refund rate, or revenue. “More watch time” is not enough.

- Hamilton’s compounding model is mathematically plausible only if the lifts are measured on independent stage populations and do not come from the same people or the same denominator. A post that multiplies target percentages without showing denominators is a forecast, not an observed result.

### Gaps

- I found no public full-funnel experiment that isolates four or more stage-specific VSLs and reports incremental revenue, CAC, show rate, close rate, and refunds together.
- Most public VSL case studies are vendor-published. Independent replication is rare.

## What scripts, titles, and anti-overuse rules are useful?

### Takeaway

The best public examples are modular and stage-specific. The evidence against overuse is practical and measurable: extra video can delay the CTA, add page weight, create friction, or distract from a simple offer. The safe operating rule is to earn each video with a named objection and a downstream metric, then test it against a no-video or text control.

### Cited Findings

- **Useful script/title patterns from public examples:** “Free report + personalized 3D home model” for the first booking step; “Why we are sending this instead of calling again” for no-answer leads; “What happens during your home visit” for show rate; “What to expect while we walk your property” for on-site close support; “Proof and why you booked” for pre-appointment nurture; and “What happens next” for post-signature reassurance. These are paraphrased from Hamilton’s stage descriptions, not claimed verbatim titles. [Josh Hamilton on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)

- **Modular production pattern.** Talking Heads recommends swappable blocks—intro, story, proof, offer, FAQ, CTA—so a team can refresh hooks, proof, or offer sections without reshooting the entire VSL. It recommends a control, hook variants, segment cuts, proof refreshes, and shorter mid-funnel companions. This is a useful production model, but its 30–60-day cadence is a vendor recommendation rather than a verified universal rule. [Talking Heads](https://talkingheads.com/vsl-videos-multiple-over-time/)

- **Overuse warning: remove friction before adding persuasion.** NextAfter’s large donation experiment found the video version converted at 0.08% while the more personal no-video treatment converted at 0.24%. The source explicitly says removing video reduced friction and strengthened the value proposition. This is a direct warning against placing another video where the buyer is ready to act. [NextAfter](https://www.nextafter.com/experiments/how-video-donation-page-affects-donor-conversion/)

- **Overuse warning: video can be neutral or negative in aggregate.** Unbounce’s customer-page analysis found video landing pages generally performed about the same or slightly worse than no-video pages, with negative patterns across industries, traffic sources, and devices. Because the analysis is observational, it is a warning signal rather than causal proof. [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-pages/video-on-landing-pages-means-more-conversions-right-wrong-heres-why/)

- **Overuse warning: page speed and CTA delay.** A 2026 CRO guide from Donnu A/B explains that adding video changes communication, load time, first-screen layout, and effort at once. Its worked example is explicitly labeled as a worked example, not a reported client case: static image 5.10%, muted autoplay 5.00%, and click-to-play 5.50%, with autoplay also showing slower LCP. Use this as a testing framework, not as independently verified performance evidence. [Donnu A/B](https://donnuab.com/blog/en/landing-page-video-ab-testing/)

- **Measurement rule.** Google supports linking video exposure to later key events, and ElasticFunnels recommends choosing winners by sales, revenue/EPC/profit, and upsell take rate rather than clicks. Together, these sources support a stage-specific scorecard: play rate and retention for diagnosis, but booked calls, show rate, close rate, revenue, refunds, and profit for decisions. [Google Analytics](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12846214?hl=en), [ElasticFunnels](https://elasticfunnels.io/solutions/vsl-funnel)

### Inferences

- A “stack” should be treated as a library of targeted sales interventions, not a requirement to put a video on every page. The video earns its place when a known question blocks the next step and the page has enough intent for the viewer to give the video time.

- The clearest anti-overuse guardrail is one video, one job, one metric, one control. If the next-step conversion does not improve without damaging downstream quality, remove or shorten the video.

### Gaps

- There is no public consensus threshold for how many VSLs are too many. The answer depends on offer complexity, stage intent, page speed, traffic quality, and whether each video replaces or adds to existing copy.
- The popular “video increases conversions by 80–86%” statistic appears widely in marketing articles, but the public primary study and methodology were not recoverable in the sources reviewed. Treat that figure as an unverified industry claim, not general evidence. [Donnu A/B discussion](https://donnuab.com/blog/en/landing-page-video-ab-testing/)
