# VSL Stacking: theory and origin

Research date: 2026-10-11. Scope: public information available by October 2026.

## Where does the phrase or method appear publicly?

### Takeaway

The clearest public definition found is Josh Hamilton’s October 2026 LinkedIn post and embedded transcript. He presents “VSL stacking” as his name for placing a separate video sales letter at each meaningful friction point in a sales process. I did not find reliable evidence that he was the first person ever to use the phrase, nor a book, peer-reviewed paper, or established industry standard that predates this public explanation.

### Cited Findings

- Josh Hamilton publicly says, “I call it VSL stacking,” and defines it as putting video “at every single point where a human would normally have to answer a question or handle an objection.” He says each video sells the next step rather than trying to close the whole journey at once. — [Josh Hamilton, “VSL Stacking Boosts Booking Rate and Sales,” LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- The same post gives a concrete four-video example for a home-services sales process: a website video, a no-contact follow-up video, a booked-appointment nurture video, and an optional in-home video. — [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn transcript](https://www.linkedin.com/videos/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- Public VSL guides usually describe a VSL as one linear video carrying a viewer through a persuasion sequence and one primary action. That is the baseline format that Hamilton says VSL stacking extends across more stages. — [Vidpros, “Video Sales Letter Funnel”](https://vidpros.com/video-sales-letter-funnel/); [Rob Palmer, “What Is a VSL?”](https://robpalmer.com/blog/what-is-a-vsl)
- The phrase “stacking” is also used in older and broader direct-response language for stacking proof, bonuses, or offer value inside one VSL. That is a different use from stacking multiple VSLs across a funnel. — [Rob Palmer, “What Is a VSL?”](https://robpalmer.com/blog/what-is-a-vsl); [Rob Palmer, “VSL Copywriting”](https://robpalmer.com/blog/vsl-copywriting)
- The supplied Instagram URLs were not readable through the research web tool, so I cannot confirm their dates, authors, or exact claims from those pages. — [Instagram reel 1](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DadGnlYt9tt/); [Instagram reel 2](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeHX8vivm3A/); [Instagram reel 3](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dd4CFzZPnFt/)

### Inferences

- Based on the searchable public record, “VSL stacking” appears to be a newly named practitioner framework rather than a settled academic or industry term. This is an inference from the lack of earlier reliable sources found, not proof that no earlier private or unindexed use exists.
- Hamilton appears to be the strongest publicly identifiable teacher of this exact phrase in the sources found. That does not prove sole authorship.

### Gaps

- No reliable source found establishes the first-ever use, original coiner, trademark, course launch, or private-client origin of the phrase.
- No independently archived version of the supplied Instagram reels was available through the research tool.
- No public book or peer-reviewed literature was found that treats “VSL stacking” as a named method.

## What does “stacking” mean in practice, and what does each micro-video sell?

### Takeaway

In the strongest direct description, stacking means matching a short or long video to the next decision in the buyer journey. Each video has one local job: remove the objection or uncertainty blocking the next step. The video does not necessarily sell the final product; it sells the next commitment, such as requesting information, booking, showing up, continuing to a proposal, accepting an offer, or starting onboarding.

### Cited Findings

- Hamilton’s stated rule is to put a VSL at each “friction point,” because prospects have different questions at different stages. He says the more stages a funnel has, the more opportunities there are to stack VSLs. — [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- Hamilton’s worked example maps the local sale for each video as follows: VSL 1 sells a free report and lead capture; VSL 2 addresses objections from leads who did not answer or book; VSL 3 explains the visit and builds show-up confidence; VSL 4 supports the rep’s close in the home. — [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn transcript](https://www.linkedin.com/videos/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- Hamilton’s public post also describes a post-signature video that reinforces the buyer’s decision and prepares onboarding. This is a post-purchase continuation of the same principle, though it is better described as onboarding or retention video than a classic sales letter. — [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- A conventional VSL funnel normally uses one page video, one desired action, and follow-up email or retargeting for people who do not act. — [Vidpros, “Video Sales Letter Funnel”](https://vidpros.com/video-sales-letter-funnel/)
- Wistia’s product documentation confirms that video CTAs can be placed at specific timestamps and can link viewers to the next page or action. This supports the mechanics of stage-specific video CTAs, but does not validate VSL stacking as a complete strategy. — [Wistia, “How to Add a Video CTA to Increase Conversions”](https://wistia.com/blog/using-video-ctas); [Wistia CTA documentation](https://support.wistia.com/en/articles/8283756-call-to-action)

### Practical stage map

| Stage | Likely micro-video job | What it sells | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website or landing page | Explain the problem, mechanism, proof, and immediate offer | Lead magnet, application, booking, or purchase | Directly described as the normal VSL role by VSL guides; Hamilton gives a free-report example. |
| Lead captured but no booking or no answer | Answer the two most common objections and reduce uncertainty | The booking or reply | Directly described by Hamilton; no independent test found. |
| Appointment booked | Show what will happen, who will attend, and why the meeting is worth keeping | Attendance / show-up | Directly described by Hamilton; no independent test found. |
| Between discovery and proposal | Re-state the offer, answer price questions, and pre-handle objections | The proposal review or close | Directly described by Hamilton; no independent test found. |
| In-person sales visit | Standardize part of the explanation while the rep focuses on the buyer | The purchase or signed agreement | Hamilton calls this optional and gives it as a field-sales example. |
| After signature | Reinforce the decision and explain onboarding | A smooth start, not a new sale | Directly described by Hamilton; this is an extension of the theory, not a standard VSL definition. |

### Inferences

- The core unit is not “one video per page.” It is “one video per unresolved decision.” A single page could contain more than one video, and a single video could be reused if the same objection recurs.
- The best operational design would begin with the funnel’s stage-specific drop-offs and objection logs, then assign one measurable next-step KPI to each video.
- “Micro-video” is a practical label, not a fixed technical length. Hamilton gives 2–5 seconds for an immediate objection clip and 2–8 minutes for a nurture video, but the public definition does not set a universal duration.

### Gaps

- No public source specifies a complete production template, naming system, analytics schema, or minimum/maximum number of videos.
- No source proves that every stage needs a VSL. Hamilton says the more stages there are, the more opportunities exist, but that is a strategic claim, not a controlled finding.

## What claims are supported by evidence, and how does the method differ from related formats?

### Takeaway

The evidence supports some underlying tactics: video can communicate product or message information, discoverability and placement matter, and measurable in-video CTAs can produce trackable actions. The evidence does not establish that a VSL stack outperforms one VSL, a webinar, or a human sales process. Hamilton’s percentage lifts and revenue projections are public targets or marketing claims, not independently verified experiments in the source.

### Cited Findings

- Hamilton gives target lifts of 10–20% more bookings for the website video, 5–10% more bookings for the no-contact follow-up, 20% better appointment sit rate, and 5–10% more closes for the optional in-home video. He then projects monthly revenue from $6.5 million to $9.5–$11.3 million. The post does not provide a test design, sample size, baseline, time window, control group, or results showing these lifts occurred. — [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn transcript](https://www.linkedin.com/videos/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- Hamilton repeats a “95% retention for video versus 10% for text” claim. The post gives no original study or method for that statistic, so it should be treated as an unsupported marketing claim in this context. — [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- A large preregistered research program comparing video and text in political messages found that video increased belief that an event occurred, but produced only a small advantage in persuasion and behavioral intentions. The context is political communication, not direct-response funnels, so it is not a direct test of VSL stacking; it does challenge any blanket claim that video is automatically much more persuasive than text. — [Tappin et al., PNAS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8617416/); [MIT News summary](https://news.mit.edu/2021/video-persuasion-politics-1116)
- Baymard’s large-scale product-page usability research found that 41% of tested users watched product videos, while 59% did not. It found that placement and discoverability changed whether users found and used videos. This supports careful placement and audience fit, not the claim that adding more videos always improves conversion. — [Baymard Institute, “UX Research on Product Page Videos”](https://baymard.com/research-articles/embedding-product-page-videos)
- Wistia reports an average video-CTA conversion rate of about 16% across its platform, based on its own analysis of 36,000 CTAs in videos hosted in 2024. It also reports that CTA performance varies by video type, length, and placement. This is a useful platform benchmark, but it is observational vendor data and is not a head-to-head test of VSL stacking. — [Wistia, “How to Add a Video CTA to Increase Conversions”](https://wistia.com/blog/using-video-ctas)
- A conventional one-long VSL is linear and usually asks for one main action after presenting the problem, mechanism, proof, offer, and close. — [Vidpros, “Video Sales Letter Funnel”](https://vidpros.com/video-sales-letter-funnel/); [Rob Palmer, “What Is a VSL?”](https://robpalmer.com/blog/what-is-a-vsl)
- A webinar is normally an event or replay built around teaching, presentation, and a larger sales moment. The VSL-stack idea is different because it distributes short, stage-specific sales arguments across the journey instead of making one event carry the whole persuasion load. The public sources found describe webinar recordings as a VSL-funnel variant, but do not provide a formal comparison study. — [Vidpros, “Video Sales Letter Funnel”](https://vidpros.com/video-sales-letter-funnel/); [Wistia CTA analysis](https://wistia.com/blog/using-video-ctas)
- A sales call is a live, interactive conversation where the representative can diagnose, answer, and adapt. VSL stacking is asynchronous and repeatable; its stated purpose is to move common explanation and objection handling earlier so the human call can focus on higher-value decisions. Hamilton describes videos as doing work a salesperson used to do, but does not show that they replace the judgment or trust-building of a live call. — [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn transcript](https://www.linkedin.com/videos/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)
- Ordinary funnel video is a broad category that may educate, demonstrate, build brand awareness, or retarget. A VSL has a direct-response job and a CTA. VSL stacking adds a sequencing rule: each video is tied to a specific stage, objection, and next action. — [Rob Palmer, “What Is a VSL?”](https://robpalmer.com/blog/what-is-a-vsl); [Josh Hamilton LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ)

### Comparison

| Format | Main unit | Typical job | Main decision-maker | What is distinctive about VSL stacking? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One long VSL | One linear video | Carry the viewer from problem to one CTA | The video controls sequence | Splits the persuasion journey into several stage-specific videos. |
| Webinar / replay | One event or event-like presentation | Teach, create a sales moment, answer broad objections | Presenter plus event format | Uses many smaller interventions instead of one event-sized presentation. |
| Sales call | Live conversation | Diagnose, answer, negotiate, close | Human rep and buyer together | Pre-handles repeatable questions before or between calls; does not prove that live selling is replaceable. |
| Ordinary funnel video | Any video asset | Education, demo, branding, proof, or traffic support | Depends on the asset | Requires every video to have a named next-step job and stage KPI. |

### Inferences

- The strongest defensible theory is operational rather than scientific: map each funnel transition, identify the friction at that transition, give the prospect a relevant video, and measure the next-step rate. This is a plausible conversion system, but it remains a practitioner hypothesis until tested in a controlled funnel.
- If the videos are simply added everywhere without stage-specific intent, the result is ordinary video proliferation, not VSL stacking.
- The most important test would compare the same funnel with and without stage-specific videos while holding traffic, offer, sales team, and follow-up constant. Public evidence for that exact test was not found.

### Gaps

- No public controlled experiment was found comparing VSL stacking against one long VSL.
- No public primary analytics showed the claimed booking, show, close, or revenue lifts.
- No independent source validates the projected compounding math from Hamilton’s example.
- No study found isolates the effect of “video at every friction point” from other changes such as better copy, faster follow-up, stronger proof, personalization, or sales-team changes.
