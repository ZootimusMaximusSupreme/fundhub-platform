## Testimonial interview SOP

Three Zoom interviews with the three customers on the funnel. Before, decision, during, after, close. Freeform delivery, structured questions.

### Before the call

- Pick the three most recent buyers with a real result. Text first: what's the number? No number in one sentence, swap them.
- Send three questions ahead, never the whole list.
- Release signed. Note any incentive given; it has to be disclosed.
- One rule at the top of the call: say the thing you're talking about in your answer. "The roadmap was the part that..." not "it was the part that...".

### Setup

- Zoom, record to cloud, gallery off, them full frame, window light on their face.
- Mute yourself while they answer. Wait five seconds after every answer.

### The run, 30 to 40 minutes

Warm up: name, the business, how long.

Before:
- Before you found this, where was your credit and funding situation, honestly?
- What had you already tried? What happened with it?
- What was that costing you?

The decision:
- What was the moment you went from thinking about it to buying it?
- What almost stopped you? What were you worried about with the $297?
- Had you been burned before? What made you think this was different?

During:
- Walk me through opening it. What did you look at first?
- What surprised you?
- What did you actually do that first week?
- When did you realize it was working?

After:
- Where was your file when you started and where is it now? Numbers.
- What did you qualify for before, and what did you qualify for after?
- What did the money go to?
- What's different about how you run the business now?

Close:
- If a friend was staring at the checkout page right now, what would you say to them?
- Who is this perfect for?

On every vague answer: give me a specific example of that. Or: walk me through the moment that happened. Never accept "it was great."

Last five minutes, repeat after me. Start the sentence, they finish it.
- Before this I was...
- The thing that actually changed was...
- If you're thinking about the $297...

### After

- Cut each to 45 to 90 seconds for the page, plus two or three clips at 10 to 30 seconds. Leave the pauses in.
- "Individual results vary" on screen under any number they say.
- One clip per person on a different objection: price, does it work, what's the result. Best one goes next to the checkout button.

## House rules

These were set while writing the seven above. New ads follow them.

- Second person, straight at the viewer. Never "most business owners have no idea", always "you have no idea".
- Strongest register is I will, you will. "I'll pull your credit. I'll show you where your score sits. I'll hand you the list."
- Say the thing. No "the full read", no "read line by line", no riddles like "which one you're sitting on".
- Never say "the number" or "your number". Spell it out: how much you qualify for right now, how much once your file is optimized.
- Score now and score after. Skip the three-bureau score mechanics and the middle score explanation.
- "Sometimes it's one item, sometimes it's twelve" is how variable file condition gets said.
- Never make the viewer feel stupid. Blame the industry, never the owner. Never say they "got closed", say the call turned into a pitch.
- Never disqualify the clean-credit owner. He is the best ICP because he wants maximum funding and most files need work whether he knows it or not.
- The product is credit card stacking. Never frame it as a bank offer they take or leave. The file either gets worked or it doesn't.
- Future pacing stays broad: starts your new business, funds the one you're already running, gets you ahead of the guys you're competing with. Never "second location".
- "10x your file", "maximum fundability", and "maximum amount of funding" are approved language.
- Always "potentially seven figures", never a flat seven figures. "On a lot of files" rather than "typically".
- Proof in full sentences: I've been doing this for ten years, I've seen hundreds of files, tens of thousands of data points across the funding ecosystem, reverse engineered. Never the clipped version.
- Never name EIN, DUNS, net-30 vendors or gas cards.
- Never write "no guarantees". It reads as what's most likely and what we're aiming at.
- Guarantee wording: if you're not happy with what you get, email us and we'll refund you. The 15 day window is in the soft pull authorization, never in an ad.
- Close is one plain sentence about the soft inquiry. Never stacked fragments.
- Industry slams stay about the industry as a whole, never a named competitor.
- Andromeda: each ad is one reason to buy with its own hook, body and CTA. Hook swaps on shared body copy read as one creative and stopped working in July 2025.
- Shoot style on everything: Chris seated, face to camera, in a location that looks expensive. Never selfie style, never phone-height handheld. Vary the location per ad, not the framing.

## Open items

- Refund window is 15 days, decided. It lives in the soft pull authorization so they agree to it at checkout. The ads keep the open wording, "if you're not happy with what you get, email us and we'll refund you," and never state the number of days.
- Andromeda volume: seven ads is under the floor of 15 to 20 unique creatives a week. Each new one needs its own reason to buy.
