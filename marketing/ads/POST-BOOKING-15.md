# The Post-Booking 30 — sample sheet

**COMPLIANCE REVIEW REQUIRED** — these are customer-facing and they touch credit repair, fee
timing, and credit-pull type. Nothing here claims a credit outcome. Keep it that way in the shoot.

These run as ads to people who **already booked a call**, in the 72 hours before it. One shared
set, all product paths. Not cold traffic. See `marketing/ads/CONCEPTS.md` for the cold-traffic sheet.

**Format per concept:** the hook as you'd say it to camera, what it argues. All 30 are short
vertical video for reels. Post organic to Instagram, cross-post to Facebook. **No copyrighted
music** — it blocks the piece from running as a paid ad later.

**Pick 15.** Run 15, not 30 — see the note at the bottom. Work by number: "kill 4, more like 9,
rewrite 12 harder."

---

## Bucket 1 — Questions (8)

**1. What this call actually is**
> "You booked a call. Here's what it is and what it isn't. About thirty minutes, we look at your
> real file, we tell you which of three doors you're standing in front of. Nobody's pitching you
> for thirty minutes."
Argues: it's a sorting call, not a sales ambush. Kills the flake reflex.

**2. Who this is for**
> "I'm going to tell you who we're actually good for, because if that's not you I'd rather you
> know before Thursday."
Argues: honest gating builds trust and self-selects.

**3. What the thirty-two dollar read is**
> "The thirty-two dollars is a soft look at your real file. It is not a bank application. It is
> not you getting approved. It's us seeing the truth instead of the number you typed in a form."
Argues: kills the "what am I paying for" confusion. Soft pull versus bank app.

**4. How we get paid**
> "I'll just tell you how we make money so you're not sitting there wondering."
Argues: transparency on the deposit plus the ten percent later. Removes hidden-fee fear.

**5. Why there's a call at all**
> "You're probably wondering why this isn't just a form. Here's what a form can't tell me."
Argues: justifies the thirty minutes. Raises the perceived value of showing up.

**6. What we actually look at**
> "Let me show you the four things I'm looking at when your file comes up on my screen."
Argues: demystifies the process. Makes them curious to see their own.

**7. Why we turn people down**
> "We say no to a lot of people, and it's not personal. Here's the line."
Argues: scarcity and credibility at once. Pre-frames the honest no.

**8. We're not a credit repair company**
> "People put us in a bucket we don't belong in. Here's the actual difference."
Argues: separates us from the category they've been burned by.

## Bucket 2 — Follow-up questions (6)

**9. What ten percent actually means**
> "People hear ten percent and their brain goes somewhere bad. Let me show you the math on a real
> number."
Argues: the second question after 4. What they pay up front is part of it, not on top.

**10. What happens right after the soft pull**
> "So we pull the file. Then what? Here's the next seventy-two hours."
Argues: the second question after 3. Removes the black-box feeling.

**11. Soft pull versus hard pull**
> "Does this ding my credit? No, and here's the difference between the two kinds of pull."
Argues: an objection hiding inside a question. Process only.

**12. What the three doors are**
> "I said three doors. Let me actually name them so you know which one you're probably walking
> through."
Argues: the second question after 1. Pre-sorts them before the call.

**13. What the deposit goes toward**
> "You're not paying me to hope. Here's what that money buys the day it clears."
Argues: the second question after 4. Turns a fee into a purchase.

**14. What happens to the people you turn down**
> "I said we say no. That doesn't mean we hang up. Here's what we do instead."
Argues: the second question after 7. Removes the fear of wasting the call.

## Bucket 3 — Objections (10)

**15. The other guys want eight to ten thousand**
> "You've probably seen the ten-thousand-dollar version of this. That's not our number and I'll
> tell you why theirs is that high."
Argues: price anchoring, without naming a competitor.

**16. Is this a scam**
> "Go ahead and Google us. I'd rather you do it now than sit on the call half-suspicious."
Argues: meets the search head-on. Point at the proof.

**17. Will it work for me specifically**
> "Every person on a call thinks their situation is the weird one. Here's how we figure out
> whether yours actually is."
Argues: reframes from promise to process.

**18. I've tried something like this before**
> "If you already paid somebody and nothing happened, you're not the exception, you're most of my
> calls. Here's what was probably different."
Argues: validates the burn, separates us from what burned them.

**19. Do I need a business**
> "No. You don't need a business, you don't need revenue, you don't need an LLC to be on this
> call."
Argues: kills the most common silent disqualifier.

**20. I don't have the cash right now**
> "Say that on the call. Don't cancel over it. There's a version of this that starts smaller than
> you think."
Argues: stops the money-shame cancel. Do not promise a specific number on camera.

**21. Why can't I just do this myself**
> "You can. Genuinely. Here's what doing it yourself actually costs you, and it isn't money."
Argues: respects the DIY instinct instead of insulting it.

**22. Can I put this on payments**
> "Some of it, yes. Most of it, no. Let me be straight about which is which."
Argues: pre-answers the finance question so the closer isn't cornered. **Facts matter here — only
the two courses finance. Everything else is cash. Do not blur it.**

**23. What if I don't qualify**
> "Worst case you spend thirty-two dollars and walk away knowing exactly where you stand. That's
> not nothing."
Argues: shrinks the downside of showing up.

**24. I need to talk to my spouse**
> "Bring them. Seriously, put them on the call. It goes better."
Argues: removes the classic stall by inviting it in.

## Bucket 4 — Expectations (6)

**25. How long this takes**
> "Nobody tells you the timeline up front, so I will."
Argues: sets a realistic clock. Stages and milestones, no dates promised.

**26. What we need from you**
> "There are three things we need from you and if you don't have them the call is short."
Argues: pre-loads the ask so the call doesn't stall.

**27. What approved does and doesn't mean**
> "Approved doesn't always come with a number attached. That's normal and here's why."
Argues: resets the expectation that trips people at the funding stage.

**28. What the first week looks like**
> "Here's your actual calendar for the seven days after you say yes."
Argues: makes the abstract concrete. Reduces post-sale anxiety.

**29. How much work is this on you**
> "This isn't hands-off and I'm not going to pretend it is. Here's your part."
Argues: honest effort-setting. Filters out people who'd churn anyway.

**30. What we're not going to do**
> "Let me tell you the three things we will never do, so you know what you're buying."
Argues: negative framing reads as honesty and kills three objections at once.

## Notes before the shoot

1. **Run 15, not 30.** Thirty is the top of the range and your budget won't deliver frequency
   across all of them. Pick 15 from this sheet, hold the rest as the swap set when the first
   batch fatigues.
2. **Course naming.** Older sales docs say *Funding Mastery*; the 2026-09-03 commit says
   *Capital Blueprint* at $5,000. Same thing renamed, or two products? None of the 30 name a
   course, so it isn't blocking, but settle it before anything says one on camera.
3. **Where the swipe-up lands.** One testimonial page, per the 2026-09-04 decision. Needs to
   exist before these run.
