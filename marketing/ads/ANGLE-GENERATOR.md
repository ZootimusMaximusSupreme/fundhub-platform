# The Angle Generator

An angle isn't a creative act. It's a combination. This file is the machine that makes them, so the
next forty concepts take an afternoon instead of a week.

Open this and `ASSET-BANK.md`. That's all you need.

---

## The formula

> **ANGLE = one ENEMY × one MECHANISM × one AUDIENCE**
>
> **HOOK = [Experience or Behavior] + [Emotion] + [Desire Frustration]**
>
> **BODY = [Validate the experience] → [Reveal the mechanism] → [Connect to the desire] → [Proof]**

18 enemies × 5 mechanisms × 6 audiences = **540 possible angles.** You'll never run out. The work is
picking the good ones, not inventing them.

---

## How to build one, start to finish

**Step 1 — Pick an enemy.** What did the industry do to this person?
Example: *brokers who blast a generic lender list and hand back a lower score.*

**Step 2 — Pick the mechanism that kills it.** Not any mechanism — the one that specifically answers
that enemy.
Example: *the Conveyor Belt — clean, apply, sweep, repeat.*

**Step 3 — Pick who's listening.** Audience plus FICO gate.
Example: *the Credit-Protective Planner, 600+.*

**Step 4 — Write the hook using the hook formula.** Experience + emotion + frustration.
Example: *"If you've ever watched your score drop twenty points from applying for funding, that
wasn't bad luck. That was somebody applying in the wrong order."*

**Step 5 — Check it against the mechanism test** (below). If it passes, it's an ad.

---

## The mechanism test — the one rule that matters

> **Delete the mechanism from your hook. Does the hook still make sense?**
>
> If yes, throw it out. The mechanism name has to be doing the differentiating work, or you've
> written an ad any competitor could run word for word.

Second half of the same rule: **end the hook by indicting the alternative, not by asking for
anything.** The ask comes later.

---

## Hook shapes that work

Five observed shapes. Rotate them so forty concepts don't all open the same way.

| Shape | How it opens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dated authority + number** | A real figure, stated flat | "Only 41% of businesses get the funding they need." |
| **Declare the promise dead** | Kill the industry's claim | "The fintech promise is broken." |
| **Absolve, blame the system** | Take it off their shoulders | "You are not alone. The system is the problem, not you." |
| **Insider says the opposite** | Contradict the expected | "Why I tell business owners to stop applying." |
| **Short mechanism line** | State the machine, plainly | "Our funding team isn't human. It's bionic." |

---

## Hook by awareness level

Match the hook to how much they already know. A product-aware hook on an unaware audience dies.

| They are | Open with |
|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Story, identity, pattern interrupt |
| **Problem-aware** | Agitation — name the pain out loud |
| **Solution-aware** | Why most solutions fail |
| **Product-aware** | Proof and comparison |
| **Most aware** | Direct CTA and offer specifics |

---

## The three altitudes

Write the same avatar three ways and pick by specificity. Specific almost always wins.

- **Broad** — desire or emotion: *"If you've been trying to get funding and keep hearing no…"*
- **Mid** — experience or behavior: *"If you've applied at three banks this year…"*
- **Specific** — stacked sub-avatar: *"If you're sitting on a 680 with two collections you keep
  meaning to deal with…"*

---

## The angle mix — what a balanced set of ten looks like

Per audience, ten angles: **3 speed · 2 proof · 2 risk-removal · 2 authority · 1 comparison/ROI.**

Eight categories to pull from: **Speed · Certainty · Risk removal · Status/identity · Process
transparency · Proof · Mistakes · Comparison.**

---

## The gate mix

Every concept gets a gate. Default is 600.

| Gate | Share | Line that carries it |
|---|---|---|
| **600+** | ~55% | "If you're at a 600 or better…" |
| **700+, no negatives** | ~20% | "If you're at 700 with a clean file…" |
| **Premium / strict** | ~15% | "Verifiable income, 800 FICO, wants a lot of capital fast, isn't desperate" |
| **Open** | ~10% | No gate — pure volume, feeds the courses |

**Disqualification is a hook device.** Telling someone who this *isn't* for makes the people who do
fit lean in. "If you're under 600, this one isn't for you" is a legitimate opening.

## Concept format — what a concept looks like before it's a script

Four lines. Scannable in seconds. This is what goes in `CONCEPTS.md`.

```
### C-07 · "The Order You Apply In"
HOOK   — [the actual first three seconds, word for word, to camera]
ANGLE  — [the argument, one line]
WHO    — [audience] · [FICO gate]
DOOR   — [funded / fixed / educated / sorted]
```

Nothing else. If it needs a paragraph to explain, it isn't a hook.

---

## Script format — once a concept is picked

```
HOOK    0–3s     The concept's hook, verbatim
BODY    10–60s   Validate → Reveal mechanism → Connect to desire → Proof
CTA     last 10–30s   Two-minute application. Book the call.
CLOSE                  No hard inquiry. No obligation. Nothing moves until you say so.

RUNTIME 60–90s | 90–120s | 2min+
SHOOT   Outfit + location note
TAG     origin_angle value for CRM message match
```

**Minimum 60 seconds. No exceptions.** (owner-set 2026-09-01)

Every ad matches the four that are running — full direct-response length, not a 30-second cut. The
controls run a minute and up and they book calls at $32–36, so the length is doing work: there is
time to name the cost, reveal the mechanism, and land the proof before the ask.

A short hook is fine. A short *ad* is not. If a concept only has 30 seconds of substance in it, it
isn't finished — go back and give it the mechanism in full.

| Runtime | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **60–90s** | The default. Most concepts live here. |
| **90–120s** | Concepts carrying the full Conveyor Belt walkthrough, or a case study. |
| **2min+** | Founder-story and VSL-style. A few, not many. |

**`origin_angle` matters.** The setter and closer scripts mirror the angle the lead came in on. A
lead from the broker-burn ad gets opened differently than one from the speed ad. The CRM carries the
field — use it.

## Test priority

When something isn't working, fix in this order. Don't skip up the list.

> **Hook > Angle > Creative > Landing page > Offer language**

Hooks are cheap to change and account for most of the variance. Offer language is expensive and
accounts for the least.

---

## The debugging map

Read the metric, fix the named layer. Don't guess.

| What you see | What's actually broken |
|---|---|
| Low CTR | Hook or angle mismatch — or the wrong avatar entirely |
| High CTR, low opt-in | Message match between ad and page, or weak offer framing |
| High opt-in, low booked calls | Missing trust and proof gates on the funnel |
| Booked but low show | Commitment friction, weak reminder sequence |
| Show but low close | Offer clarity, objection handling, qualification |
| Closes but churns or refunds | Overpromising — the ad wrote a check fulfillment can't cash |

**Benchmarks:** cold CTR under 0.3% → swap the hook. CPL over 3x target → pause the ad. Book rate
under 20% of leads → the application page or the qualification flow, not the ad.

**Don't decide anything before ~50–100 clicks or 10–30 leads.** Early numbers are noise.

**Current baseline to beat: $32–36 per booked call.**

## Production constraint — read before generating anything

Chris films every ad himself. Twenty ads means five to ten outfits, mixed and matched, different
backdrops. **If they look the same, Meta flags them.**

So:
- Twenty great ads beats sixty decent ones. The shoot day sets the number, not ambition.
- Every concept needs a distinct visual identity, not just distinct words.
- Group by outfit when scheduling the shoot so he changes clothes five times, not twenty.
- Sedona is the location. Use it — the backdrop variety is free differentiation.

## What kills a concept

Run every one of these before a concept goes on the sheet:

1. **Fails the mechanism test.** Delete the mechanism, hook still works → dead.
2. **Duplicate of another concept** in argument, not just wording.
3. **Sounds like an AI wrote it.** Read it out loud. If Chris wouldn't say it, rewrite it.
4. **Uses a banned word** from `ASSET-BANK.md` §8.
5. **Names the tech stack.** Never. It's "our system."
6. **Leads with white-label.** Never as the lead.
7. **Asks for something in the hook.** The hook indicts the alternative. The CTA asks.
