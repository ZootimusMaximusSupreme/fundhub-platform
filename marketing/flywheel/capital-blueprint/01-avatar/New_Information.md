## 1. Emerging Trend/Statistic
### Lenders are moving from "What's your credit score?" to "Can your cash flow cover the payment?"

**Source:**
- **Primary:** NAGGL, on SBA Procedural Notice 5000-875701 (issued January 16, 2026; effective March 1, 2026). https://www.naggl.org/sba-notice-formally-announcing-sunsetting-of-sbss-scoring-and-providing-new-underwriting-requirements-for-7a-small-loans/ (also covered by the Experian Business Information blog, July 22, 2026)
- **Supporting:** Experian News blog, Molly Poppie, September 29, 2026. https://www.experian.com/blogs/news/2026/09/29/cash-flow-insights-to-small-businesses/

**The New Information:**
- As of **March 1, 2026**, the SBA stopped using the FICO SBSS score for 7(a) small loans.
- 7(a) loans **under $350K** now need a **debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) of at least 1.1:1** on historical and/or projected cash flow. They are analysed the same way as similar non-SBA commercial loans.
- Lenders' internal models **may not rely solely on consumer credit scores**.
- Experian launched Cashflow Attributes for commercial lending. It reports "up to a 24% lift in predictive performance when paired with traditional commercial credit data."
- Experian's exact words: *"The value is not in replacing established credit information, but in adding relevant signals when the commercial credit file does not yet tell the full story."*
- **[PARAPHRASE]** A related FinRegLab study (June 2025) found the largest cash-flow gains for low-score entrepreneurs whose businesses are under five years old. ⚠ VERIFY: locate the FinRegLab study directly before citing.

**Why it's NEW:**
Most funding marketing still tells owners to "fix your credit score to qualify." For small SBA loans, the main test has shifted from a single score to provable cash-flow coverage. A major credit bureau is also saying publicly that the credit file alone is incomplete for many businesses. Competitor content hasn't caught up. This gives younger and lower-score businesses a credible, new way to understand their situation.

> **⚠ Conflict to resolve before downstream use:** The CRS report (IN12549, April 28, 2025) describes SOP 50 10 8 raising the minimum SBSS score from 155 to 165 (effective June 1, 2025). The March 2026 notice above **sunsets SBSS for 7(a) small loans**, so the "155 → 165" point is now outdated for that product. **Do not use the SBSS-165 hooks.** These other SOP 50 10 8 changes may still be relevant, pending confirmation with an SBA lender:
> - the personal-resources / "credit elsewhere" test
> - the Small Loan maximum cut from $500K to $350K

**How to Use Ethically:**
- Cite the notice number and effective date. Explain DSCR in plain language (cash available ÷ debt payments).
- Never imply guaranteed SBA approval. Tell owners to confirm details with an SBA lender, since the SBA has revised this notice at least once.
- Use Experian's figure exactly as an **"up to" 24% lift in predictive performance**. It is **not** an approval-rate increase.
- Don't say credit no longer matters. Experian says cash-flow data **adds to** the credit file and doesn't replace it.

**3 Marketing Hook Samples:**
1. "As of March 1, 2026, the SBA stopped using its small-business credit score for small 7(a) loans. Here's the number that matters now."
2. "1.1. That's the minimum cash-flow coverage ratio the SBA now looks for on 7(a) loans under $350K. Do you know yours?"
3. "Even Experian says your credit file 'does not yet tell the full story.' The Capital Blueprint helps you tell the rest."

## 2. Contrarian Expert Opinion
### Bankruptcy professionals say stacked merchant cash advances are showing up in case after case

**Source:**
Bloomberg Law, Alex Wolf, "Merchant Cash Advances Piling Up in Small-Business Bankruptcies," February 24, 2026. https://news.bgov.com/bankruptcy-law/merchant-cash-advances-piling-up-in-small-business-bankruptcies

**The New Information:**
- Bankruptcy filings listing merchant cash advance funders as creditors surged in 2023 and **peaked in 2024 at more than 230 cases**.
- The US MCA market is about **$20B**, projected to exceed **$30B**.
- **Case example:** Rogers Landworks LLC listed **21 MCA deals totalling more than $3.6M** before filing Chapter 11.
- **Kathleen DiSanto**, bankruptcy trustee (Bush Ross PA): *"I can't think of a case in a long time where I haven't seen them."*
- **Patricia Fugée**, attorney (FisherBroyles): *"It begs for regulation. It begs for education."*

**Why it's NEW:**
Competitors either sell MCAs or vaguely call them "expensive." The bankruptcy-court view is almost absent from funding marketing. It moves the problem from "high cost" to "a path that, when stacked, can end in court." This is a contrarian position in a market built on speed. Fugée's call for *education* fits an education-first Blueprint offer.

**How to Use Ethically:**
- Quote the experts exactly, with name, firm, and source. Do not shorten or reword their words inside quotation marks.
- Don't claim that all MCAs lead to bankruptcy. A single advance can be a legitimate tool. The risk discussed here is stacking and having no plan.
- Present the Blueprint as education and planning done *before* urgent money becomes the only option. Don't use it as a scare tactic.
- **Hook correction:** An earlier draft hook attributed the phrase "last Hail Mary" to bankruptcy lawyers. That phrase does **not** appear in the verified notes, so it has been removed. Only restore it if the exact wording and speaker are confirmed in the article.
- **Hook correction:** An earlier draft hook described DiSanto as a "Florida" trustee. That location isn't in the notes, so confirm it in the article before using it.

**3 Marketing Hook Samples:**
1. "'I can't think of a case in a long time where I haven't seen them.' That's a bankruptcy trustee describing merchant cash advances (Bloomberg Law, Feb 2026). Here's how to plan so you never need a stack of them."
2. "One company signed 21 cash advances before filing Chapter 11. Stacking doesn't start as a strategy. It starts as having no plan."
3. "A bankruptcy attorney told Bloomberg Law that MCAs 'beg for education.' That's what the Capital Blueprint is: education before you sign."

## 3. New Case Study or Data Point
### Most online-lender borrowers say the real cost was higher than they expected

**Source:**
America's Credit Unions, Curt Long (Chief Economist), March 31, 2026. This is an analysis of the Federal Reserve's 2025 Small Business Credit Survey / 2026 Report on Employer Firms. https://www.americascreditunions.org/node/25651
⚠ VERIFY: confirm the figures against the primary Fed report at fedsmallbusiness.org before publishing.

**The New Information:**
- **Cost surprise:** 60% of online-lender borrowers said actual costs were higher than expected. The figure was 37% at small banks and 32% at large banks.
- **Satisfaction:** 35% for online lenders, compared with 76% for credit unions and 64–65% for banks.
- **Market share:** online lenders received about 29% of applications.
- **Offer acceptance:** online lenders had the lowest acceptance rate, 84% compared with 94% at credit unions.

**Why it's NEW:**
Competitors compete on speed and approval odds. Almost nobody uses Fed data showing that the real pain is the **cost surprise**: money borrowers didn't understand before signing. This lines up directly with the Blueprint's promise of clarity before signing.

**How to Use Ethically:**
- Cite it as Fed survey data via America's Credit Unions.
- Frame it as **borrower perception**, not proof that any lender is predatory.
- Position the Blueprint as clarity before signing. Never present it as a guarantee of cheaper capital.

**3 Marketing Hook Samples:**
1. "In the Fed's latest survey, 60% of business owners who borrowed from online lenders said it cost more than they expected. The Capital Blueprint is built so you know what it costs before you sign."
2. "Nearly 3 in 10 small-business loan applications go to online lenders, the type of lender owners rated lowest for satisfaction. Know your options before you apply."
3. "Getting approved isn't the hard part. Understanding the true cost before you sign is."
