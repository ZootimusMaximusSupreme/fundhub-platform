## 2. The Client Journey (The Process)

*Methodology becomes Process: the Foundation's four internal steps (Assess, Diagnose, Blueprint, Route), retold from the client's side.*

> ⚠️ **[NOT ON FILE]:** The real delivery format, number of calls, timeline, and support period. The journey below is the **shape** of the experience. Do not add specifics such as "two strategy calls" or "a 20-page report" until Chris supplies them.

### Step 0: "I decide to stop guessing." (Enrollment)
- The owner commits to the Blueprint. **[FACT]** It costs $5,000 and **can be financed**, so cash flow does not have to block the decision.
- *What the client feels:* relief at having a defined next move instead of another blind application.
- **[NOT ON FILE]:** Whether the $32 UnderwriteIQ assessment is the usual entry point before this step, or is included inside the Blueprint.

### Step 1: "I find out where I stand, without hurting my credit." (Assess)
- Fundhub runs the **UnderwriteIQ** assessment. **[FACT]** It is a **soft pull**.
- *What the client experiences:* the owner gets a look at their profile with no hard inquiry, so the fear of "will checking hurt me?" is answered at the start.

### Step 2: "I finally see what the lender sees." (Diagnose)
- Fundhub reads the profile the way an underwriter would and translates it into plain terms: what is helping approval and what is hurting it.
- If the owner has been **declined before**, this is where they learn *why*. **[FACT]** Fundhub already offers a Decline Autopsy product, which shows it has this diagnostic capability. **[INFERENCE]** That capability is applied here.
- *What the client experiences:* the "black box" of lending opens up. Past declines stop feeling random.

### Step 3: "I get my plan." (Blueprint)
- The owner receives a **written, sequenced plan**: what to fix first, what to apply for, and in what order. **[INFERENCE]**
- *What the client experiences:* a to-do list they can follow, replacing "apply wherever and hope."
- **[NOT ON FILE]:** The format (document, dashboard, recorded walkthrough, or other) and the delivery timeline.

### Step 4: "I choose how to execute." (Route)
- The owner picks their path. **[FACT]** Every option below exists in-house:
  - **Execute it themselves**, following the plan.
  - **Fix credit first** with Credit repair done-for-you ($1,000) or the Repair test run ($200), if the diagnosis calls for it.
  - **Hand off execution** to Funding done-for-you ($3,000 + 10% success fee).
- *What the client experiences:* no dead end and no handoff to strangers. The same company that diagnosed the file can carry out any next step.

> **Upsell sensitivity:** Step 4 must be positioned as *the client's choice, driven by their diagnosis*. It must not read as a funnel to more spending. If the Blueprint feels like a $5,000 sales pitch for a $3,000 service, it confirms the plan-versus-money objection. **[INFERENCE]**

## 6. The Unique Mechanism

*Differentiator becomes the Unique Mechanism: the named "how" that explains why this works when guessing doesn't.*

### Proposed name: **The Underwriter's-Eye Sequence**

> **Read your file the way an underwriter will. Then apply in the order that file can support.**

**Status:** Proposed name. It needs Chris's approval. **[NOT ON FILE]** Trademark availability has not been checked, so do not use ™ or ® until it is cleared.

**Why this name:**
- **"Underwriter's-Eye"** captures the core differentiator: Fundhub starts from *how a lender judges the file*, using its own named assessment, **UnderwriteIQ**. The name echoes "Underwrite" so the mechanism and the assessment read as one system.
- **"Sequence"** captures what competitors who hand over a lender list don't offer: **order**. It is not only *where* to apply but *what first, what next, and what to fix before either*.
- **It is easy to say on a sales call.** "We run you through the Underwriter's-Eye Sequence" sounds like a method, not a pitch.

**The mechanism in three moves:**
1. **See it.** A soft-pull UnderwriteIQ assessment shows the file with no hard inquiry. **[FACT: soft pull]**
2. **Read it.** Fundhub translates the file the way an underwriter would: what helps, what hurts, and why past declines happened. **[INFERENCE]**
3. **Sequence it.** The plan orders the owner's moves: fix first, then apply in the order the file can support. **[INFERENCE]**

**Why a named mechanism is necessary here (Schwartz):** **[INFERENCE]** Business funding is a crowded, claim-saturated market. Prospects have heard "we'll get you funded" from many brokers. In a sophisticated market, a bigger promise loses. A *new and believable mechanism* is what wins. The Underwriter's-Eye Sequence gives the prospect a reason to believe the outcome could be different *this time*, which directly answers the objection "I've already been declined. Why would this be different?"

## 7. The Hidden Mechanisms

*The reasons **why** the Underwriter's-Eye Sequence works. This is the "because" that makes the mechanism believable.*

### Hidden Mechanism 1: Soft pulls show the file without leaving a mark
- **[INDUSTRY PRINCIPLE]** Soft inquiries are not visible to lenders as credit-seeking activity and do not affect credit scores. Hard inquiries, which come with actual applications, can.
- **[FACT]** UnderwriteIQ is a soft pull.
- **Why it matters:** The owner can learn the truth about their file *before* spending any hard inquiries. Diagnosis comes at no credit cost, so the first move carries no risk.

### Hidden Mechanism 2: Underwriters and owners are reading different documents
- **[INFERENCE]** An owner thinks about their business: revenue, potential, plans. An underwriter reads a *file*: credit history, utilization, inquiries, time in business, entity data. The owner usually can't see that file the way the underwriter does.
- **Why it matters:** Most declines come from this information gap, not from the business being bad. Translating the file closes the gap before the application, not after the rejection.

### Hidden Mechanism 3: Declines have causes, and causes can be diagnosed
- **[FACT]** Fundhub built a dedicated Decline Autopsy product, which shows that decline reasons are specific enough to analyze.
- **[INFERENCE]** A decline that feels random usually traces back to identifiable factors in the file.
- **Why it matters:** If the cause is known, it can be addressed. This turns "I got declined" into "I know what to fix." It is the believable answer to "Why would this time be different?"

### Hidden Mechanism 4: Order changes outcomes
- **[INDUSTRY PRINCIPLE]** Lenders generally consider recent inquiries and newly opened accounts when they evaluate an application. Each application can therefore affect how the next lender reads the file.
- **[INFERENCE]** This means the *sequence* of applications matters, not only the choice of lenders. Applying randomly can put weaker-fit applications first and leave the file looking worse for the applications that mattered most.
- **Why it matters:** This is the core reason "Sequence" is in the mechanism's name, and the main thing a list of lenders can't provide.
- ⚠️ Fundhub's actual sequencing logic is **[NOT ON FILE]**. Chris should confirm how the Blueprint determines order before this hidden mechanism is used in copy.

### Hidden Mechanism 5: Fix first, apply second
- **[FACT]** Credit repair done-for-you ($1,000) and a Repair test run ($200) exist in Fundhub's lineup, which signals that credit issues are a common barrier for its prospects.
- **[INFERENCE]** Addressing flagged issues *before* applying avoids spending inquiries on applications the file was not yet ready to win.
- **Why it matters:** It flips the usual order (apply, get declined, then fix) into the order that respects how underwriting works.

### Hidden Mechanism 6: The diagnosis and the execution live under one roof
- **[FACT]** Fundhub offers every next step in-house: credit repair, done-for-you funding, and education.
- **[INFERENCE]** When the team that diagnosed the file also carries out the next step, nothing gets lost in a handoff, and the plan is written by people who know what execution actually requires.
- **Why it matters:** A plan is only as good as the owner's ability to act on it. Here, every branch of the plan connects to a real option.

### Hidden Mechanism 7: Fundability is built over time, not won once
- **[FACT]** Gene's testimonial describes funding received "over the last three years" across "three LLCs." *(He is not confirmed as a Blueprint client.)*
- **[INFERENCE]** Funding for established owners is often a series of approvals rather than a single event. Each one shapes the file for the next.
- **Why it matters:** A sequenced plan built on how underwriters read the file serves the owner's *next* application as well as the current one. This supports the identity desire: "I want to be fundable whenever the business needs capital."
