FUNDHUB REAL ESTATE LENDING MARKET RESEARCH
Verified 2026-11-02

PURPOSE

This research gives offer construction a real product map for one ecosystem with two connected markets:

1. Homeowners who need substantial money for a real home project.
2. People with strong, verifiable income or credit who want a path into real estate opportunities, including people who need preparation before they can qualify.

The public lender facts below show what can be marketed now as product discovery and preparation, what requires a contractor relationship, and what needs a direct commercial conversation before it becomes a Fundhub partner offer.

EXECUTIVE FINDINGS

1. HFS is the only researched provider with a current public claim of unsecured home-improvement financing to $450,000. HFS is a platform, not the originating lender. Its public pages do not name the lender that supplies the $450,000 option. It offers national coverage, a soft initial inquiry, direct-to-homeowner funding, fixed terms from 1 to 30 years, and a free contractor referral link.

2. BHG is the strongest clearly documented direct-to-consumer high-limit product. It advertises unsecured home-improvement loans from $20,000 to $250,000, fixed APRs from 6.49% to 28.89%, terms from 2 to 10 years, a soft initial rate check, and lump-sum consumer funding in as few as five days. The loans are funded by Pinnacle Bank or County Bank. The public minimums are 640 FICO and $100,000 verifiable annual income; BHG's current large-loan guidance says the maximum amounts usually call for 740+ credit and $150,000+ income.

3. Lyon is a financing consultant that pairs applicants with lenders. Its detailed contractor material and primary product bullets support unsecured financing to $200,000, while a separate hero statement says $250,000. It serves the 48 contiguous states, supports co-applicants, requires at least one applicant to be on the deed, and usually pays the contractor in stages after the customer confirms progress.

4. The next useful tier is $150,000–$250,000. Hearth advertises a multi-lender marketplace to $250,000. Next-Financing and Enhancify advertise marketplace access to $200,000. Navy Federal, FNBO Slice, Amerant Bank, UNFCU and Nelnet Bank reach $150,000 with very different membership, income, state, co-applicant and contractor conditions.

5. The product is not one universal loan. There are three operating lanes:
   - direct-to-homeowner funds: HFS, BHG and many marketplace personal-loan offers;
   - contractor-controlled or contractor-paid funds: FNBO Slice, Nelnet/Buildertrend, GreenSky and most Lyon projects;
   - mixed marketplaces: Hearth, Next-Financing, Enhancify, Acorn and Viking, where the actual lender and disbursement depend on the chosen offer.

6. Standard multi-person application structures are publicly documented. Navy Federal requires a qualified co-applicant for amounts above $50,000. BHG allows a cosigner or co-borrower. Lyon's application accepts a co-applicant and says at least one applicant must be on the deed. Nelnet's contractor terms recognize co-borrowers and cosigners. The public sources do not describe a paid unrelated borrower, paid guarantor, or temporary title partner program. That structure is therefore a separate partnership design question for each lender rather than a feature that can be inferred from an ordinary co-applicant form.

7. The best first commercial path is hybrid:
   - acquire and prepare homeowners;
   - route the cleanest high-limit profiles first to HFS and BHG;
   - recruit licensed contractors for FNBO, Nelnet, Lyon, GreenSky and other point-of-sale programs;
   - build a preparation track for strong-income applicants whose credit or files are not ready;
   - add secured home-equity, investor and non-QM products as clearly separate branches.

- HFS: third-party lender network not named publicly. Count HFS as one distribution platform until lender roster is obtained.
- BHG: Pinnacle Bank or County Bank funds consumer loans. Count those two banks as the funding layer, BHG as distribution/underwriting experience.
- Lyon: exclusive lenders not named publicly. Count Lyon as one consultant channel until roster is obtained.
- Hearth: 18+ lenders plus HELOC/card offers. Do not count the advertised 18 as unique until the roster is supplied.
- Next-Financing: 17 lenders, not named on the product page.
- Enhancify: 30+ lenders/network providers, not a single originator.
- GreenSky: Pinnacle Bank dba Synovus Bank and Comenity Capital Bank are the current listed program lenders.
- Upgrade: Cross River Bank originates the home-improvement loans.
- Acorn: 20+ lenders; visible examples include Best Egg and Upgrade. Upgrade/Cross River must not be counted again as a separate source when reached through Acorn.
- FNBO, Nelnet Bank, Navy Federal, Amerant, UNFCU and SoFi are direct lender/credit-union programs in this map.

MULTI-PERSON APPLICATION STRUCTURES ACTUALLY SHOWN IN PUBLIC SOURCES

Provider | Public structure | Key detail

Navy Federal | Qualified co-applicant | Required for $50,001–$150,000. The application collects income, employment and full identity information for both applicants.

BHG | Cosigner or co-borrower | Can be added at the beginning or during the process. Each person supplies identity and income documents. BHG accepts W-2, self-employment, business ownership, freelance, seasonal, Social Security, pension and disability income sources.

Lyon | Co-applicant | Application contains a full co-applicant section. At least one applicant must be the owner of record on the deed for the project property.

Nelnet / Buildertrend | Co-borrower or cosigner | Contractor terms recognize both. The borrower cannot also be the contractor. The public terms define a cosigner as responsible for the obligation without compensation.

Acorn | Co-borrower | Co-borrowers can be added after the initial applicant submits information; the final lender controls approval.

GreenSky | Joint applicants | Public merchant training says joint applicants are welcome on all applications.

HFS, Hearth, Next-Financing, Enhancify, FNBO Slice, Amerant and UNFCU | No broad joint-applicant rule confirmed in the reviewed public product pages | Ask the provider which products accept spouse, household member, non-occupant, non-owner, cosigner or co-borrower applications and whose income, credit and debts are counted.

The offer-construction implication is simple: Fundhub can build matching, readiness and application-preparation experiences around the applicant structures each provider actually accepts. The exact partner role must be a field in the lender matrix rather than one universal rule.

DISBURSEMENT AND CONTRACTOR MAP

Direct to homeowner

- HFS: proceeds paid to homeowner; homeowner controls contractor payment schedule.
- BHG: one lump sum to consumer.
- Hearth: public page says financing is disbursed directly to customer.
- Next-Financing: consumer receives proceeds and uses them for the project.
- Most ordinary personal-loan offers through Enhancify, Acorn and SoFi: consumer receives loan proceeds; final lender terms control.

Paid or controlled through contractor channel

- FNBO Slice: FNBO pays approved contractor by ACH; staged funding may apply.
- Nelnet / Buildertrend: contractor requests milestone funds; borrower approves each request; Nelnet pays contractor.
- GreenSky: merchant/contractor program with early and staged funding options.
- Lyon: most projects use staged contractor payments; some selected lenders may provide funds directly to consumer.
- Upgrade Home Improvement: project funding is sent to contractor.

This split should be visible in Fundhub routing because the two experiences attract different buyers. Direct-funding customers value control and contractor flexibility. Contractor-paid customers value coordinated draws, fewer payment handoffs and a program already attached to the job.

UNDERWRITING PATTERN FOR OFFER CONSTRUCTION

Published minimums are entry points, not maximum-funding profiles.

- BHG public entry: 640+ credit, $100,000+ verifiable income, two years of steady employment. BHG's current maximum-loan article says 740+ credit and $150,000+ income are usually needed for its largest amounts and longest terms. It evaluates DTI, credit history and multiple income streams.
- Lyon public entry: options may exist from 640; overall credit, payment history, income, debt, recent bankruptcies and tax liens are reviewed. At least one applicant must own the project property.
- Navy Federal: credit history and income are reviewed; more than $50,000 requires a qualified co-applicant.
- Amerant installment product: maximum equals 10% of gross annual income, so the amount itself creates a steep income screen.
- HFS, Hearth, Next-Financing, Enhancify, Acorn and Viking: the platform pages do not publish one universal maximum-funding score, income or DTI because the lender controls the offer.

Illustrative 10-year monthly payments, before fees, using ordinary amortization. These are capacity examples for internal offer design, not lender quotations:

Principal | 8% APR | 12% APR | 18% APR | 25% APR
$50,000 | $607 | $717 | $901 | $1,137
$100,000 | $1,213 | $1,435 | $1,802 | $2,275
$150,000 | $1,820 | $2,152 | $2,703 | $3,412
$200,000 | $2,427 | $2,869 | $3,604 | $4,550
$250,000 | $3,033 | $3,587 | $4,505 | $5,687

That payment scale explains why the best high-limit audience is not merely a homeowner with a project. It is a homeowner or applicant team with strong documented monthly cash flow, low existing debt, stable income and a project large enough to justify the application effort.

DOCUMENT PREPARATION FACTS

BHG's current application guide provides the most complete public checklist in this set.

W-2 applicants
- government photo ID and Social Security number;
- two recent pay stubs and/or most recent W-2;
- employer contact;
- proof of address;
- bank routing/account details for ACH.

Self-employed / 1099 applicants
- government photo ID and Social Security number;
- two years of federal returns including Schedule C or K-1;
- three to six months of personal bank statements;
- year-to-date profit and loss if requested;
- proof of address.

Business owners
- self-employed package;
- two years of business returns;
- three to six months of business bank statements;
- ownership documents;
- support for additional income streams.

Co-borrowers
- separate identity, credit, debt and income package for each applicant.

Project and property layer
- Lyon requires at least one applicant on the deed and a home-improvement contract before final loan documents/funding.
- FNBO, Nelnet and GreenSky are contractor programs, so contractor enrollment, a real invoice/contract and approved project funding path are central.
- HFS says the applicant can estimate the project cost at inquiry and refine it later.
- Direct personal-loan products may not require a contractor agreement upfront, but the provider can request purpose, identity, income and bank verification.

THE TWO-SIDED PRODUCT ECOSYSTEM

Homeowner demand side

Entry promise: compare substantial financing paths for a real project while keeping the current mortgage untouched when an unsecured option fits.

Primary segments:
- high-income owner planning a $100,000–$250,000 addition, pool, ADU, full remodel or outdoor project;
- dual-income household able to support a joint application;
- self-employed homeowner with strong documented cash flow but more complex files;
- homeowner who wants one direct lump sum and contractor control;
- homeowner already working with a contractor and better suited to staged point-of-sale financing;
- near-qualified homeowner who needs file preparation and time before applying.

Products to route:
- $100,000–$450,000 high-limit unsecured marketplace path: HFS;
- $20,000–$250,000 high-income unsecured path: BHG;
- pool/outdoor projects with deed ownership and staged payments: Lyon;
- co-applicant member path: Navy Federal;
- approved-contractor paths: FNBO Slice, Nelnet, GreenSky, Upgrade;
- broad multi-lender fallbacks: Hearth, Next-Financing, Enhancify, Acorn;
- $100,000 direct bank fallback: SoFi;
- secured home-equity branch: Figure and other HELOC/home-equity products;
- renovation mortgage/cash-out branch for projects where secured funding is preferred.

Qualified borrower and capital-partner side

Entry promise: organize the person's credit, income, debt capacity, real-estate goal and documentation, then match that profile to the provider structures and real-estate products that fit.

Product tracks:
- standard joint/co-borrower application where a provider accepts the relationship;
- property-owner/co-owner project preparation where the provider requires deed ownership;
- self-employed income-file preparation;
- credit and debt-capacity readiness queue;
- investor financing education and matching for DSCR rental, fix-and-flip, bridge and non-QM products;
- contractor, builder and project-partner recruitment;
- longer-term property acquisition, renovation and refinance products.

The person with strong credit or income is a separate avatar from the homeowner. Their reason to enter is access to structured real-estate opportunities, financing readiness, deal flow, education and a repeatable path to future products. The homeowner's reason is completing a project on workable monthly terms. The marketplace can connect the journeys only through the applicant and ownership structures a selected provider supports.

READINESS PRODUCT

The preparation lane is commercially important because it retains people who are valuable but not ready for the primary application today.

Core deliverables:
- verified income-source inventory;
- debt and monthly-payment inventory;
- credit-file readiness plan;
- W-2, self-employed or joint-applicant document vault;
- property/project file;
- contractor estimate and contract readiness;
- lender-fit profile;
- application timing plan;
- status-based rematch when the file changes.

Natural follow-on products:
- smaller unsecured loans and personal lines;
- contractor point-of-sale loans;
- credit-card funding for appropriate smaller projects;
- Figure or another HELOC for equity users;
- bank-statement/P&L/1099 mortgage options for self-employed borrowers;
- DSCR, bridge and fix-and-flip financing for investor properties;
- business credit and working-capital products for contractors;
- contractor sales, quoting, payment and financing enablement.

COMMERCIAL HYPOTHESES FOR THE OFFER TEAM

1. Lead with a project-financing comparison and preparation offer, not one lender name. The product landscape changes at $100,000, $150,000, $200,000 and $250,000, and the funding route changes by provider.

2. The highest-value lead is a documented household, not a raw consumer. The intake should capture owner status, state, project, requested amount, contractor status, income sources, monthly debt, housing payment, credit band, co-applicant availability and preferred funding route.

3. Two funnels can feed one operating system:
   - homeowner project funnel;
   - qualified borrower/capital-partner funnel.
   Each gets its own avatar, promise and VSL. Both feed the same readiness, documentation, lender-match and follow-up engine.

4. Contractor acquisition is a third revenue and distribution lane. A contractor can bring the project and homeowner; Fundhub can bring preparation, matching, status tracking and additional lender coverage.

5. HFS and BHG should be contacted first because they define the large-loan promise. Lyon, Hearth, Next-Financing and Enhancify determine contractor and marketplace coverage. FNBO and Nelnet determine the direct bank contractor path.

6. The strongest guarantee is a service guarantee around completion of Fundhub's preparation and matching work. Loan amount, rate, approval and funding remain provider decisions and should not be used as the service guarantee.
