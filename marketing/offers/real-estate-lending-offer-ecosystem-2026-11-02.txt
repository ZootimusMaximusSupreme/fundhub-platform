FUNDHUB REAL ESTATE LENDING OFFER ECOSYSTEM
Built: November 2, 2026

THE COMMERCIAL DECISION

Launch one operating system with two separate front doors:

1. Renovation Funding Map for homeowners with a real project.
2. Deal-Ready Profile for people with strong documented income, credit, or both who want a defined path into real estate.

The homeowner offer launches first because the demand, project, and lender route already exist. The Deal-Ready offer launches as a profile, readiness, and opportunity-matching product. Contractor acquisition becomes the third lane because contractors already control high-intent projects and several important lender programs.

The initial promise is not one lender, one maximum amount, or one universal borrower structure. Fundhub organizes the project or participant, shows the best available route, prepares the file, coordinates the handoff, and keeps the next route ready.

THE MARKET OPENING

Homeowners spent heavily on large projects even as ordinary personal-loan limits stayed below many renovation budgets. Current public programs show:

- HFS publicly advertises unsecured home-improvement financing to $450,000 through third-party lenders.
- BHG publicly advertises unsecured home-improvement loans to $250,000.
- Lyon repeatedly documents unsecured contractor financing to $200,000, with one current page also mentioning $250,000.
- Hearth advertises a marketplace to $250,000.
- Next-Financing and Enhancify advertise marketplace access to $200,000.
- Navy Federal, FNBO Slice, Nelnet Bank, Amerant, and UNFCU reach $150,000 in narrower structures.

The opportunity is bigger than a rate-comparison page. The customer needs to know whether a program fits the project amount, whether it pays the homeowner or contractor, what documents it needs, whether a joint applicant is accepted, and what the payment does to the household budget.

The best starting customer is a household earning roughly $200,000 to $350,000+, self-reporting good-to-excellent credit, carrying manageable monthly debt, and planning a $75,000 to $175,000 project with a real estimate or contractor. The campaign can capture larger requests, but the offer wins through the quality of the route map, not through the largest advertised maximum.

OFFER 1: RENOVATION FUNDING MAP

WHO IT IS FOR

Homeowners with a defined $50,000 to $250,000+ project who want to understand the payment, documents, funding recipient, contractor requirements, and best next application before they start applying.

CORE PROMISE

See the best realistic path for funding a major home project while keeping control of the first-mortgage decision.

DREAM OUTCOME

The homeowner completes the renovation they actually want, keeps the current mortgage unchanged when an unsecured route fits, preserves enough cash for overruns, and knows exactly how the project will be funded.

WHAT THE CUSTOMER GETS

1. Project Map
   - project type, amount, state, property, contractor, timeline, and payment schedule;
   - full-project amount and any practical phase-one amount.

2. Capacity Map
   - household income type and range;
   - housing payment and existing monthly debt;
   - self-reported credit band;
   - joint-applicant availability;
   - estimated payment range at several terms and APR assumptions.

3. Route Map
   - direct-to-homeowner options;
   - contractor-funded options;
   - suitable multi-lender marketplace paths;
   - separate secured alternatives when the customer chooses to compare them;
   - a smaller project or readiness route when the full request does not fit.

4. Readiness Pack
   - identity checklist;
   - W-2, self-employed, commission, retirement, or joint-applicant income checklist;
   - ownership and housing documents;
   - contractor estimate, contract, and project documents;
   - missing-item tracker.

5. Application Handoff
   - selected provider or partner route;
   - organized handoff packet;
   - application milestones;
   - status follow-up;
   - second route preserved if the first route changes.

6. Project Funding Tracker
   - application started;
   - documents pending;
   - decision received;
   - amount and terms offered;
   - borrower acceptance;
   - homeowner or contractor funded;
   - project follow-up.

THE OFFER STACK

Core asset: Personalized Renovation Funding Map.

Bonus 1: Payment Range Builder
The homeowner sees what $50,000, $100,000, $150,000, $200,000, and $250,000 mean as monthly obligations under several planning assumptions.

Bonus 2: Direct Funding vs. Contractor Funding Guide
The homeowner knows who receives the money and what that changes before selecting a route.

One organized place for income, ownership, project, and contractor records.

Shows which researched paths publicly support a co-applicant, co-borrower, or cosigner and what needs direct confirmation.

Bonus 5: Project Gap Plan

If the first selected provider does not return a workable offer, Fundhub reviews the next appropriate path using the completed intake.

SERVICE PROMISE

OFFER 2: DEAL-READY PROFILE

WHO IT IS FOR

People with strong, verifiable income, good credit, manageable obligations, or a useful combination of those strengths who want a real estate path but do not have the property, project, contractor network, financing route, or operating process.

CORE PROMISE

Turn the financial strength you already built into a clear real estate participation path, with the profile, documents, role selection, opportunity view, and coordination needed to make an informed decision.

DREAM OUTCOME

The participant stops watching real estate from the outside and enters a defined opportunity with a role they understand, complete information, organized documents, visible economics, a clear timeline, and a planned exit.

WHAT THE CUSTOMER GETS

1. Financial Readiness Profile
   - income source and documentation;
   - credit range and history summary supplied by the participant;
   - housing payment and current monthly obligations;
   - liquidity and timing;
   - state and location preferences.

2. Role Selector
   - direct borrower;
   - co-applicant or joint borrower where a selected provider accepts it;
   - property owner or co-owner;
   - project or capital partner;
   - direct buyer;
   - readiness-track participant;
   - future product customer.

3. Deal-Ready File
   - identity and income documents;
   - participation preferences;
   - borrowing goals;
   - role boundaries;
   - near-term personal financing goals;
   - missing-item plan.

4. Standard Opportunity View
   - property or project;
   - parties involved;
   - requested role;
   - use of funds;
   - responsibilities;
   - financing path;
   - timeline;
   - economics;
   - decision points;
   - planned exit.

5. Match and Coordination
   - current product or opportunity lane;
   - document coordination;
   - provider, homeowner, contractor, or project handoff;
   - status tracking;
   - future match when the current opportunity is not right.

6. Ready Track
   - exact baseline;
   - measurable credit, document, debt, income-seasoning, or liquidity milestones;
   - monthly checkpoint;
   - re-profile when milestones are complete;
   - routing into the best current product.

THE OFFER STACK

Core asset: Deal-Ready Profile.

Bonus 1: Role Fit Map
The participant selects how they want to be involved before seeing an opportunity.

Bonus 2: Deal Review Template
Every opportunity is viewed through the same questions, so the customer is not evaluating random pitches in random formats.

Bonus 3: Document Vault
The participant organizes the records needed for future matching and financing.

Bonus 4: 90-Day Ready Plan
People who are close but not ready leave with measurable milestones and a re-profile date.

Bonus 5: Lifetime Product Route
One profile can lead to owner-occupied renovation, direct personal financing, investment-property financing, DSCR, bank-statement, contractor, business-capital, or future property opportunities as the customer changes.

THE ROUTING LOGIC

HOMEOWNER ROUTING

Route A: Direct-to-homeowner high-limit
- defined project;
- wants control of contractor payment;
- strong documented profile;
- HFS or BHG first, then appropriate marketplace routes.

Route B: Contractor-funded
- approved contractor or project category;
- customer accepts staged contractor payment;
- Lyon, FNBO Slice, Nelnet, GreenSky, Upgrade, or network platform as appropriate.

Route C: Joint applicant
- household needs combined strength;
- route only to providers that accept the actual applicant relationship;
- Navy Federal is the clearest public high-limit co-applicant example, with other providers confirmed case by case.

Route D: Smaller unsecured
- $25,000 to $100,000 need;
- SoFi, Acorn, Upgrade, GreenSky, or other confirmed products.

Route E: Ready Track
- income, credit, debt, documents, ownership, or project timing needs work.

Route F: Separate property-backed product
- customer chooses a HELOC, home-equity loan, refinance, or renovation mortgage comparison.

PARTICIPANT ROUTING

Route 1: Match ready
- complete Deal-Ready Profile;
- documented strength;
- clear role;
- real opportunity available.

Route 2: Direct financing fit
- participant has their own eligible project or acquisition goal;
- route to the product family that fits that goal.

Route 3: Income strong, credit not ready
- Two-Round Ready Trial or Deal-Ready Concierge.

Route 4: Credit strong, documentation complex
- income-document preparation and a product family suited to the available evidence.

Route 5: Opportunity curious
- role education, milestone plan, smaller first step, and future re-profile.

THE FIRST CREATIVE TEST

Homeowner majority angles:

1. Keep the first mortgage.
2. A $150,000 project is not a normal personal-loan search.
3. Direct-to-homeowner versus contractor-paid financing.
4. Enough money to finish, not only enough to start.

Participant majority angles:

1. You have the profile, but not the deal.
2. Turn strong income and credit into a Deal-Ready Profile.
3. See the role, numbers, timeline, and exit before deciding.
4. Get a measurable readiness path if today is too early.

Contractor majority angles:

1. Stop losing six-figure projects because one lender caps out.
2. Add direct-to-homeowner and staged contractor funding to the same desk.
3. Know exactly where every financing application stands.
4. Turn declined or partial approvals into a second route.

FINAL RECOMMENDATION

Launch the Renovation Funding Map first. It has the clearest customer, project, provider universe, and contractor distribution path.

Build the Deal-Ready Profile at the same time as a separate front door. Begin with readiness and defined roles. Add live opportunity matching only as actual opportunities, provider structures, and operating partners are available.

Use the contractor Project Finance Desk to acquire homeowners with a real project already attached.

This creates one ecosystem with three acquisition lanes, a shared document and matching engine, and several honest ways to create lifetime value without forcing every person into the same loan.
