THE COMMERCIAL DECISION

Launch one operating system with two separate front doors:

1. Renovation Funding Map for high-income people who already own property and have a real project.
2. Deal-Ready Profile for high-income people with strong financials and good credit who want to enter real estate; they may not own property yet.

Start with home-improvement loans and the homeowner offer. The Deal-Ready offer is a separate profile, readiness, and opportunity-matching path. Contractor acquisition becomes the third lane because contractors already control high-intent projects and access to several researched lender programs.

The initial promise is not one lender, one maximum amount, or one universal borrower structure. Fundhub organizes the project or participant, shows the best available route, prepares the file, coordinates the handoff, and keeps the next route ready.

THE TWO PRIMARY CUSTOMER PROFILES

Real estate entrant:
- high income, strong financials, and good credit;
- wants to enter real estate but may have no property, project, or operating team yet;
- needs a Deal-Ready Profile, a defined role, and a suitable project or financing path.

Existing property owner:
- high income and already owns property;
- wants funding for a major home-improvement or property project;
- good credit is useful, but is not a universal marketing entry requirement for this profile;
- needs a Renovation Funding Map that compares the actual property, income, debt, credit, project, and program requirements.

These describe the target customer. Actual loan qualification, permitted use, ownership, credit, income, contractor, and state requirements come from each selected program. No universal real estate score floor is set here.

PROJECT BUDGET AND FINANCING TARGET

Use five request markers: $75,000, $100,000, $175,000, $250,000, and $500,000+.

Keep three amounts separate in every map:
1. Total project budget, including contingency and the customer's own contribution.
2. Combined financing target across all proposed facilities.
3. Each facility's requested and offered amount, subject to that lender's own limit.

The target range is $75,000 to $500,000+ in project requests. A $500,000+ project or combined financing request is not a claim that one home-improvement lender offers that amount. Loans or lines may be combined where each program permits the structure, timing, debt exposure, and intended use. Do not promise a guaranteed combined total or unrestricted proceeds.

For every proposed facility, record amount, lender/program, term, rate, payment, permitted use, borrower, recipient of funds, security, application stage, and conditions. Then show total financing, total monthly payments, existing obligations, aggregate debt burden/DTI on a stated income basis, available reserves, and any remaining project gap. Review each next application against the obligations already accepted or funded; do not reuse an earlier debt picture unchanged.

PRODUCT ORDER

Home-improvement loans first. Potential later branches are refinance, HELOC, and business equity lines through lender partners with nationwide reach and a confirmed state/product matrix. These are future partner routes, not established Fundhub lender relationships or universal nationwide availability.

THE MARKET OPENING

The November 2 research snapshot showed large-project demand and the following publicly advertised program limits. These are individual-program research figures, not Fundhub partner commitments or combined-financing limits:

- HFS publicly advertises unsecured home-improvement financing to $450,000 through third-party lenders.
- BHG publicly advertises unsecured home-improvement loans to $250,000.
- Lyon repeatedly documents unsecured contractor financing to $200,000, with one current page also mentioning $250,000.
- Hearth advertises a marketplace to $250,000.
- Next-Financing and Enhancify advertise marketplace access to $200,000.
- Navy Federal, FNBO Slice, Nelnet Bank, Amerant, and UNFCU reach $150,000 in narrower structures.

The opportunity is bigger than a rate-comparison page. The customer needs to know whether a program fits the project amount, whether it pays the homeowner or contractor, what documents it needs, whether a joint applicant is accepted, and what the payment does to the household budget.

The proposed starting income profile is a household earning roughly $200,000 to $350,000+, with manageable monthly debt and a $75,000 to $500,000+ project request. This income range is a planning profile, not an owner-final minimum or lender rule. Prioritize the five request markers above, an estimate or defined scope, and a workable payment plan. Collect the property owner's actual credit facts without making good credit a universal entry gate; the real estate entrant profile still targets strong financials and good credit.

SERVICE PROMISE

Once the homeowner completes the required intake, Fundhub delivers the route summary, document checklist, and next-step recommendation within two business days. If information is missing, the homeowner receives the exact missing-item list instead of a silent delay.

For a paid engagement validly using the current Funding Agreement, both action clocks begin at signing: work begins within 24 business hours and the first authorized applications are submitted within 72 business hours. Client delay or error does not pause either clock. If Fundhub misses either deadline, the client may request return of the deposit. If both deadlines are met, lack of approval or funding alone does not return the deposit. No back-end success fee is due without funding; nonwaivable cancellation, refund, and dispute rights remain intact.

Written withdrawal of application authority stops new applications and does not undo applications already submitted. The agreement allows limited status follow-up afterward. Do not add a new cancellation window, approval deadline, or payment clock to these signed terms.

THE FIRST CREATIVE TEST

Homeowner majority angles:

1. Keep the first mortgage.
2. A $175,000 or $500,000+ project needs a complete financing map.
3. Direct-to-homeowner versus contractor-paid financing.
4. Enough money to finish, not only enough to start.

Participant majority angles:

1. You have the profile, but not the deal.
2. Turn strong income and credit into a Deal-Ready Profile.
3. See the role, numbers, timeline, and exit before deciding.
4. Get a measurable readiness path if today is too early.

Contractor majority angles:

1. Stop losing six-figure projects because one lender caps out.
2. Add direct-to-homeowner and staged contractor funding to the same desk.
3. Know exactly where every financing application stands.
4. Turn declined or partial approvals into a second route.

FINAL RECOMMENDATION

Start with home-improvement loans and the Renovation Funding Map for high-income property owners. Use $75,000, $100,000, $175,000, $250,000, and $500,000+ request markers; distinguish the project from each facility and assess permitted combinations as a complete payment and debt picture.

Owner decision for this draft: approve the homeowner free-map-to-paid-service model using the existing $3,000 credited deposit and 10% approved-funding economics, with written coverage for the actual renovation facility before collection. This is the recommended first model; approval of the model does not itself confirm a lender route or activate a fee on every consumer loan.

Build the Deal-Ready Profile at the same time as a separate front door. Begin with readiness and defined roles. Add live opportunity matching only as actual opportunities, provider structures, and operating partners are available.

Keep the primary entrant profile high-income, financially strong, and good-credit, with no current-property prerequisite. Keep the property-owner profile high-income without a universal good-credit entry gate. Each actual lender decision still uses that product's own requirements and permitted use.

Use the contractor Project Finance Desk to acquire homeowners with a real project already attached.

This creates one ecosystem with three acquisition lanes, a shared document and matching engine, and several honest ways to create lifetime value without forcing every person into the same loan.
