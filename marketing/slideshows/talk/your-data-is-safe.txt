3. PD-03 / The full record — A dispute letter names the month and code of a late, and only the full record shows both.

A dispute letter has to name the exact month and the code of a late, and only the full record shows you BOTH.

This is one account from the sample report, a Toyota auto loan, closed and paid as agreed.

4. PD-04 / Your business — What does a bank see when it looks up your business?

What does a bank see when it looks up your BUSINESS? Your business has a file of its own, and we read it the same way we read yours.

First comes the match. We look up your company in LexisNexis Top Business. You pick it from the list, so your file lands on the right business.

The Experian Business check reads five things. It reads your business score, any blemishes, your business balances, your industry code and your business name.

Each one comes with its exact FIX. Balances get paid down under 10% before you apply, and the name matches everywhere the company is listed.

The industry code a lender reads has to come off the low-risk list, and the check flags any other code.

A business that clears those five rows shows a bank a company it can lend to.

5. PD-05 / Three letters — Each dispute letter names the field we need the bureau to review.

When every letter names the exact field, your file gets ready for funding QUICKER. That's what all this deeper data is for.

Each thing we find on your file goes into one of three letters. One is for accounts, one for your name and old addresses, and one for inquiries.

Each letter goes where it can change the line: to the bureau that printed it, or straight to the company that reported it.

6. PD-06 / Handled right — Your most sensitive identifier gets stripped out before your report is ever saved.

Handing a funding company the details a bureau needs to find your file takes TRUST. This is what happens to those details after you do.

Your most sensitive identifier gets stripped out before your report is ever saved. Only the last four stay, and those are MASKED.
