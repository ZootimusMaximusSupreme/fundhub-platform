An item that holds up against one letter gets a TOUGHER letter in the next round. A dispute runs that way for up to six rounds.





Round one is the Metro 2 dispute, and the bureau has to recheck the item. The second round asks how it checked, so the bureau has to show its work.





Round three is a final notice. It tells the bureau to delete what it can't verify, and it goes out when the second round didn't settle the item.





Round four is a complaint to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, a federal regulator. Round five goes to your state attorney general. You sign those two YOURSELF.





Round six sends the final notice one more time, after the two complaints. It's the same final notice from round three, sent again.





That's six chances to get an item looked at again, from the bureau itself up to a federal regulator and your state.
