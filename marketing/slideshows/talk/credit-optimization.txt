A dispute runs up to six rounds, and each round after the first brings in a STRONGER law than the one before it.





Round one is the Metro 2 dispute, and the bureau has to recheck the item. The second round asks for the method of verification, so the bureau has to show how it checked.





The third round is a final notice that says delete what you can't verify. Three rounds in, and each letter cites more law than the one before it.





The fourth and fifth rounds go to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, a federal regulator, and to your state attorney general. Those two carry YOUR signature.





The sixth round sends the final notice again, after the complaints. Each step up puts a higher authority on your side of the dispute.
