Those 13 points sort into SIX groups. The first three all sit on your personal credit file, and that's the file that goes first.





Group one is your card balances. Every card gets its own paydown target, and that target comes from the card's own limit on your report.





Group two is the negative items on your report. The Blueprint writes a dispute letter for each one, and you print and mail them yourself.





Group three is what your file is MISSING. The engine wants three or more accounts in good standing, so your plan shows you what to add.





Groups four and five are the money side. You open accounts at the banks on your match list, and you keep deposits there before you apply.





Group six is your business on paper: its name, its industry code and its age. Each group sets up the one after it, so the order matters.

Working out of order COSTS you, and the business side shows it best. Every business you own gets five checks, and two of them sit on your personal file.





Check one asks if your personal file is fundable. Check two needs a seasoned, high-limit card on your own report, open for 24 months or longer.





That's the rule I mentioned. Your personal file gets ready FIRST. Filing the business early still helps, because it keeps aging while it waits.





The personal side works the same way. Open a new card in the middle of the work, and that card adds a hard INQUIRY to your report.





The engine wants hard inquiries at zero. So one new card puts that check right back on your list, and you're redoing work you already did.





Work done out of order sits waiting or gets done twice. The whole time, the capital you want for your business stays on hold.
