Applying On Your Own

1. AY-01 / The inquiry — Our file review tracks hard inquiries before another application goes out.

Our system checks every personal file for hard inquiries, and the target is ZERO. That means the first application you send on your own already counts against you.

Your own report shows how many are counting against you right now, and what they're holding back. This one is a sample report, so you can see how it reads.

There's one on TransUnion, one on Experian and one on Equifax. Two came from business card applications, and one came from asking for a bigger limit.

That last one stands out. In this sample, even asking for a higher limit on a card the person already had left a HARD inquiry behind.

Every one of them stays after the bank answers. Whichever bank you try next sees all of them, and it approves LESS because they're there.

Every extra pull gives the next bank another recent application to weigh. That's the cost of applying without checking the order first.

2. AY-02 / The checks — A file can pass the three checks that make it fundable and still fail on inquiries.

Our system runs SIX checks on every personal file, and each one comes back as a plain pass or fail, with nothing in between.

It looks at your score and how much of your limits you use. It also looks at negative items, your oldest card, and your accounts in good standing.

The first three checks decide if our system calls your file fundable. A file can pass all three and still fail on inquiries, so a good score never means they can WAIT.

I set that rule inside the system myself. Inquiries cost fundability, and every one above zero gets reviewed before we plan the next application.

Between rounds, every new inquiry on your file gets disputed. That way the next bank looks at a report we've already started cleaning up.

3. AY-03 / One bureau — Three applications on your own can all land on the one bureau the next bank reads.

Every bank pulls its own bureau, and on your own you don't know which one it reads. Three banks that all read Experian put three fresh pulls on ONE report.

The next bank that reads Experian finds all three close together and weighs them with the rest of the file.

Which bureau each bank reads decides where every pull lands, and on your own, that part happens blind.

On a plan, the pulls get SPREAD across Experian, Equifax and TransUnion, and every bank on the list is one that fits your file.

When a bureau already shows a fresh pull, the bank that reads it waits until that bureau is clear, and the newest inquiries get disputed before the next round.

Both sides start with the same file and the same score, and on a plan, no bureau collects a stack of fresh pulls at once.

4. AY-04 / After the round — On a plan, your file gets reviewed before the next round.

Every no you collect on your own adds a pull that the next bank reads, and the next approval comes back SMALLER because of it.

On a plan, each round ends with your file in shape for the next one. The banks that read a fresh pull wait, and the new pulls get disputed.

That gives us a current file to review before the next round of the funding sequence.

You can apply yourself. Our job is to read the file, track each bank's criteria, choose the order, prepare the applications you authorize and handle the follow-up. That's what the funding fee buys you.

We put a lot on the line with every application we send, so each one only goes where your file fits.

5. AY-05 / The soft pull — Only you can see the pull we use to read your file.

Does reading your file first cost you an inquiry too? Here's the form you sign before anyone looks, and line one answers it.

It says this is a soft pull. It doesn't lower your score, and no lender counts it as an application for credit.

On your report, a soft pull is visible to YOU and nobody else. The next bank that looks at your file won't find it there.

A hard pull needs your separate consent, written into the agreement. It only happens when an application you approved actually goes out.

That way you find out what's holding your file back, and what gets fixed first, without adding a single inquiry to the count.

That's the order the whole plan runs on. Your file gets read with no hard pull, and only then does anything go to a bank.

6. AY-06 / Your inquiries — Every inquiry on your report shows up on the call, with what each one holds back.

The inquiries counting against you show up on the call, every one of them, on your own report, next to what each one is holding back.

The soft pull runs live during the call. Your score doesn't change, and no bank ever sees it, since nothing has been applied for.

With the screen shared, your advisor takes you through it. You see how much you qualify for based on where you're at right now, and what keeps the rest out of reach.

Then you choose how it runs. Work the steps yourself in the order your file calls for, or let the Fundhub team send every application and every follow-up.

Either way, nothing goes to a bank until your file has been read, every inquiry has a plan, and the order is set.

Your file gets read first, and every application after that goes out on a plan built from it, whichever way you choose to run it.
