Pre-Underwriting

1. PU-01 / The red flag — A good score can still hide a card balance a bank reads before it decides.

When a bank turns down a good score, the reason is printed right under it on your report, and the bank only tells you after the no.

Your own report already prints which line that is for you. It's sitting on your file right now, before a single application goes anywhere.

This is a sample TransUnion report on FICO Score 9. The score reads 706, which is GOOD credit, and on paper this file gets a yes.

Right under the score, the bureau prints the reasons holding it down, in its own words, and the top one says the card balances run too high.

That's the RED FLAG. The bank weighs that reason line, and a red flag like this one is what gets good credit denied.

And that same line shows up in one more place: the letter the bank mails you after it says no.

2. PU-02 / The reasons — The risk factors under your score can show up in a bank's denial letter.

When a bank turns you down, its letter lists its reasons. Your report can show related risk factors before you apply.

Put the two side by side. Your report shows risk factors a bank can also name in a denial letter.

That lets us see a risk before you apply and compare it with any denial letter you already have.

And the top reason on this sample has a SIMPLE fix, one that changes the balance the bank reads on that exact line.

Reading it first means that reason gets worked on before any bank ever sees your application.

3. PU-03 / The inquiries — A hard-pull application leaves an inquiry even when the bank says no.

A no costs you more than one application. That money was going to pay for your next hire, a bigger job or more ads.

A funding application can set off a HARD pull. Even when the bank says no, a hard inquiry stays on the report and later banks can see it.

This sample file already shows three. One on Experian, one on TransUnion, one on Equifax, from two card applications and one ask for a higher limit.

The next bank reads every one of them before it decides. Stack enough and the banks start saying no, even to a good score.

That's why the order flips. Your file gets read and fixed FIRST, and only then does an application go out.

4. PU-04 / Pre-underwriting — Pre-underwriting means we review your file the way a lender does before anything goes out.

We review your file the way a lender does, BEFORE a single application leaves our hands, and that's what pre-underwriting means.

Our system, UnderwriteIQ, reads your file with the same kind of models the banks use. It checks your card balances, your inquiries, your main card and any negative accounts.

When it finds a red flag, that gets handled FIRST. On this sample, the Visa sits at about 40% used, so it gets paid down to $800 before the statement closes.

That's a SIMPLE tweak. It changes the balance the bureau reports, and that's the balance the bank reads on that line.

The balance the bank reads on your first application is the paid-down one, and that's the whole point of doing it FIRST.

Next comes which banks see your file, and in what order, and that part gets planned from the same report.
