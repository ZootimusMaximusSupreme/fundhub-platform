AY-02 / The checks

One inquiry is enough to fail one of the six checks our system runs on your file.

  • The other five look at your score, how much of your limits you use, negative items, your oldest card and your accounts in good standing.
  • A file can pass the first three, which decide if it's fundable, and still fail on inquiries, so a good score never means they can wait.
  • Here's what's really going on underneath. Our system runs SIX checks on every personal file, and each one comes back as a plain pass or fail.
  • It looks at your score and how much of your limits you use. It also looks at negative items, your oldest card, and your accounts in good standing.
  • The fifth check is hard inquiries, and its target is ZERO. One inquiry fails it just the same as three, so your first application already costs you that check.
  • The first three checks decide if our system calls your file fundable. A file can pass all three and still fail on inquiries, so a good score never means they can WAIT.
  • I set that rule inside the system myself. Inquiries cost fundability, and every one above zero gets flagged to come off your file.
  • Between rounds, every new inquiry on your file gets disputed. That way the next bank looks at a report we've already started cleaning up.

AY-03 / Two ways

Three applications on your own put three fresh pulls in front of the fourth bank.

  • On a plan, applications go only to banks that fit your file, with the pulls spread over all three bureaus.
  • If one bureau already shows a fresh inquiry, the banks that read that bureau wait their turn, and the newest inquiries get disputed before the next round leaves.
  • Here's what applying on your own costs you. The first bank pulls your file, and then the second bank pulls it again with the first pull sitting right there.
  • By the third bank, your report shows a run of fresh pulls close together. The next lender sees that run and approves LESS than it would on a clean report.
  • Now look at the right side. On a plan, applications only go to banks that fit your file, and the pulls are SPREAD across Experian, Equifax and TransUnion.
  • If one bureau already shows a fresh inquiry, the banks that read that bureau wait their turn. Then the newest inquiries get disputed before the next round leaves.
  • Both sides start with the same file and the same score. On the right, every application went where it did for a REASON, and that protects your total.

  • The first line on the form you sign before anyone looks calls it a soft pull, and your score doesn't move.
  • A hard pull needs your separate consent, and it only happens when an application you approved goes out.
  • Does reading your file first cost you an inquiry too? Here's the form you sign before anyone looks, and line one answers it.
