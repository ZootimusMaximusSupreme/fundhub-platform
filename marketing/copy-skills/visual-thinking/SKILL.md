# Visual Thinking

Make ideas and relationships available to examine, change, explain, and discuss. Use both Dan Roam books as attributed foundations. The routing, evidence checks, tool selection, and specialist handoffs below are our implementation. Work can serve marketing, sales, teaching, strategy, operations, or personal understanding; keep the assignment's sources and destination in their proper scope.

## Route from the intended outcome

Infer the job from ordinary language and context; do not require a mode selection or intake form. Choose a primary route and combine others only when they help the requested outcome. An unclear idea may need exploration before a persuasive explanation. A small edit does not need a workshop.

| User intent | Work to perform | Relevant guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand or organize messy material | Extract entities and relationships; map what is known; explain consequential gaps | Methods |
| Solve a problem or choose an approach | Map the current situation; investigate competing explanations; compare feasible alternatives and trade-offs | Methods, then the decision section of Applications |
| Develop or discover an idea | Explore alternative views with SQVID or pattern changes; select useful directions | Methods |
| Explain or teach | Match audience knowledge; choose and progressively reveal the representation; support application or teach-back | Teaching section of Applications |
| Sell or advocate an idea | Clarify the audience's decision; draw the supported value, mechanism, comparison, or proposed change; invite genuine correction | Sales section of Applications |
| Facilitate, research, or listen visually | Externalize participants' accounts; distinguish agreement from disagreement; let them correct the model | Collaboration section of Applications |
| Interpret, critique, or revise an existing visual | Inspect the actual visual; recover its model; examine meaning, evidence, and readability; revise only if requested | Review section of Delivery |

For diagram/framework selection consult Methods; for actual asset production, cross-format reuse, revisions, or specialist work consult Delivery and Collaboration. For book attribution, competing source rules, or source limits consult Sources and conflicts. Load relevant sections progressively; normal tasks do not require full-book retrieval.

## Select capabilities without turning them into a checklist

Briefly consider these signals internally before producing the result. Use the capabilities that address an actual need:

- Messy inputs: extract a tentative model from the supplied speech, notes, documents, screenshots, or diagrams. Inspect visual inputs with available image/PDF tools; do not guess unreadable content.
- Unclear idea or premature conclusion: explore alternatives through SQVID, reversal, regrouping, or recombination.
- Audience needs orientation: adapt detail and progressively reveal the explanation.
- People disagree or evidence is missing: listen visually, expose alternative accounts, or propose the smallest useful research question. Do not invent participant feedback.
- Meaningful choices: make criteria, dependencies, trade-offs, and uncertainty inspectable.
- Important claims or quantities: retain provenance and distinguish observations, calculations, hypotheses, proposals, and fictional illustrations.
- Related existing assets or revised facts: preserve established meanings and update affected views consistently within the authorized scope.
- Requested writing or campaign planning: use the relevant available specialist as described in Delivery and Collaboration.

Capability coverage means recognizing relevance, not invoking every feature. Do not add persuasion, a CTA, six diagrams, or multiple options to a task that does not need them. A sentence or table can be the best answer; honor an explicit diagram request where a truthful useful diagram is possible.

## Produce and check

Establish the intended takeaway or decision, actual facts, audience, and medium from context. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the output; complete useful independent work meanwhile. Keep consequential assumptions visible without burdening every label with a disclaimer.

Choose a representation whose relationships answer the question. Deliver the requested diagram, explanation, critique, storyboard, or usable asset rather than only describing how one could be made. Sketches and polished visuals are both valid. If a required production tool is unavailable, provide the usable source/brief possible and state the limitation.

Check meaning as well as appearance: labels and connectors, scales and units, implied causality, missing branches, evidence, audience fit, and readability at the intended size. For a substantial persuasive argument or consequential decision, examine a plausible competing explanation or alternative. Preserve inconvenient facts; a coherent picture is not proof of the depicted mechanism or outcome.

Stop when the requested outcome is satisfied and material defects are repaired. Distinguish our inspection from actual audience comprehension or business-performance evidence. Drafts and research do not authorize publication, sending, live-system changes, or edits to other skills. No other skill depends on this one.
