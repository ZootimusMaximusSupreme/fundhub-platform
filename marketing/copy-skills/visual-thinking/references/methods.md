# Methods: investigation, imagination, and representation

These are selective adaptations of Dan Roam, not an exhaustive replacement for either book. Source locators and conflicts are in sources-and-conflicts.md.

## Look, see, imagine, show

- **Look:** gather relevant material and establish orientation. Lay evidence out together when comparison helps; identify boundaries, coordinates, sources, dates, and omissions. Data scarcity calls for useful questions, not invented inputs.
- **See:** select and group meaningful patterns. Separate people, quantities, locations, timing, interactions, and explanations. A surprising pattern is a candidate insight, not automatically a cause.
- **Imagine:** rearrange, compare, reverse, or extend what is known. Explore how changing a relationship changes the interpretation. Label possibilities rather than silently turning them into observations.
- **Show:** choose a framework, establish coordinates, add the information, and explain it. Others' questions may send the work back to looking or imagining.

These functions form an iterative cycle, not four mandatory approvals. Three resources—eyes, mind's eye, hand-eye coordination—are the book's accessible organizing model. An AI implementation can externalize these activities through inspected inputs and editable representations without claiming human perception or neurological effects.

Begin with a named object when facing a blank page. Add another relevant object and a meaningful relationship. A line should have a clear meaning: order, dependency, influence, transfer, membership, or a causal claim. Simple shapes are sufficient; they are not required final aesthetics.

## SQVID: alternative views and audience fit

| Contrast | Useful question |
| --- | --- |
| Simple / Elaborate | Does the audience need an overview, supporting detail, or both in an appropriate order? |
| Qualitative / Quantitative | Do characteristics, measurements, or a combination explain the issue? |
| Vision / Execution | Are we showing the destination, the work to reach it, or their connection? |
| Individual / Comparison | Does the idea need its own identity or a meaningful reference point? |
| Delta (change) / Status quo | Are we describing what exists, a proposed change, or the difference? |

For deliberate exploration, sketch both ends of the relevant contrasts. The full book exercise generates ten views; ordinary tasks need only useful contrasts. For presentation, choose a combination appropriate to this audience and purpose. Do not expose every exploration as a final asset. Simple is contrasted with elaborate, not with complex: important complexity can remain in an understandable picture. Expert and novice audiences may need the same overview and detail in different orders.

## Six question families

| Question | Candidate representation | Construction judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who / what? | Portrait, labeled entity, participant map | Identify distinguishing traits and roles. Buyer, user, approver, and influencer can differ. |
| How many / how much? | Chart | Define measure, units, denominator, period, and comparison. Calculate meaningful quantities; choose geometry that represents them accurately. |
| Where? | Map | Define spatial, organizational, or conceptual relationships. Formal hierarchy does not establish informal influence. |
| When? | Timeline | Distinguish order, duration, dependencies, milestones, and acceptance evidence. Equal spacing must not imply equal duration unless intended. |
| How? | Flowchart/process model | Name actions, responses, branches, and conditions. Cover material outcomes, including unknown/exception states when needed. Temporal sequence alone does not prove causation. |
| Why? | Multivariable plot (Napkin) or visual equation/synthesis (Draw to Win) | Connect relevant relationships to an interpretation; keep assumptions and alternative explanations inspectable. A conceptual equation is not arithmetic or causal proof. |

Choose by the actual question, not by a fixed visual funnel. Combine families where useful: quantity plus time, organizational structure plus influence, or process plus costs. The Visual Thinking Codex combines six framework rows with five SQVID contrast columns; it is a selection aid, not 36 mandatory assets. Some combinations add no value.

For numbers, distinguish counts, totals, per-person values, percentages, and denominators. Verify data-derived geometry and labels together. A concept sketch can be deliberately nonnumeric; do not fabricate data to fill a quantitative chart. A multivariable plot can reveal association while leaving the causal mechanism unproven.

## Generate alternatives deliberately

Draw the current arrangement first. Try a relevant reversal, opposite, regrouping, separation/recombination, removal, or constraint. Explain what changes in the underlying idea—not just how the picture rotates. Keep promising alternatives and evaluate their assumptions, feasibility, user value, and trade-offs when the task calls for a recommendation.

Visual analogies and memorable symbols can compress a learned idea. State the important correspondence and where the analogy stops if that boundary matters. Do not let an attractive metaphor conceal actual dependencies or imply a result. A new symbol needs explanation before it can serve as shared shorthand.
