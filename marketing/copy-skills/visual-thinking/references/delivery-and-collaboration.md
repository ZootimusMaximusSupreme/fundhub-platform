# Delivery, interpretation, revision, and specialist collaboration

## Make the requested result usable

Choose the simplest suitable output and honor the requested medium. A thinking sketch, diagram, presentation storyboard, reusable visual brief, polished asset, or a concise table can all be appropriate. A production request needs a usable result, not only advice about making one.

Use available rendering capabilities and their own instructions: Mermaid for a small static relationship/process diagram; editable SVG or appropriate diagram tools for precise vector work; plotting tools for quantitative figures; the visualization skill for useful interactive explanations; presentations for decks; image generation for requested raster illustration; design tools for a requested design destination. Do not assume every tool is available or invent a successful render. Visual Thinking supplies the meaning and visual plan; rendering tools execute the selected format. Do not force image generation for precise charts, or require a particular renderer to reason visually.

For reusable work, preserve a compact model beside the output when useful: entities/roles, relationship meanings, important facts and sources, assumptions, audience, and stable terms/symbols. This need not be a separate file for a small chat diagram. Save only to the user's authorized destination. Do not put client material into this general skill.

Inspect a generated visual at its intended display size. Check labels, arrow directions, overlap/clipping, units/scales, legend, and whether a reader can follow the intended relationship. If rendering is unavailable, validate the source as far as possible and state that visual appearance is unverified. A short text equivalent and non-color cues preserve essential meaning where images or color are unavailable. Do not claim accessibility certification from these checks.

## Interpret and critique

Inspect the actual artifact. Explain visible entities, measures, relationships, and apparent takeaway before interpreting intent. Distinguish labels you can read from ambiguous ones. Extract reusable structure without treating unsupported claims as facts about a new business.

For critique, locate the specific weakness: unclear audience/point, misleading relationship, unsupported inference, missing branch, inappropriate comparison, illegible layout, or mismatch between picture and narration. Separate factual correction from stylistic preference. Preserve effective material. A critique-only request does not authorize rewriting the asset.

For consequential visual arguments, compare the representation with its source: matched dates and populations, denominators, baseline, area versus radius scaling, equal axes where comparisons imply equality, current versus forecast, and correlation versus causality. Apply only relevant checks. Check a plausible competing interpretation before declaring the diagram proves the desired conclusion.

## Revise and reuse consistently

When a fact, term, or relationship changes, find the affected labels, narration, examples, totals, geometry, and conclusions. Update authorized assets together; flag affected assets outside the permitted edit scope rather than changing them. Preserve unrelated content and locked wording. A new number can change the takeaway, not merely a label.

Across a campaign or learning series, retain stable meanings for entities, symbols, and terminology. Adapt detail and layout for each medium; consistency does not require identical visuals. Do not invent a brand system for one diagram. For marketing or a client's branded artifact, reuse supplied or authorized brand sources when relevant.

## Work with existing skills without creating dependencies

Visual Thinking can reason, diagram, label, narrate, teach, and advocate independently. Other skills remain independently usable too. Do not edit their instructions or voice profiles as part of ordinary collaboration, and do not create a new requirement for them to call Visual Thinking.

- **Copybot:** use for requested persuasive copy accompanying a visual, unless the user requests otherwise. Check availability in the catalog or through the installed skill catalog before reading. Load its actual instructions and relevant references; when writing the owner's copy, preserve its automatic business/personal voice-loading route. Do not substitute chat banter, a generic "brother" tone, or this skill's voice guess. Labels and explanatory narration alone do not require a copywriting pass. Copybot-only requests proceed without visual planning. If Copybot is unavailable, complete the requested work with available guidance; retrieve the actual voice source if accessible and disclose a material voice-source gap rather than claiming a match.
- **Sequence Maker:** use for requested campaign sequencing or detailed persuasive progression. A visual explanation's progressive reveal is native here and does not automatically require it. Carry the intended message, facts, audience, necessary context, and visual role into the plan; the six diagram families do not dictate six emails or six emotional stages.
- **Positioning:** use when the assignment actually calls for strategic positioning. A map can depict an established position without reopening strategy. Do not invent competitor traits or claim customer perceptions from the layout alone.
- **Story Writing:** use for requested narrative craft or developed scenes. Storyboards can explain a process without creating a dramatic story. Do not force McKee or Campbell into ordinary diagrams.
- **Marketing Evaluation:** use for requested KPI diagnosis, attribution, or comparison planning. Internal visual QA is not a compulsory marketing audit.
- **Channel workflows:** retain sending, publishing, scheduling, and live-state decisions. This skill adds no execution authority.

Check collaborator availability before loading it. If an optional skill is absent, continue the useful authorized work without installing anything or creating a dependency error. Pass only the relevant facts, sources, intended outcome, visual relationships, and user constraints. Distinguish required facts from suggested execution; the specialist may improve the suggestion. Integrate once and repair real issues instead of bouncing indefinitely between skills.
