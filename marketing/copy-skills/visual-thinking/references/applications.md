# Applications: sales, teaching, collaboration, and decisions

Choose relevant sections. These are routes through shared methods, not separate compulsory workflows.

## Sell or advocate an idea

Establish whom the explanation serves, what decision is being considered, the actual offer or proposal, relevant alternatives, supported value, and consequential uncertainty. Preserve established offer terms and positioning. A visual can explain or compare an offer without redesigning it.

Draw the present situation and a relevant possible improvement when that clarifies the decision. Keep three kinds of "after" distinct: an observed outcome, a proposed future, and a fictional illustration. The graphic itself must not imply guaranteed approval, revenue, speed, savings, or transformation when the evidence does not support it.

Choose the useful sales form: a mechanism explanation, process, before/after model, alternative comparison, objection clarification, or an interactive conversation sketch. A skeptical buyer may need detail and evidence before accepting a simple summary. Avoid treating skepticism as merely a persuasion obstacle; the objection may identify a real limitation.

For a live discussion, prepare enough to explain accurately and leave meaningful room for the other person to correct the model. Roam's 75/25 preparation idea is a heuristic, not a quota. Invite substantive changes, including ones that weaken the proposal. A prospect's participation or agreement with a diagram is not consent to buy. Without actual participants, provide a draft facilitation script or proposed questions; do not simulate their responses as customer research.

For a substantial pitch, orient the audience, reveal the important pattern, explore implications/options, and state the supported recommendation. Prepare supporting detail for challenges. Rehearsal helps explanation; it should not turn participation into a predetermined performance.

## Explain and teach

Determine what the learner needs to understand or do and their available context. Preserve necessary distinctions while reducing needless decoding. Select a conceptual overview, demonstration, worked example, progressive reveal, or practice activity to suit the outcome.

A candidate progression is people/objects, quantities, relationships, timing, process, and significance. Reorder or omit parts when learning requires it; both books allow adaptation. A storyboard may show the growing diagram plus the narration and takeaway at each stage. Use Sequence Maker only if detailed persuasive progression is part of the request; ordinary instructional ordering is native here.

When teaching a skill, move beyond fluent explanation: demonstrate, invite an attempt, ask the learner to complete or explain the model, and identify the consequential misunderstanding. Design this as an exercise if no learner is present. Do not claim understanding was tested merely because we inspected the output.

For leadership or onboarding, distinguish destination from route. A purpose symbol can help memory; a process map clarifies responsibilities and next actions. Neither substitutes for the other where both matter. Do not force a hero, villain, quest, or inspirational ending into a neutral explanation.

## Collaborate, research, and listen visually

Extract participants' terms and relationships faithfully. Distinguish what someone actually said from inferred meaning. A tentative model can be offered for correction: who is missing, what happens first, where does this branch go, whose decision is it? Preserve unresolved disagreement rather than drawing a false consensus.

Make room for people who prefer annotating, talking, or correcting to initiating a drawing. Roam's pen-color categories are participation metaphors, not diagnoses. An AI can consolidate supplied contributions; it must not invent a workshop, interview, agreement, or approval.

For customer research, map the current behavior, people, workarounds, tools, and friction before imposing the offer's preferred journey. Keep reported experiences separate from observed behavior and hypotheses. Use the drawing to identify a useful next question. A small research sample does not automatically establish market-wide demand.

## Solve and decide

Identify the decision, constraints, and current situation. Map relevant relationships and expose the assumptions behind a candidate diagnosis. Compare alternatives against the criteria that actually matter, including retaining the current approach where credible. Avoid decorative scoring systems or made-up weights.

When a missing fact could reverse the choice, show the uncertainty and the smallest useful way to resolve it. It can be appropriate to recommend a provisional direction, a reversible trial, or no change. Proposal diagrams do not authorize implementation.

For marketing performance, a chart can reveal a denominator error, missing comparison, or a possible explanation. Use Marketing Evaluation when statistical interpretation, attribution, or experiment planning is requested. A clearer diagram does not establish a conversion lift, and an association does not by itself prove why a campaign changed.
