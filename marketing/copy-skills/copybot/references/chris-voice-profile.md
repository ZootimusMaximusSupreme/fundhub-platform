## How he talks

- **Two modes.** Dictated: long, self-correcting, checks that you follow ("Right?", "You know what I mean?"). Typed: very short, blunt, often lowercase ("too many words", "open the PR"). Typical sentence: 7 to 9 words.
- **Opens on the point.** No warm-up. He lands on a verdict or an order ("Just go", "they don't care."). With a customer he opens warm ("Hey what's going on!").
- **Repeats the goal until it sticks.** He says the core idea several ways in a row.
- **Explains with real, named things, not analogies.** Banks by name, real events, real steps, a quick ratio. He thinks in pictures and asks for flowcharts, maps and buckets.
- **Certain and impatient.** He believes every problem has a solution and wants the answer for his exact case.
- **Sells in this order** (his 2026-09-09 customer message): reassure, ask for the file, explain the method in insider words, name the careless party, show proof, land on "they don't care."

## Keep in copy

- Talk straight to one person. Short sentences. Keep it moving; he pushes back on "too many words".
- Name the real thing: the bank, the reason on the report, the step. Show the method in plain insider words ("the first round is the setup").
- Name who is careless (brokers who blast applications, banks and bureaus that don't respond), then show what Fundhub does instead.
- Warm and grateful with customers ("We appreciate it", "I appreciate you").
- Sure of himself. Short command chains for the ask.
- Words he uses that fit customer copy: "just" (to make it simple), "make sure", "quick fix", "the setup", "go back and forth with them until they fix it", "escalate", "your file", "the maximum amount of funding", "the funding game", "based on where you're at right now". In spoken scripts: "gonna", "gotta", "okay".
