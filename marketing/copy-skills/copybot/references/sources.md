## Decisions when advice differs

- Biggest-benefit headlines are one useful approach, not mandatory. Awareness, relevance, and skepticism govern the entry point.
- Great Leads' six families describe the lead's organizing approach. Cashvertising's twelve devices describe ways to begin wording it. They are different levels.
- Clear relationships and preserved meaning take priority over short-word or sentence-length targets. Rhythm can require longer sentences.
- Cleverness, metaphor, contrast, and implication are useful when they carry the idea. Cut them when they obscure it.
- "One" means coherent direction, not a ban on supporting claims, emotional movement, or blended lead types.
- Historical claims such as quotation marks increasing reading by 25% are attributed book claims, not reliable forecasts for a new piece.
- Explanation, analogy, and plausibility do not substitute for factual evidence.
