# Copy Booster

Use after or while the underlying message exists. Move copy from abstract information toward a clear internal experience without changing the supported claim.

## Internal representations

Use relevant sensory channels when they make the experience clearer:

- Visual: appearance, size, shape, color, movement, condition, surroundings, and before/after differences.
- Auditory: voices, noise, silence, rhythm, and mechanical sounds.
- Kinesthetic: physical feeling, emotion, comfort, relief, or effort.
- Olfactory: smell when it naturally contributes.
- Gustatory: taste when the product or experience naturally involves it.

The source abbreviates these as V-A-K-O-G. Choose only the channels that clarify the point; do not try to maximize their number. Never invent a sensory experience.

## Strengthening methods

- Replace or support vague claims with supplied exact prices, savings, quantities, dimensions, times, frequencies, warranties, rebates, or measurable outcomes.
- Use powerful visual adjectives only when they create a truthful, useful picture rather than decorative hype.
- Show the product or service in motion: sequence, interaction, transformation, and what the reader observes.
- Translate a feature into what it means in the reader's life.
- Demonstrate "easy," "fast," "clean," "effective," or similar abstractions instead of merely asserting them.
- Contrast the supported before and after.
- Use here-and-now authenticity only for a genuine current event or experience; never manufacture it.

## Apply selectively

Find vague assertions and replace them with supported detail, a concrete explanation, or a relevant demonstration. Use sensory description only where it makes the experience easier to understand. Do not invent detail to make a scene vivid, or assume a feature guarantees a life outcome.

Stop when the reader can picture or understand the point. More adjectives and more sensory channels are not inherently stronger. Source examples demonstrate techniques only; do not transfer their facts or experiences into a new assignment.
