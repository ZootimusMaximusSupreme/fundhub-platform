# Writing and revision

## Write a complete piece

Use the supplied topic, reader, channel, intended response, evidence, and constraints. Infer harmless format choices; do not turn writing into an intake questionnaire. An existing offer is context when relevant, not a prerequisite for every task.

1. Find the central thought worth communicating. State internally what the reader should understand, feel, or do differently after reading. Keep the promise within the available support.
2. Choose the entry point: what does this reader already understand, recognize, want, or doubt? Use the lead reference for substantial opening work. Avoid inventing a detailed audience profile.
3. Sketch the necessary movement of ideas. What must be understood before the next claim can make sense? Where will a reader ask "why?", "how?", "is that true?", or "what does that mean for me?" Answer relevant questions where they arise.
   For substantial pieces, a brief pre-outline can select the material, its order, and useful approaches before prose drafting. Choose only what fits the facts and reader; discard techniques that require missing events or evidence. The final opening can emerge after the body. An outline may change as drafting reveals a better explanation or transition.
4. Draft enough to develop the thought. Show the relevant action, relationship, or consequence directly; commentary about how concrete or useful the explanation is usually adds less than the explanation itself. Cut repetition that adds neither meaning nor intentional emphasis, including an ending that merely explains the point again.
5. Review support and sequence with the argument reference. Repair a missing logical step before trying to fix it with stronger adjectives.
6. Edit clarity and rhythm, then calibrate voice. Voice can inform the draft from the start; the final voice pass must not alter facts, promises, or logic.
7. Read the finished piece as a continuous message, not a checklist of satisfied instructions. Check the requested outcome and return the copy. Surface an assumption or factual gap only when it materially affects using or interpreting the draft.

## From material to composition

When customer language or a studied example is supplied, distinguish the jobs of those inputs. Customer material can supply experiences, concerns, desired outcomes, wording, and objections. A studied piece can suggest a relationship or question sequence. Voice samples guide expression. Keep those sources attached to the claims they actually support; do not transfer another author's personal story or product mechanism into this assignment.

Preserve exact quotations when presenting them as quotations. When composing from several answers, arrange and paraphrase without inventing events, chronology, motives, or broader agreement. Frequency in a small set of interviews suggests a pattern to investigate, not market-wide certainty. Ask for more input only when necessary to complete this assignment; a small edit does not require customer research.

Use a study extraction as a hypothesis about a useful approach. Decide which parts fit the target, adapt the relationship rather than just swapping nouns, and omit parts whose required facts are absent. If the adaptation feels forced, return to the reader's actual question. A new explanation must describe a supported process, not invent a product capability to fill a mechanism slot.

## Revise

Locate the largest relevant weakness first: confusing central idea, broken sequence, unsupported claim, weak opening, vague language, ambiguity, or mismatched voice. Work only as broadly as the request permits. For a full rewrite, repair structure before line editing; for a sentence edit, stay with the sentence.

Preserve successful language, intentional repetition, relevant detail, and the writer's position. Compare the revision against the original for changes in actor, action, timing, causality, certainty, conditions, and promised result. "May help" must not become "will"; "decided to call" must not become "called."

## Options

Produce the requested count. Vary meaningful choices—entry point, emphasis, rhythm, or structure—rather than swapping synonyms. Keep facts and locked positioning stable unless the user asks to explore different angles.

## Analysis

Point to specific wording, explain the reader problem, and recommend the smallest useful fix. Separate observed issues from hypotheses about performance. Do not claim an alternative will convert better without relevant testing. Do not rewrite unless requested.

## Organic posts

Develop an observation, useful lesson, argument, or story into a satisfying point. Let personality be more prominent when requested. Do not bolt a product pitch, urgency, or CTA onto a post that does not call for one. A story needs relevance and a payoff; it does not need to become a sales letter.
