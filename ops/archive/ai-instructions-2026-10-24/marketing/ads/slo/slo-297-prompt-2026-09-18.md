### How to use this package (ships in the pack)

Heading: HOW TO USE THIS PACKAGE

- Mail certified with return receipt. Keep the receipt — it starts the 30-day clock.
- One envelope per bureau. Never combine.
- Keep every response. Photograph it the day it arrives.
- Do not use a PO box return address. Use the home address on your ID.
- Sign by hand, in ink.
- Wait the full 30 days plus mail time before Round 2.
- Do not file the CFPB or state AG complaints until Round 3 failed. Sign the declaration on those pages — the onboarding box is not that signature.
- Metro 2 disputes correct inaccurate reporting. Accurate derogatories may be corrected rather than deleted.



### Decision tree (ships in the pack)

Heading: DECISION TREE

- All items deleted → stop. Do not send Round 2 or 3.
- Some deleted / some verified → send Round 2 only for the verified items (cross out the rest).
- All verified → send Round 2 MOV demand.
- No response after 30 days + mail time → Round 2 with late-response argument; keep your certified-mail receipt.
- Round 3 still on the report → file the CFPB complaint, then the state AG complaint. Sign the declaration on each. Do not file them with Round 1.

### What is in one bureau letter

Header: date (Round 1 dated; Round 2 and 3 undated until they write it) · consumer name · address · last four of SSN if present · bureau name · Re: Round n Metro 2 dispute or FCRA dispute — rule ids.

Body parts: opening · lead · demand · ask · next · one paragraph per claim.

Each claim paragraph:

- heading Violation or Request, plus rule id, plus the account name
- field line
- Severity
- account line
- reason
- Observed / Expected
- Legal basis when present

Then: CITATIONS · CLOSING · handwritten sign-off.

If every claim on the letter confirms the file, the subject says confirmation, not dispute. No method-of-verification demand. No deletion of the listed items.

### Round 2 — this is the escalation that does not re-ask

It does not re-ask the bureau to look. It demands they show the work.

Hooks: FCRA § 611(a)(7) · FCRA § 611(a)(6)(B)(iii) · 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b)

Demand, as written: describe the method of verification for each item — who you contacted, what records they sent, and what you compared. Give each furnisher's name, address, and telephone number.

Ask: if you cannot produce that method, delete the items.

R2+ can print PRIOR BUREAU RESPONSE (evidence): date, outcome, account ending last4, excerpt.

### Round 3

Hooks: FCRA § 611(a)(5)(A) · 15 U.S.C. § 1681n · 15 U.S.C. § 1681o
Demand: delete each item you cannot verify. Deletion within 15 days. Written confirmation of every deletion.

### Furnisher validation letter (direct to the collector)

Cover: send this if still inside the 30-day validation window from the collector's first contact.

Via certified mail, return receipt requested.

Demands in writing:

1. The original signed agreement that created this alleged debt.
2. An itemized balance from a defined itemization date, showing interest, fees, payments, and credits.
3. The complete chain of title and all assignments from the original creditor to you.
4. Proof that you own this alleged debt or are authorized to collect it.
5. Statute of limitations in their state, including the date the collector says it began.

No FundHub brand on this letter.

# THE POSITIONING — the whole argument. Do not drift off it.

The enemy is the learning curve. Not a competitor. Not a broker. The six months.

People don't want to learn funding. They want funding. Fast, and the most of it they can get. Right now there is no clear road to it, so they buy a course, and the course teaches. Modules, homework, community, trust the process. Six months of trial and error later, they've got skills they didn't ask for and still no money. The skills are real. They're just not the thing they were buying.

The mechanism: download the brain of someone who's done this hundreds of times. Go from A to Z without the middle. We're shortcutting the learning curve to zero, because they don't need to know anything. They just need to follow the instructions.

Chris's own framing. Use it or something like it in at least one script: Frodo doesn't need the whole journey. Fly straight to the mountain, drop the ring in the lava, done.

That last one is the whole product in one line: now, next, how long, how fast.

Why they buy instead of learning it: they have a business to run. They're busy. That's not a weakness to poke at. It's the reason the package exists. Most people don't have time to sit there and learn this. With the deliverables they don't have to.

They've been declined, gotten the runaround, or simply don't know where they stand and nobody will tell them straight. Some of them have already bought a course and are months in with nothing to show. Default credit gate is 600+.

The one thing they all have in common: they do not want to become an expert in this. They want the money and they want to go back to work.

The one that trips writers up. "This will not affect you at all" is banned as a blanket promise about the whole thing. It is **not** banned to say the soft pull does not touch the score. That is true. Keep the promise attached to the soft pull:

Right: "No hard inquiry. Soft pull only. Zero impact on your score."

Wrong: "Working with us will not affect your credit at all."

## Avoid these — the market has poisoned them

- "lenders compete for you"
- "get matched with 75 lenders" as a headline
- "fast and easy"
- "cash advance" used as a good thing
- "unlimited offers"
- "apply now to get calls from our partners"
- anything that hints several companies will phone them
- "secret sauce"
- "guaranteed approval"

Speed is demoted, not dropped. "Funded in 24 hours" is table stakes now, not a difference. Lead with trust. Earn the speed claim second. When you use it, use the real number: about **7 days against an industry 30–45**. Not an adjective.

**Words that do work:** "no spam calls" · "we don't sell your number" · "soft pull" · "won't touch your credit score" · "see your real offers" · "one honest application" · "no equity" · "no daily payments" · "know the real cost" · "judged on your business, not just your FICO" · "owners the banks ignore" · "bridge the gap" · "before anyone pulls your credit".

## Two more

- **Never name the tech stack.** No vendor names, ever. It is "our system", or the name we gave it.
- **Never lead with white-label.** If it shows up at all, it is a door mentioned at the end. Not the lead.



## What kills a concept

Run these before a script ships.

1. It fails the mechanism test (below).
2. It is a duplicate of another script in **argument**, not just in wording.
3. It sounds like an AI wrote it. Read it out loud. If Chris would not say it, rewrite it.
4. It uses a banned word or a poisoned phrase from the lists above.
5. It names the tech stack.
6. It asks for something in the hook. The hook names the problem with the other path. The CTA asks.



# PART 2 — HOW LONG, AND HOW THE HOOK WORKS



## Word count

Speaking rate: 150 words per minute.

The SOP floor is 60 seconds. A short hook is fine. A short ad is not.

For this job, owner-set 2026-09-18: **60 to 80 seconds. 150 to 200 words.** Hard max 80 seconds. Count the words. Print the count.

## The cause-first hook test

The first thing out of Chris's mouth names the **reason** their problem happened. A person, a company, or a broken process that did it to them. Not the symptom. Not the offer. Not his credentials. Not a question. The cause.

The hook is the first **two sentences**. All four must pass.

1. **A cause is named by the end of sentence two.** Somebody or something did this, or failed to do it. "the guy who got you funded", "nobody cleaned the file", "your broker", "the last company", "applications going in on a file nobody cleaned first". A passive sentence with no actor does not count. "owners never get shown" is not enough. Who is not showing them?
2. **No ask.** The hook contains no click, book, call, apply, tap, watch, stop, sign up, comment or DM. The hook names the problem with the other path. The CTA asks.
3. **No question mark in sentence one.** A question puts the point off. Make the statement instead.
4. **The subject of sentence one is not us.** Not the price, not the product, not the founder's record, not the brand. Those all belong in the body. They belong there quickly. Just not first.



### Three that pass

1. "If your business got denied for funding, you didn't lose because of your credit. You lost because nobody looked at your file the way a bank actually looks at it."
  Why it passes: rules out the cause they blame themselves for, then names the real one, inside two sentences.
2. "The guy who got you funded left a pile of hard inquiries on your credit. Ask him when he's taking them off. He's not, because he has no way to."
  Why it passes: a named actor doing a named thing, in sentence one.
3. "Round one funded. Round two came back no. That wasn't your credit slipping — that was the inquiries round one just created, still sitting on your file when the next batch went out."
  Why it passes: effect first, then the cause arrives and corrects the one they assumed.



### One allowed exception

Disqualification openers. Telling someone this is not for them is a real hook device. A hook that opens on the gate — "If you're under a 600, this one isn't for you" — passes check 4, as long as a real cause lands by the end of sentence two.

# PART 3 — THE ANGLE, THE SCRIPT, THE CLOSE



## The angle formula

An angle is not a creative act. It is a combination.

> ANGLE = one ENEMY × one MECHANISM × one AUDIENCE
>
> HOOK = [Experience or Behavior] + [Emotion] + [Desire Frustration]
>
> BODY = [Validate the experience] → [Reveal the mechanism] → [Connect to the desire] → [Proof]

One enemy. One mechanism. One audience. An ad that argues two things argues nothing.

**Enemy** = what the industry, the course, or the six months did to this person.
**Mechanism** = the specific way we do it. Download the brain. A to Z without the middle. The finished file instead of the class.
**Audience** = who is listening, plus the credit gate.

### How to build one

1. Pick an enemy. What did the industry do to this person? Example: a course that taught for six months and never handed them the finished file.
2. Pick the mechanism that kills it. Not any mechanism. The one that answers that enemy. Example: download the brain. A to Z without the middle.
3. Pick who's listening. Audience plus gate. Example: busy owner, 600+.
4. Write the hook using the hook formula. Experience + emotion + frustration.
5. Check it against the mechanism test. If it passes, it's an ad.



### The mechanism test

Delete the mechanism from the hook. Does the hook still make sense?

If yes, throw it out. The mechanism has to be doing the work, or a competitor can run the same ad word for word.

Second half of the same rule: end the hook by naming the problem with the other path, not by asking for anything.

"Secret sauce is always sketchy. Legit sauce is never a secret." Publish the mechanism. Do not tease it.

## Script format

TYPE is cold. origin_angle is required. That tag is a short name for the argument so we know which ad the person came from. Fill it in.

For this job, the CTA is checkout. Not a booked call.

```
TYPE          cold
ORIGIN_ANGLE  short tag for this argument
ANGLE         one line — the enemy × the mechanism × the audience
FOR           who this one is for, one line
AWARENESS     unaware | problem-aware | solution-aware | product-aware | most-aware
GATE          600+ | 700+ no negatives | premium | open
HOOK          0–3s      first two sentences, must pass all four cause-first checks
BODY          10–60s    Validate → Reveal the mechanism → Connect to the desire → Proof
CTA           last 10–30s   checkout, never a booked call
CLOSE         the two promises
WORDS         <count>
SHOOT         outfit + location note
```

| Shape                     | How it opens                |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------- |

## Headline altitudes

Write the same person three ways. Pick by how specific it is. Specific almost always wins.

- Broad — desire or emotion: "If you've been trying to get funding and keep hearing no…"
- Mid — experience or behavior: "If you've applied at three banks this year…"
- Specific — stacked: "If you're sitting on a 680 and you already bought the course…"

Do not mash more than one desire into one headline.

## The angle mix for a set of ten

3 speed · 2 proof · 2 risk-removal · 2 authority · 1 comparison/ROI.

Eight categories to pull from: Speed · Certainty · Risk removal · Status/identity · Process transparency · Proof · Mistakes · Comparison.

The overall enemy of this offer is the learning curve. Each of the ten still needs its own reason to buy. Ten hats on one argument is still one argument.

## The gate mix

Every script gets a gate. Default is 600.


| Gate               | Share of the set |
| ------------------ | ---------------- |
| 600+               | about 55%        |
| 700+, no negatives | about 20%        |
| Premium / strict   | about 15%        |
| Open, no gate      | about 10%        |


Disqualification is a hook device. Telling someone who this isn't for makes the people who do fit lean in.

## The close carries two promises, always

The wording may vary. Two promises may never be missing:

1. **No hard pull.** Say it as "no hard inquiry", "soft pull only", or "zero score impact". Any of the three.
2. **Nothing happens without their say-so.** Say it as "no obligation", "nothing moves until you say so", or both.

Do not reword either promise into something new. Pick from the phrasings above, or match their shape exactly.

Real examples from running ads:

- "Zero score impact. No obligation. Nothing moves until you say so."
- "Soft pull only. Zero impact on your score. Nothing moves until you say so."
- "There's no hard inquiry. There's no obligation. Nothing moves on your file until you tell us to move it."



## Proof — this is the entire list. Do not add to it.

- Close to a decade in business funding.
- Over $25 million secured for our clients.
- Koi Poke — one restaurant, already turned away once, now a franchise with multiple locations.

That is the list. Anything else is a made-up win.

## Client-journey craft

When you name steps, open them with You. Even the ones we built.

Name the outcome, not the task. "See what your file supports." Not "Complete the analysis phase."

# PART 5 — THE VOICE

Study these pairs. Left is a generic AI draft. Right is how Chris actually writes. Match the right column.

First person. Chris talking. Never brand voice.

Short sentences. One idea each. Line breaks carry the pacing.

Name the cost before the fix.

Absolution: "That's not on you."

Write like a confident founder talking to another founder. No filler. Every sentence earns the next.

## Pair 1 — hook

Model: "Getting denied for business funding is incredibly frustrating, and it might not even be about your credit score at all."

Chris: "If your business got denied for funding, you didn't lose because of your credit. You lost because nobody looked at your file the way a bank actually looks at it."

Why: Chris names an actual actor doing the harm and states it as flat fact. The generic line hedges with "might not even be" and blames nobody.

## Pair 2 — body

Model: "Unfortunately, many funding consultants take a one-size-fits-all approach when submitting your applications to lenders, which can hurt your chances."

Chris: "And then they logged into Experian, pulled up a list of lenders they use for every single client, and started blasting out applications."

Why: a specific scene you can picture, instead of the abstract phrase "one-size-fits-all".

## Pair 3 — CTA shape

Model: "If you're ready to learn more about your funding options, click the link below to get started today."

Chris: "Click the link below. Fill out a short two minute application. Book your free funding strategy call."

Why: three flat back-to-back commands with a real number attached.

For this job, keep that shape. Point it at the purchase. Never "book a free strategy call." Never apply first.

## Pair 4 — close

Model: "Click below to find out what you may qualify for today."

Chris: "Click below. Let's go find out your number."

Why: "let's" puts him on their side of the click, and "your number" is a specific thing to go get.

## Pair 5 — hook

Model: "Are you a business owner struggling to secure the funding your company needs? In the next few minutes, I'll show you exactly how to get approved."

Chris: "If you're a business owner who's been trying to get funding, and you've either been denied, gotten the runaround, or you just honestly don't know what you qualify for, I want to talk to you for a couple minutes."

Why: three specific situations instead of one vague word, and "I want to talk to you" instead of opening on a question.

## Pair 6 — body

Model: "You had big plans for that funding, whether it was growing your business or improving your personal life, and now those plans are on hold."

Chris: "You had a plan. You were going to take that capital and actually do something with it. Scale your ads, build out your team, maybe finally take that trip, pay off some debt, actually build the thing your family's been watching you work toward."

Why: five concrete, different-sized dreams instead of two generic buckets.

## Pair 7 — CTA shape

Model: "Click the link below to apply and schedule a call with our team to discuss your funding options."

Chris: "Click the link below. Fill out the short application. And book your free funding strategy call."

Why: three short steps in the order you'd actually do them.

Again: that shape, pointed at buy. Wrong funnel if you send them to a call.

## Pair 8 — close

Model: "If you've been applying without success, we can help you understand what's really going on with your file."

Chris: "If you have been applying and not getting anywhere, the file knows why. Let us show you what it says."

Why: Chris gives the file itself a voice instead of the generic "we can help you understand."

## Pair 9 — sound only, not structure

Model: "In today's fast-paced world, are you about to submit another funding application? Wait — you'll want to see this first."

Chris: "Stop. Before you fill out another funding application... watch this."

Why: this pair is different from the other eight. The real Chris line fails the cause-first hook test. It asks twice ("watch this") and never names who or what caused the problem. Do not use this pair to teach hook structure. It is here to show a difference in sound only: short, blunt commands ("Stop.") next to a padded AI-style question with a banned opener ("in today's fast-paced world"). For hook structure, lead with why the last path failed, then earn the "stop."
