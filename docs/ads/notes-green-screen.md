## The concept

One green screen ad format works for both funnels. Chris stands in front of an iPhone Notes
screenshot of the 7-step funding process, points at each step, and talks through it.

- **Reference ad:** Dr. Matt Shiver's "Facebook Ads for Beginners (Coaches)" Notes app ad. He walks
  through each step on the note, says what to do and why, then goes to the call to action.
- **What the note is:** the roadmap for anyone who wants funding forever. The viewer gets real
  steps, in order, on screen.
- **SLO version:** teaches the steps and sells the $297 roadmap built from their own credit file. It
  matches the /roadmap page's "funding forever" frame.
- **Sorting hat version:** same note. Where you are in the process decides which steps you still
  need to get the maximum amount of funding, and the ad ends on booking a call.
- **Rule:** the note, the ads, the landing page and the deliverables all follow the same 7-step
  order.

**Audience:** this market is at the highest awareness level. They know what credit card stacking
and funding are, so the ads never explain it as if they don't. The easiest buyers already know it
and want to see Chris as the authority and get closed.

## How to film it

The whole shoot runs on the phone: Notes for the background, Instagram's green screen effect to
record, Submagic for captions.

1. Put the iPhone in dark mode, open Notes, paste the note from the next section, and set the first
   line to Title.
2. Screenshot the note. To scroll down it while you talk, screen-record a slow scroll instead and
   use that video.
3. In Instagram, open Reels, tap Effects, search "Green Screen," and pick the screenshot or
   recording as the background.
4. Stand in the bottom right of the frame and point at each step as you say it.
5. Save the reel to your camera roll without posting it.
6. Run it through Submagic for captions.

## The note

This is FundHub's funding process in order, approved on 10/2/2026. Paste it into Notes exactly as
written.

```
How to Get the Maximum Amount of Funding (Step by Step) 💰

1. Check your credit 🔍
Pull your report and make sure everything on it is accurate and in line.
2. Open your businesses (if needed) 🏢
Do this early, because the longer a business has been open, the more funding it qualifies for.
3. Optimize your personal and business information 📋
Make sure your names, addresses and business details match on every report.
4. Remove negative items and pay down balances 🧹
Get the negative items off your report and pay your cards down before you apply.
5. Find the banks that approve files like yours 🏦
Look for the banks that approve your type of credit in your state.
6. Apply in the right order 📊
The order you apply in decides how many approvals you get.
7. Remove inquiries and repeat 🔁
Clear the inquiries from each round before the next one, then do it again for every business.
```

Formatting rules for the note:

- Each explanation line tells the viewer what to do, in plain words. Offer features like "soft pull,
  zero impact on your score" stay off the note.
- A number, the step, then one explanation line stacked under it. No bullets, no check marks.
- One emoji per step, only at the end of the step line.
- The whole note fits on one screen.

## How bridges and loops work

Every ad opens with a cause-first hook, then a bridge on line 2. The bridge gives the viewer one
concrete fact and holds back the part that makes it useful until right before the CTA.

1. **Hook:** cause first, and it tells them something they don't already know. The model line is
   "The condition of your credit determines where you are in the funding process."
2. **Bridge (line 2):** adds something the hook didn't. It's one concrete fact plus one specific
   thing held back. No greeting, intro, credentials or restating the hook.
3. **The hold:** the loop stays open across several lines. It never resolves in the same sentence or
   the next paragraph.
4. **Payoff:** lands right before the CTA, so the CTA is the next thing they hear.
5. **Double loop:** open loop A on line 2, open loop B in the middle, close B, close A, then the CTA.

**What failed:** "Two of these steps decide whether you get funded once or forever" held something
vague and handed them nothing.

**What works:** "Banks will fund you again for every business you open, and one step on this list
makes that possible." It hands them a real fact and holds back which step, and that's paid off as
step seven.

## Copy rules

These rules apply to every script in this concept.

- **Length:** SLO ads run about a minute (around 150 words, up to about 1:15). Sorting hat ads run
  115 to 125 words with no introduction.
- **Structure:** hook first, cause first. Line 2 is a bridge with an open loop.
- **Certainty:** no "could," "potentially" or "no guarantees." Say what's most likely based on the
  data.
- **Voice:** second person, straight at the viewer. Never make them feel stupid.
- **Words to avoid:** "credit repair" (say optimize your credit), "your number" or "the number,"
  "carry," EIN, DUNS and net-30.
- **Constructions to avoid:** "it's not X, it's Y," two-sentence parallels, slogans, cute images,
  and telling them what something isn't.
- **Refund line:** "If you're not happy with what you get, email us and we'll refund you." Never
  state a day count.
- **Terms:** a funding sequence holds several rounds, so the next one is never "round two."
- **Handoff for filming:** plain text paragraphs with blank lines between them and no markdown, so
  it pastes into BigVU in one go.
