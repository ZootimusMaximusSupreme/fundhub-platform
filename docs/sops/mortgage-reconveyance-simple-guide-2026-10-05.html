Five rules Bryan gives. Then the words you will see in this guide, in plain English.

Learn each step before you do it. Bryan: "don't do nothing you don't understand."

TrustorThe deed of trust's word for the borrower. That is you.

02

Get the county list Lessons 12, 33

  • Search the owner's name on your county records website. (Bryan used his company's name, because his company owned the property.)
  • Go from the first paper to the last. Write down each number and date. For the deed of trust, pick the type "deed of trust." Bryan warns of a typo in that list.
You needYour county records website.
Done whenYou have a list of every paper with its number and date.
03

Read your deed Lessons 3, 21, 33

  • Who gave it to you? Who got it? What did you pay? The price line often says $1 or $10. Bryan calls that line your receipt.
  • Was it signed, sealed, witnessed, and handed over? Did anyone tell you at closing to accept it? Bryan says they did not.
You needYour recorded deed.
Done whenYou can answer every question above.
04

Read the deed of trust Lessons 5–12, 33

  • Read the word list at the front first. Bryan says the tricks are there. Find "trustor" (that is you). Bryan says the trustor holds the power.
  • Find the lender, the trustee, and the loan amount. Read what "loan" means. Ask: what was really loaned?
  • Go to the signature page. Bryan's had only the borrowers and a notary. Did the trustee ever sign to accept the job?
You needYour recorded deed of trust. One sheet for notes.
Done whenYour notes sheet has every answer.
05

Get the note and closing papers Lessons 18, 21, 33

  • Ask the lender for a copy of your note. Check the dates and amounts. Is there a notary stamp? (His had none.) Is "pay to the order of" on the last page? Ignore a "true copy" stamp. Bryan calls it a scare tactic.
  • Right after closing, ask for the closing papers with everyone's signatures. Bryan got sent to the lawyer named on the papers, who never called back. Bryan also says to check your papers for an arbitration rule (a rule that sends a fight to a private judge, not a court).
You needYour written requests.
Done whenThe copies, or your requests, are in your folder.

Phase 2 · Steps 6–8

Accept and secure

Make your ownership official on the record. Then check for liens and file your UCC papers.

Flow
06Accept your deed. Take it to the county.

No: stay calm. Ask for the reason in writing.
07UCC-11 search. Get a certified copy.
08Record the UCC-1 package at the county
08File the UCC-1 with the state, using the county number
06

Accept your deed Lessons 3, 4, 8, 33

  • Bryan: "That's actually the first step is accepting that." Get a certified copy of your recorded deed from the county.
  • Write a one-page acceptance. It says you are the owner on the deed and you accept it. (See the Papers page.)
  • Have it notarized. Bryan says a notary at UPS works (someone on the call says about $5). They check your ID and do not read the paper.
  • Record both together, the acceptance on top. If the clerk pushes back, explain calmly. If they still say no, get the reason in writing. Bryan says clerks must give it.
You needCertified copy of your deed + your signed, notarized acceptance.
Done whenBoth are recorded and you have the number. Or you have the clerk's "no" in writing.
07

Check for liens (UCC-11 search) Lessons 16, 17, 33

  • Find your state's UCC-11 form on the Secretary of State website. Fill in your information. The search is on your name.
  • Send it in. Get a certified copy of the answer. Bryan: "That's going to prove that there's no lien on you."
  • Why: to see if the lender ever filed its claim. If nothing is on file, Bryan says they never "perfected" (made official) their claim. Check again now and then.
You needYour state's UCC-11 form.
Done whenThe certified answer is in your folder, with its date.
08

File your UCC-1 package Lessons 15, 16, 33

  • Stack three papers: your note, your security agreement (a paper that says who gets a claim on the property), and the UCC-1 form on top. Sign them. Bryan says "all of it is notarized," but he said that about a filing that is not a house.
  • Record the stack at the county first. The county gives it a number. In Texas, real property needs an extra page (an addendum).
  • Then file the UCC-1 with the state. Write the county number in the "collateral" box (the box about the property). Keep both numbers.
  • Bryan's trust is the secured party (the one that holds the claim). He says a trust is not required. It "makes it look better."
You needNote + security agreement + UCC-1 form.
Done whenYou have the county number and the state filing number.

09

Send the dispute letter Lessons 26, 33

  • Bryan calls it "the first letter I sent them after closing." It goes to a person at the lender. The letter names the lender's president or CEO.
  • It asks for 3 things: a certified copy of the note, a certified copy of the deed of trust, and where to send payment. Someone must sign the copies "under penalty of perjury" (swearing it is true).
  • It gives them 15 to 90 days from when they get it. It warns a "payment in full" is coming. No answer means they agree.
  • Attach your UCC-1. Sign and seal.
You needThe letter + your UCC-1.
Done whenYour copy and the mail number are in your folder.
10

Send the conditional acceptance offer Lesson 33

  • It says three things: there is a problem, here is time to fix it, and here is what happens if you don't. It is an offer to settle.
  • Send it by certified mail. Bryan: "a gentleman always gives notice." His went March 1.
You needThe offer letter.
Done whenYour copy and the certified receipt are in your folder.
11

Pay with money orders Lessons 24–26, 33

  • Pay the amount they expect, on time. Use post office money orders. Each holds $1,000 at most, so Bryan used two.
  • Write "payment in full" words on the front and "full and final settlement" words on the back. (See the Papers page.)
  • Put a short note in every envelope. Never send the payment alone. Pay every month for 3 months. His first went by registered mail. He doesn't say how he mailed the rest. Check online that each was cashed, and save the page.
You needMoney orders, notes, registered mail.
Done whenCopies of front and back, notes, receipts, and "cashed" pages are saved.
12

Wait 90 days or more Lessons 26, 33

  • Bryan: "it can't be no sooner than 90 days." In that time they can send your money back. If they keep it and stay quiet, he treats that as yes.
  • Bryan does not say which day starts the count. His own next step came 54 days after his first payment.
You needA calendar.
Done when90 days passed and you wrote down the end date.

Bona Fide Dispute LetterStep 9 · Lessons 26, 33
  1. From: you. Bryan signed "as the [settlor] on behalf of the trust."
  2. To: a person at the lender. The letter names the lender's president or CEO as a "real party [in] interest" (the one who truly has the right to be paid).
  3. Your loan account number.
  4. Opening: "This private matter concerning the mortgage filed in the county of [county]," with your state and parcel number (the county's number for your land).
  5. Who owns what: your company gave its property to your trust on [date]. The trust's claim is on file with the state. "UCC financial statement attached."
  6. Say no loan is on file under the UCC rules (Article 9). Say no UCC-3 was ever filed. (A UCC-3 is the form used to move a claim to someone else.)
  7. Challenge them to prove they are the real party and really gave value.
  8. "Take notice, the note and the mortgage are inseparable." His letter also cites a federal banking law.
  9. The offer to settle, if they send 3 things: (a) a certified copy of the note, (b) a certified copy of the deed of trust, each signed under penalty of perjury, (c) where to send payment.
  10. Time: "not less than 15 days and not more than 90 days" from when they get it.
  11. Silence: no answer means full agreement. A payment in full may be applied.
  12. Close: "[govern yourselves] accordingly." Sign and seal.

13

Ask for a statement of account Lessons 27, 28, 33

  • You write down what you think you still owe. Bryan wrote zero. You ask them to sign back and agree or fix your number.
  • Send it to a real person by name (his went to the president/CEO), at the lender's address. Bryan: companies can't talk, people can.
  • Sign it in front of a notary. Send it by certified mail. Write down the day and time it was delivered. Ask only for the "statement of account," not the other requests the law allows.

14

Wait 14 days for their signed answer Lessons 27, 28

  • They must sign and send back a "yes" or a fix within 14 days after they get it.
  • Bryan's deadline math: mail date + 3 days for mail + 2 weeks + 3 days for return mail + 1 day for a Sunday. If no one signs, he says your number stands.
You needA calendar.

15

Send a default notice Lesson 28

  • Send it to the same person. Say they missed the deadline and sent no signed record.
  • List what you already sent: the offer, the payments, the money orders. Say your number stands. Ask them to fix their books.
  • Sign it and add your seal. Bryan's was dated the deadline day.
You needDates and receipts from your folder.
Done whenYour copy and receipt are saved.
16

Send the notice of dishonor Lesson 33

  • Send it to the lender's CEO by name, with a copy to the trustee named in the deed of trust. Bryan says the trustee is the one who will "try to bust a move" (act against you) later.
  • It says no one ever signed to accept the deed of trust. So their powers are void. It tells the whole story with dates and mail numbers.
  • It asks them to release the lien within 10 business days and refund your payments. Attach your fee schedule. Sign it. In it, you speak as the trustor, not the borrower.

Papers · Phase 4 · Part 2

Notice of dishonor and fee schedule

This notice tells the whole story in one paper. You need every date and mail number from earlier steps.

Notice of Dishonor for Non-Acceptance of the Deed of Trust Contract OfferStep 16 · Lesson 33
  1. Top line: "notice to agent is notice to [principal]," and the other way around. (Telling the worker counts as telling the boss.)
  2. Title, "pursuant to UCC 2-202."
  3. Heading: the deed of trust date, a certified mail number, and the loan number.
  4. Date. To: the CEO by name (Bryan calls him the agent), at the company address. Copy to: the trustee, at his address.
  5. Say no signed record shows anyone accepted the deed of trust "contract offer."
  6. Statement of facts: the trustee and the lender never signed. "Nor were either of the two parties physically present at closing."
  7. The law: UCC 2-206 and your state's version. If no one tells you an offer was accepted in time, you may treat it as dead ("lapsed").
  8. "Please take notice": you write as the trustor, not the borrower. Name the deed of trust, its date, county, address, and parcel number.
  9. "Please also note": all powers given to the trustee and lender are no good ("null and void").
  10. The story, in order. Your conditional acceptance (date, certified mail). Your payment (registered mail number, delivery date and time). The money orders they cashed (UCC 3-311). The statement of account (delivery date and time), "as mentioned in previous default notice." Balance: zero.
  11. Demand: release all liens "within 10 business days of the receipt of this notice," and a full refund of your payments by a certified funds check (a check the bank promises is good), sent with tracking.
  12. "Take further notice": if it can't be settled privately, the trust is ready to take legal action.
  13. Fee schedule attached. "This notice has been authenticated by signature below." Sign.

More on the reconveyance

Why Bryan says he can replace the trustee, what the paper does, and what he leaves open.

Flow · how the earlier steps lead here
04The trustee in the deed of trust never signed to accept
16Your notice says the trustee's powers are void
20–21Certificate of Dishonor and your note recorded
22About 2 weeks. A letter says they will make the loan due now.
23You, as trustor, name a new trustee
23The paper says the debt is paid and the papers are handed in

23Record it. Nothing came after it in his file.
Bryan's reasons
  • The trustee never signed to accept the job.
  • "when we're the trust[or] we got all the power." He says the trustor can remove a trustee who never accepted.
  • A contract needs both sides there, "especially in a trust."
  • He put that trustee on notice early (step 16), because he expected the trustee to make the next move.
What set it off
  • A letter about trying to "accelerate" (make the whole loan due now).
  • He says the trustee sent it. He also reads a lawyer's letter dated July 13. The transcript doesn't settle if they are the same letter.
  • He recorded the substitution July 27. His reply to the lawyer, the next day, says the trustee was replaced and points to a county number.
Names change by state
  • An article Bryan reads says deed of reconveyance, full reconveyance, and satisfaction of mortgage: "It means the same thing."
  • It says in some states a full reconveyance is signed by the trustee and a notary.
  • Lesson 11: when a loan is paid, title goes back to the borrower by reconveyance.
Mortgage states (2 parties)
  • A mortgage has two parties. A deed of trust has three. Bryan says the papers are the same kind.
  • To a Pennsylvania caller: "it depends on what your angle is." Settle, or treat the paper as no good ("dissolve" it) because no one accepted it.
  • Check your own state's law.
Not always last
  • In Bryan's own file, it was the last paper recorded.
  • In a client's file (MERS lesson 6), Bryan became the substitute trustee himself. In that file he shows the substitution before the notice of dishonor.
  • So "last" is his example, not a rule he states.
Bryan doesn't say
  • Where the blank form comes from. He says he "found this document" at the county.
  • A notary, who signs, or who may be the new trustee.
  • The property description, fees, or whether to mail copies.
  • What "without warranty" means.
