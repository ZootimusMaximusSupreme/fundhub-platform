Word list
DeedThe paper that says who owns the land.
Deed of trustThe lender's paper on your home. Some states call it a mortgage.
NoteYour written promise to pay the loan.
Trustor (borrower)Bryan's word for the borrower. That is you.
Beneficiary (lender)The company that made the loan.
TrusteeThe middle person named in the deed of trust.
County recorderThe county office that keeps the public list of land papers.
RecordTake a paper to the county so it goes on that public list.
Certified copyAn official copy with the county's stamp on it.
NotaryA person who watches you sign and then stamps the paper.
Certified or registered mailMail with a tracking number and proof it arrived.
UCCBusiness rules. Every state has its own copy of them.
Secretary of StateThe state office where UCC papers are filed.
LienA claim someone has on your property.

The big picture

The whole trip, in 8 stops

This is the order Bryan used on his own home. Each stop is one page in this guide.

PHASE1

Get ready Steps 1–5

Find and read your papers. No mailing yet.

PHASE2

Accept and secure Steps 6–8

Accept your deed on the record. Check for liens. File your UCC papers.

PHASE3

Dispute and pay Steps 9–12

Tell them there is a problem. Pay "in full" with money orders. Wait 90 days.

PHASE4

Statement of account Steps 13–16

Ask them to confirm what you owe. If they stay quiet, send default notices.

PHASE5

Notary Steps 17–20

A notary sends two letters and becomes your witness that they ignored you.

PHASE6

Record and wait Steps 21–22

Put four papers on the public record. Wait about two weeks.

PHASE7

Reconveyance Step 23

Record the substitution of trustee for reconveyance. It is the last paper.

PHASE8

After Step 24

Keep the whole file. Answer any letters that come.

2

Get the county list Lessons 12, 33

  • Look up your name in your county's land records. Go from the first paper to the last.
  • Write down each paper's number and date. Save your deed and your deed of trust.
You needYour county records website.
✓ Done whenYou have a list of every paper with its number and date.
3

Read your deed Lessons 3, 21

  • Who gave it to you? Who got it? What did you pay?
  • Was it signed, sealed, witnessed, and handed over? Did you ever accept it?
You needYour recorded deed.
✓ Done whenYou can answer every question above.
4

Read the deed of trust Lessons 5–12, 33

  • Read the word list at the front first. Bryan says the tricks are there.
  • Find the borrower, lender, trustee, and loan amount. Ask what "loan" really means.
  • Check the signature page. Who signed? Did the trustee sign?
You needYour recorded deed of trust. One sheet for notes.
✓ Done whenYour notes sheet has every answer.
5

Get the note and closing papers Lessons 18, 21, 33

  • Ask the lender for a copy of your note. Look for a notary stamp, and for "pay to the order of" on the last page.
  • Ask for the closing papers with everyone's signatures. Check for an arbitration rule.
You needYour written requests.
✓ Done whenThe copies, or your requests, are in your folder.

2
Phase 2 · Steps 6–8

Accept and secure

Make your ownership official on the record. Then check for liens and file your UCC papers.

6 Accept your deed➜7 Check for liens (UCC-11)➜8 File your UCC-1 package
“That's actually the first step is accepting that.Bryan, lesson 33
6

Accept your deed Lessons 3, 4, 8, 33

  • Get a certified copy of your deed from the county.
  • Write a one-page "Acknowledgment and Acceptance of Warranty Deed." Say you are the owner on the deed and you accept it. Black out any Social Security or driver's license number.
  • Have it notarized before you go. Bryan says a UPS store notary works, about $5.
  • Record both papers together at the county.
  • If the clerk says no, stay calm. Ask them to write down why.
You needCertified copy of your deed + your signed, notarized acceptance.
✓ Done whenBoth are recorded and you wrote down the number. Or you have the clerk's "no" in writing.
7

Check for liens (UCC-11 search) Lessons 16, 17, 33

  • Get your state's UCC-11 form from the Secretary of State.
  • Fill it out and send it in. It shows who has a claim on you or your property.
  • Get a certified copy of the answer. Bryan says check again now and then.
You needYour state's UCC-11 form.
✓ Done whenThe certified answer is in your folder, with its date.
8

File your UCC-1 package Lessons 15, 16, 33

  • Put three papers together: your note, your security agreement, and the UCC-1 form. Get them notarized.
  • Record them at the county first. Bryan: "You register first on the county level."
  • Then file the UCC-1 with the Secretary of State. Write the county's number on it.
  • Bryan used his trust as the secured party. He says you don't have to use a trust.
You needNote + security agreement + UCC-1 form.
✓ Done whenYou have the county number and the state filing number.

3
Phase 3 · Steps 9–12

Dispute and pay

Tell them there is a problem. Then pay in a special way and wait.

9 Dispute letter➜10 Conditional acceptance➜11 Money orders × 3➜12 Wait 90+ days
9

Send the dispute letter Lessons 26, 33

  • Ask for 3 things: a certified copy of the note, a certified copy of the deed of trust, and where to send payment.
  • Give them 15 to 90 days to answer.
  • Tell them a "payment in full" is coming. Say no answer means they agree.
  • Attach your UCC-1. Sign it and seal it. Mail it and keep the tracking number.
You needThe letter + your UCC-1.
✓ Done whenYour copy and the mail receipt are in your folder.
10

Send the conditional acceptance offer Lesson 33

  • Tell them there is a problem, give them time to fix it, and say what happens if they don't.
  • Send it by certified mail. Bryan: "a gentleman always gives notice."
You needThe offer letter.
✓ Done whenYour copy and the certified receipt are in your folder.
11

Pay with money orders Lessons 24–26, 33

  • Use post office money orders. Each one holds $1,000 at most.
  • Front: write that it is payment in full, on the condition the debt is canceled. Add the loan number.
  • Back: write that cashing it means "full and final settlement" of the account.
  • Add a short note. Send by registered mail. Do it every month for 3 months.
  • Check online that each one was cashed. Print it.
You needMoney orders, notes, registered mail.
✓ Done whenFront/back copies, notes, receipts, and "cashed" printouts are saved.
12

Wait 90 days or more Lessons 26, 33

  • Bryan: "it can't be no sooner than 90 days." In that time they can send your money back.
You needA calendar.
✓ Done when90 days passed and you wrote down the end date.

Phase 4 · Steps 13–16

Statement of account

Ask them to confirm what you owe. If they stay quiet, tell them they are in default.

13 Statement of account➜14 Wait 14 days➜15 Default notice➜16 Notice of dishonor
13

Ask for a statement of account Lessons 27, 28, 33

  • Fill in the form: your account number, your property, what you started with, what you paid, and what you believe you owe now.
  • Send it to a real person by name, like the company's CEO.
  • Sign it in front of a notary. Send it by certified mail.
You needThe statement of account form + cover letter.
✓ Done whenYour copy and receipt are saved, with the delivery date.
14

Wait 14 days for their signed answer Lessons 27, 28

  • They must sign and send back a "yes" or a fix within 14 days after they get it.
  • If no one signs it, Bryan says your number stands.
You needA calendar.
✓ Done whenThe deadline passed. Any answer is saved.
15

Send a default notice Lesson 28

  • Say they did not answer by the deadline.
  • List everything you already sent: the offer, the payments, the money orders.
  • Say your number stands. Sign it and seal it. Mail it.
You needDates and receipts from your folder.
✓ Done whenYour copy and receipt are saved.
16

Send the notice of dishonor Lesson 33

  • Send it to the lender's CEO and to the trustee.
  • Say they never accepted or signed the deed of trust. Tell the whole story so far.
  • Ask them to release the lien within 10 business days and refund you. Attach your fee list.
  • Sign as the trustor (the borrower). Mail it to both.
You needThe notice + your fee list.
✓ Done whenBoth mail receipts are saved.

Phase 5 · Steps 17–20

Notary

A notary sends two letters for you and becomes your witness that they ignored you.

17 Notary packet➜18 Notice of Breach➜10 days➜19 Chance to fix➜10 days➜20 Certificate
“first is always the presentmentBryan, lesson 29
17

Give the notary your packet Lessons 29, 30, 33

  • Find a notary who will do a "notarial presentment." Bryan says many don't know it, so you may need to explain it.
  • Write and sign a request to the notary. It counts as your sworn statement.
  • Give the notary one copy of everything you sent, with the mail receipts.
You needYour signed request + one full set of copies.
✓ Done whenThe notary has your packet and you kept a copy.
18

Notary sends the "Notice of Breach" Lesson 30

  • The notary signs and mails it, not you. It says they ignored your papers.
  • They have 10 days from the postmark to answer the notary.
You needNothing new.
✓ Done when10 days passed. Copy and receipt are saved.
19

Notary sends the "Opportunity to Cure" Lesson 30

  • This is their last chance to fix it. They get 10 more days from the postmark.
  • Bryan says the notary may give about 5 extra days.
You needNothing new.
✓ Done whenThe time passed with no fix. Copy and receipt are saved.
20

Notary signs the "Certificate of Dishonor" Lessons 29, 30

  • The notary signs it as a witness that no one answered.
  • A second, different notary stamps the first notary's signature.
  • Your signed request from step 17 is attached.
You needA second notary.
✓ Done whenIt is signed, double-stamped, and your request is attached.

Phase 6 · Steps 21–22

Record and wait

Put four papers on the public record. Then give them about two weeks.

21 Record 4 papers➜22 Wait ~2 weeks
21

Record four papers at the county Lessons 19, 33

  • A copy of your notice of dishonor (step 16).
  • Your note. Bryan's has a notary stamp and a line where he accepts it.
  • Your fee list.
  • The Certificate of Dishonor (step 20).
You needThe four papers.
✓ Done whenEach one has a county number and a copy is saved.
22

Wait about two weeks Lesson 33

  • Bryan: "I just give them about two weeks to try to respond."
You needA calendar.
✓ Done whenAbout two weeks passed. Any answer is saved.
7
Phase 7 · Step 23

Reconveyance

The last paper. It goes on the county record last.

23

Record the substitution of trustee for reconveyance Lessons 11, 33

  • You, as the borrower, remove the old trustee. Bryan says that trustee never accepted.
  • You name a new trustee. Bryan named his son.
  • The paper says everything is paid, and the note and deed of trust are canceled.
  • The new trustee signs the property back "without warranty."
  • Record it. In Bryan's file it was the last paper on the county list.
You needThe substitution paper + your new trustee.
✓ Done whenIt is recorded and it is the last paper on your county list.

Phase 8 · Step 24

After

Keep everything. Answer any letters that come.

24

Keep the file and answer letters Lesson 33

  • Keep the whole file and every receipt, start to finish.
  • If a lawyer or the loan company writes, Bryan sends it back and lists what they did wrong.
  • He points to the county numbers of your recorded papers.
  • He tells them to mail his trust by certified or registered mail.
You needYour folder.
✓ Done whenEvery letter is answered and saved.
Extras

Optional: MERS check

Lessons 35–37.
  • Is "MERS" named on your deed of trust?
  • If yes, look at the county list for an "assignment."
  • Check who signed it and who stamped it.

If the county won't record

Lesson 33.
  • File it at your federal district court as a "miscellaneous" filing.
  • Get a certified copy.
  • Take that copy to the county.

If you are in a foreclosure case

Lesson 33.
  • Bryan still says get your deed acceptance (step 6) and your certified UCC-11 (step 7) ready.
  • He says his way is for before trouble starts, not after.

If the home was already sold

Lesson 33.
  • Ask the county if there was extra money from the sale.
  • Get the answer in writing.

Your state, not Texas

Bryan cites Texas law next to each UCC rule. Find your own state's version of each one and use your own county recorder. Bryan: "you need to also find your state, if you're not in Texas."
