## What good setting is

A setter gets a person onto a strategy call with the closer, on time, at a computer, and clear on why that hour matters.

The setter warms the lead from the first minute until the closer is on the line. The setter does not close. The setter does not quote what they qualify for. The setter does not pull credit.

Right now Chris does both jobs. He still runs the setter steps. The handoff note goes on the CRM card. When a closer is a different person, that same note goes out as a three-way text.

## Two kinds of leads

**They already booked.** This call is short. About 3 to 5 minutes. Confirm the time. Get them excited. Make sure they will be at a computer. Answer questions. Cole’s rule: do not put a second full sales call in front of a booked closer call. That creates two chances to no-show.

**They opted in and did not book.** This is the main setter job in Cole’s system. Call them, find the problem and the goal, sell the strategy call, and put it on the calendar. That call can run 5 to 10 minutes.

## Speed

Call within **60 seconds** of the opt-in or the booking. That is the Fundhub rule.

Cole’s tested window is inside 10 minutes. Inside that window, answer rates were about 2 to 3 times higher. 60 seconds is inside his window.

If the lead lands outside texting hours (before 6:00 a.m. or after 10:00 p.m. Phoenix time), do not call or text. Call them first thing at 6:00 a.m.

Fight for the newest lead first. A lead from the last hour beats a lead from last week.

## If they do not answer

1. Call.
2. They do not pick up. Hang up. Call again right away. Cole calls this the double dial. Do this every time.
3. They still do not pick up. Then, in this order:
   - Leave the voicemail below.
   - Send the text below.
   - Send the video of Chris.
4. Inside the first 2 hours, call up to 2 more times. Cole’s cadence is 2 to 3 calls in the first 2 hours. The double dial is the first try.
5. After that, keep working the lead for 7 days if they never booked. Newest leads first. Stop when they book, when they say stop, or when the 7 days are up.

## Call script — they already booked

Say it like a person. Do not read it like a form.

1. **Name.** “Hey, is this [Name]?”
2. **Who and why.** “Hey [Name], it’s Chris with Fundhub. You just booked your strategy call for [day and time]. I am calling to make sure that time still works.”
3. **If the time is bad.** Move it. Then stop. Do not pitch.
4. **Frame.** “On that call we pull your credit with you on the line and map what you qualify for based on where you are right now. We have not pulled anything yet. Your score does not move. You will want a computer and a quiet spot.”
5. **Warm, from what they already said.** Use the survey. Do not invent. Pick one or two. “You said you are looking at about [their target] for [what they said they need it for]. Still the main thing?” “You said this would change [what they wrote]. Still true?”
6. **One pain question.** “What is the part that is stuck right now?” Let them talk. Do not fix it on this call. Write it down for the closer.
7. **Edify the call.** “Come ready. We will look at your file together and you will leave knowing the next move. Anything that would stop you from being there at [time]?”
8. **Close the loop.** “Perfect. I will text you the time. See you then.”

If they ask the price: “We walk the options on the strategy call after we see the file. The only charge before that call is the $32 soft pull, and your score does not move.”

If they ask what they are approved for: “We do not know until we pull the file with you on the call. That is what the call is for.”

## Call script — they did not book

This is Cole’s triage, in Fundhub words. Sell the strategy call. Do not sell a program.

1. **Name.** “Hey, is this [Name]?”
2. **Why you called.** “Hey [Name], it’s Chris with Fundhub. You just [watched the video / started the application] about getting your funding path mapped. Wanted to make sure that reached you.”
3. **Open the door.** “The next step is a strategy call. On that call we pull your credit with you on the line and show you what you qualify for from where you are right now. I have not pulled anything. You open to grabbing a time?”
4. **If they want a time now.** Offer two times. Book it. Jump to step 7.
5. **If they need a minute.** “Cool. So I put you with the right plan, what is the main thing you need the money for?” Then:
   - “How long has that been the case?”
   - “What have you already tried?”
   - “What is that costing you right now?”
   - “If this was handled, what does the next 12 months look like?”
   Stay here until you can say their problem, why it matters, and their goal in one sentence each. This part stays under 10 minutes.
6. **Sell the call, then book.** “I can get you on the calendar. You will be at a computer. We pull the file live with you, and your score does not move. I have [time] or [time]. Which one works?”
7. **Lock it.** They accept the calendar invite before you hang up.
8. **Help-me qualify.** “The person on that call needs a short note so you two can start fast. You good if I confirm a couple things?” Confirm only what they already said: the goal, personal or business, and that they can be at a computer. Do not ask for a Social Security number. Do not ask them to read a card number.

## Three-way text to the closer

Send this the minute the time is locked. Put the lead and the closer on the same text.

> Hey [Name] — Chris at Fundhub. You are set for [day and time]. Be at a computer. We pull your credit live on that call. Your score does not move.
>
> [Closer first name], [Name] is looking at [their goal, in their words]. The stuck part is [one sentence]. Survey said [one useful fact]. They can be at a computer.

**While Chris is both setter and closer:** send that text to the lead only. Paste the same note on the CRM card. Do not text yourself.

The closer reads that note before the call. The setter does not make the closer re-ask the whole story.

## Keep them warm until the call

The setter stays on the lead from the set until the closer is live.

- Same day: the confirm text above.
- They go quiet: one human text. Do not stack three messages in an hour.
- Day before and 2 hours before: the system already sends reminders. The setter only jumps in if they reply or if the reminder bounces.
- They reply with a question about a product: answer it, or say you will cover it on the call. Do not quote an approval. Do not promise a funding amount.
- They need to move the time: move it. Confirm the new time by text.
- After a no-show: call within 10 minutes. “Hey [Name], it’s Chris. We had [time] on the books. I am free at [time] or [time]. Which one should I hold?”

Cole’s follow-up rule underneath this: get the meeting today when you can. If they will not book today, leave the door open. Set the next touch. Do not punish them for saying not yet.

## What the setter never says

- A dollar amount they qualify for, or that they are approved.
- That credit was already pulled.
- The words Social Security number. That shows up only on checkout step 3.
- “We think,” “you might,” “maybe,” “probably.”
- A pitch of the $3,000 funding deposit, the $1,000 repair, the $200 repair trial, or the $5,000 to $10,000 Capital Blueprint. Those are closer offers. If they ask what it costs, use the one price line in the booked-call script.
- A second full discovery on a lead who already booked.

## Daily rhythm

Cole’s day shape, cut down for one person:

- **Start of day.** Clear every lead that came in overnight. Those get the 6:00 a.m. calls first. Then confirm today’s strategy calls.
- **All day.** New opt-in or new booking: call within 60 seconds. That interrupt wins over old follow-up.
- **Power hour.** One 60-minute block for people who did not answer yesterday. Phone down between dials only long enough to double dial. Cole’s floor on a real setter team is about 100 dials a day. One person who is also the closer will not hit that. Hit the new leads first. Use the power hour for the rest.
- **End of day.** Every booked call for tomorrow has a note: problem, goal, time, computer yes or no. Anyone with no note gets a text tonight, inside texting hours.

## When a second person becomes the closer

Keep this SOP. Change three lines.

- The 60-second call still happens. The setter still does not pitch.
- The video uses the “someone else is the closer” take.
- The three-way text is real: lead, setter, and closer on one thread, with the note above.
