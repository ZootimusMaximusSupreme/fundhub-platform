## Step 4 — Accept the warranty deed

**Course lesson 4.** Deed Acceptance Example.  
**Blank from the course:** `Ack_Acceptance of Deed TEMP.docx`

The course says a deed is not finished until it is:

1. **Signed**
2. **Sealed** (stamp or seal, when you use one)
3. **Attested / acknowledged** (notary or the acknowledgment the statute wants)
4. **Delivered** to the grantee
5. **Accepted** by the grantee

They say the title company does not have you accept the deed at closing. So you accept it yourself.

Do this:

1. Order a **certified copy** of the recorded warranty deed from the county recorder.
2. Write an **Acknowledgment and Acceptance of Warranty Deed**. You are the grantee. You say you accept the deed and the land on it.
3. Sign it. Seal it if that is your practice. Get it acknowledged.
4. Staple or attach the **certified copy** of the deed.
5. Take it to the recorder and ask them to file it so the record shows you accepted.
6. If the clerk says no, stay calm. Ask them to **write down why** they refused.
7. Scan the signed paper and the filing receipt into Drive.

**This house:** the acceptance papers already in Drive are for **Cooper City, Florida**, not this Scottsdale unit. Do this step again for **#4011** if this unit still needs its own acceptance. Florida folder: Reconveyance → **DEED ACCEPTANCE**.

## Step 15 — Mail the statement of account

**Course lessons 27 and 28.** SOA1 and SOA2.  
**Blanks on lesson 28:** `Request-Regarding-a-Statement-of-Account.pdf` and `Request4Accounting.pdf`

This is **UCC 9-210**. A debtor signs a paper that says what they believe is still owed, on a named date, on a named loan. The secured party has **14 days after they receive it** to sign a reply that **approves or corrects** that number. One response is free.

Do this:

1. Download the statement-of-account PDF from lesson 28 (it is also in the course zip).
2. Fill in:
   - Who it goes to (the creditor / servicer on this loan — use the address from the deed of trust and the loan file)
   - Who it is from (you, or the trust, the same way you send the rest of the mail)
   - Account or loan number **6000041372**
   - Collateral: **7137 E Rancho Vista Dr Unit 4011**
   - The balance you say is unpaid
   - The date
3. **Sign it.** In this lesson, “authenticated” means **signed**.
4. Mail it **certified** (he also shows registered mail).
5. Keep the receipt. Scan the signed letter and the receipt into Drive.
6. Count **14 days from when they receive it**.

**In Drive:** this letter has not been sent.

**Done when:** the signed letter and the mail receipt are in Drive, and you have written the date the 14 days end.

## Step 17 — Mail a private presentment yourself

**Course lesson 29.** NotaryPre1.

He says the **first** move is always a **presentment**. You mail them and ask them to **do something specific**: prove you have a contract with them, or perform. You do this **before** a notary. If they ignore you, that silence is the start of dishonor, because a dispute has to be talked about to be settled. You need **proof**. Save every receipt. He points at UCC Article 3 (negotiable instruments) and the rule that a **protest** is a certificate of dishonor by a notary (that certificate is step 21, not this step).

Do this:

1. Write the presentment to the living person at the company (he does not address only the company name). Use the address from the deed of trust / loan file. His example name was a credit-union president in Texas. **Your** name and address go here instead.
2. Say what you want them to do.
3. Mail it certified. Keep the receipt.
4. If they answer, save the answer and deal with it before you go to the notary.
5. If they do not answer, go to step 18. Give the notary a copy of this letter and the receipt.

**In Drive:** not sent.

**Done when:** the letter and the receipt are in Drive.

## Step 20 — Chance to cure (10 more days)

**Course lesson 30, third paper.** **Notice of Protest and Opportunity to Cure.**

The notary writes that they already sent the breach notice, you did not perform, you are in default, and you may still cure within **10 days from the postmark** of this new letter. If you do not, the notary will issue a certificate of dishonor.

1. Notary mails it after the first 10 days with no real answer.
2. File the copy and the receipt.
3. Wait the 10 days. He sometimes waits a few extra days. Do not skip the wait.

**Template in Drive:** Opportunity to Cure, same folders. Not mailed.

**Done when:** the second 10 days have passed with no cure, and the receipt is filed.

## Step 21 — Certificate of dishonor

**Course lesson 30, fourth paper.** **Certificate of Dishonor.**

The notary certifies:

- The date of the notice of breach (10 days given).
- The date of the opportunity to cure (10 more days given).
- No response came to the notary.
- The notary interviewed you and attaches the affidavit you gave them in step 18.
- The other side dishonored by not accepting or not performing, so they are held to the terms of what you presented.

He also has a **second notary** acknowledge the first notary’s certificate (the first notary is the witness; a different notary notarizes that).

1. Notary issues the certificate after the cure window.
2. Second notary acknowledges it.
3. Attach your step-18 affidavit.
4. Scan the whole set into Drive.

**Template in Drive:** Certificate of Dishonor, same folders. Not issued for this house.

**Done when:** the signed certificate (with the second notarization) is in Drive.
