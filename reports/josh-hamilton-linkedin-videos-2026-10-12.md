## 1. Respect Client Time on Sales Calls

Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_nobody-shows-up-to-your-sales-calls-because-activity-7506457267710414848-i2Hh

Visible date: approximately 1 week before October 12, 2026.

Transcript record: LinkedIn exposes an automatic transcript on the public result. The video is a first-person story about booking a call with a potential vendor, receiving no useful confirmation or preparation, and canceling even though the buyer had money, need, and high intent. Hamilton’s point is that booking is not the same as commitment. After the booking, the funnel should explain the call, what the buyer should bring, who they will meet, expected timing, pricing realities, best- and worst-case outcomes, onboarding, team roles, client examples, and the work required from the buyer. The closing question is whether the seller is earning the prospect’s time or assuming the prospect owes them attendance.

## 2. VSL Stacking Boosts Booking Rate and Sales

Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-hamiltonx_vsl-stacking-theres-a-specific-strategy-activity-7511529327398686720-ZSiZ

Visible date: approximately 5 days before October 12, 2026.

Transcript record: LinkedIn exposes an automatic transcript on the public result. Hamilton describes placing a separate video at every funnel friction point where a person would normally answer a question or objection. His example uses a website video for a free report, a follow-up video for leads who do not answer, a pre-appointment video to improve show-up, and an optional in-home video to support the close. He says each video sells only the next step. He gives target lifts and a revenue projection for a home-services business, but the post does not provide a control group, sample size, dates, or independent verification.
