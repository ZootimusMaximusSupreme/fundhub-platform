VSL Stacking Theory

Short sales videos placed at the moments where a buyer must make a new decision. Each video sells the next step, not the whole journey.

The honest takeawayA useful practitioner framework. Not a proven law.

One video. One unresolved question. One next action.

Where it fits best

Complex, high-trust offers with several decisions: lead capture, booked calls, show-ups, proposals, and onboarding.

Where it can hurt

Simple offers, fast purchases, weak relevance, slow pages, repeated scripts, or a video that delays the main action.

What is known vs. claimed

The framework is documented. The big performance promise is not.

The smallest useful test

Do not film a library. Add one focused video at one measured friction point.

1

Pick the leak

Find the highest-value drop-off with a repeated question.

2

Add one 60–90 sec VSL

Keep the offer, page, traffic, and follow-up the same.

3

Judge the next step

Use qualified bookings, shows, purchases, or revenue—not watch time alone.

Keep, change, or remove

Keep: the downstream business result improves enough to cover cost.

Change: clicks rise, but qualified conversion, speed, or margin falls.

Remove: the video repeats the main VSL or delays a simple action.
